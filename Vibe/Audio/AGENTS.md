# Audio

The playback engine: `AudioPlayer` and its categories, `AudioVoiceBus`, `AudioTrack`, `AudioError`, `MusicalKey.h`, and the fade math. Every feature beside it has its own `AGENTS.md`: `FX/` (the DJ master-bus segment), `Loading/` (materialization, admission, the transfer registry), `Metadata/`, `Waveform/` (data only; rendering is `Vibe/WaveformUI/`), `Analysis/`, `Levels/`, `Mac/Devices/`, `Mac/Convert/` and `iOS/`.

## The model in one page

Vibe owns playback and renders it. One C function, `VibeMasterBusRender` (`AudioPlayer+Pipeline`), renders `voice bus → [varispeed] → [FX] → [meter] → [volume]` straight into the output's buffers, on both platforms. The **voice bus** (`AudioVoiceBus`) is decode → ring → per-frame gain → mix. The varispeed is Vibe's own converter. The FX units are Apple's DSP, hosted through the AudioUnit C API. Both are skipped while they have nothing to do. The meter is the equalizer's analyzer. The volume is `AudioPlayer.volume`'s gain. It is skipped at exactly 1, so full volume is every sample untouched (`testVolumeIsExactAtFullAndSilentAtZero`). It ramps across one slice when it moves under a running output. It lands on its target directly at every output start, so a start plays at the volume it was left at (`testAStartPlaysAtTheVolumeItWasLeftAt`). The debug `--silent` is its target of 0. Only the mac's Volume control moves it. One output unit pulls the render on both platforms: `AudioOutputUnit`, an output audio unit Vibe hosts, calling the render through a C proc. On macOS it is a HALOutput unit bound to the chosen device (`Mac/Devices/AGENTS.md`); on iOS it is RemoteIO, whose route is `AVAudioSession`'s (`iOS/AGENTS.md`). There is no `AVAudioEngine`.

- A **voice** is one file being rendered: a decoder filling a ring, a gain with at most one pending ramp, an optional queued successor file. At most one voice is **current** — the transport's track — and the rest are **retiring**, fading out after a track change, seek or stop, and dying on their own.
- The **transport** (`AudioPlayer.m`) is the state machine `{Stopped, Loading, Playing, Paused}`. Every verb publishes its new state and delivers its delegate event **at once, on the queue**; the audio follows within the declick length. Nothing in the transport waits for a fade.
- The transport hears three things from the bus, through a **drain** it runs itself: a voice went **live** (its first frames are buffered), the current voice passed its **boundary** into the queued successor (promote, `didAutoAdvance`), a voice **ended** (the current one: `didFinishPlaying` on clean EOF or an attributed error on failure; a retiring one: the output-liveness fold).
- **Every fade exists only to keep the output click-free.** A ramp is evaluated per frame on the audio thread from the frame index, so it lands on its target exactly. **Declick** (Settings > Audio, default on, every mode) decides what a transport edge does: on, the ≤10 ms declick, every other frame untouched; off, a cut. A crossfade longer than the declick is the user's choice and fades either way; bit-perfect output holds the crossfade at the declick, so with Declick off it applies no gain at all, because a ramp changes the bitstream.

The reading path is `AudioPlayer.h` → `AudioPlayer.m` → `AudioVoiceBus.h` → `AudioPlayer+Pipeline.h` → the platform half.

**`AudioPlayer.m` is the single writer of the state it publishes; each category owns one vocabulary and declares its methods in its own header.** All of them share `AudioPlayerInternal.h`, which also declares `(PlatformOutput)` once for the two platform implementations. **That shared header is the cost of every split**: a category that pushes more state into it than it takes out of `AudioPlayer.m` is not worth making.

**State is `{Stopped, Playing, Paused, Loading}`, and Loading keeps its intent live; buffering is a flag beside it, never a state** (below, Opening a file). Loading covers the in-flight open and reports zero position and duration; play/pause during it toggles whether the opened file starts or parks, and a seek replaces its start position. Exactly one of `isPlaying`/`isPaused`/`isStopped` is true; `isLoading` is orthogonal. A pause reports Paused the moment it is requested. An action that must order itself after already-submitted transport commands calls `getPlaybackIntent:forTrack:` once: Loading answers the request's intent, otherwise `(position, state == Paused)`.

**`PlaybackRequestCoordinator` owns the in-flight open**: identity, row, intent, slow-load state and what the delegate must be told (Foundation-only, tested). An identifier is never reused, not even across `invalidate`, so a worker blocked on a dead mount cannot consume a later open of the same path. A re-drop or replay rebinds the row in place instead of starting a second open, re-firing `didBeginLoading:` when the request is already slow. A seek is accepted on either identity, the row or the exact submitted play, because the identifier covers a seek issued before its play reached the queue and the row one issued after.

**The published tuple is written whole.** `publishState:voice:file:window:startSeconds:baseFrames:` writes state, voice, file (as its rate and the track's window of it, so a getter never messages an object) and the position's origin under `_stateLock` in one acquisition; the two `unpublishVoiceOnQueue…` variants and an estimated length's window (`republishEstimatedWindowOnQueue`) are the only partial writers, safe because they never move the origin. Position is `startSeconds + (consumed − promotedBaseFrames) / busRate`, read from the voice's own counter, so it holds across an output stop and needs no cache.

## The voice bus

`AudioVoiceBus.h` carries the contract; this is the shape.

**Format.** The bus has one format for its life — the output's: float32 non-interleaved, stereo, at the rate the output runs at — so a mode toggle or a device rate change rebuilds the segment, and the bus owns conversion. A file is read direct when its rate, sample format and channels are the bus's (`VibePCMFormatsMatch`, the one rule the bus, the pipeline and the bit-perfect splice share): under bit-perfect output the prepare has moved the device and the output to the file's rate first (`Mac/Devices/AGENTS.md`), so a stereo file at that rate is the bitstream unchanged. Otherwise it is converted on the decode queue by a `VibeConverter` (`AudioResampler.h`), which is also what a device that refuses a file's rate gets: **r8brain-free-src on both platforms**, linear phase, its 24-bit preset, a 1% transition band, with no quality setting. It replaced Apple's `AudioConverterRef`, equal or better on every measure at a twentieth of the cost (`docs/audio-quality.md`); `Tests/ResamplerQualityTests.m` holds it to the bounds both met. It pulls float32 frames through a `VibeConverterInputProc`: a file that runs dry with the stream open answers no frames, which ends that fill with what it produced and keeps the filter primed for a successor. r8brain has no end-of-stream call — feeding zeros and stopping at the input's length × ratio is upstream's own way to take its tail (its `example.cpp`) — so the stream's end is the silence flush below. A channel difference is mixed first, on the file's own rate — AudioToolbox's standard matrix between the two layouts (`kAudioFormatProperty_MatrixMixMap`: a 5.1 file's center and surrounds fold into stereo at −3 dB, its LFE dropped; another order at the same width is a permutation), mono into every channel, a file with no standard layout keeping its first channels — so a converter carries the bus's channels only, and none is needed at the bus rate. Every file, lossy or lossless, reaches the bus as the float32 its reader decodes; a lossy one's precision is left to the output's one rounding into the device (`Mac/Devices/AGENTS.md`). **MPEG files (MP1, MP2, MP3) are decoded by dr_mp3 by default**, because Apple's MPEG decoder offers only 16-bit integer output on arm64, which rounds without dither and clips a master's overs before the float conversion; its x86_64 build outputs float32 and still clips them. Apple's is the alternative; the mac's Settings > Advanced > MP3 decoder chooses (`AppSettings.appleMPEGDecoder`, pushed to `AudioFileHandle.appleMPEGDecoder` at launch and by `VibeSettingsLiveEffectMP3Decoder`, which also reopens the parked next track and replays a playing MPEG file where it was; a handle keeps the decoder it opened with, and a prefetch that lands under a choice since changed (`decoderChoiceIsStale`: its path's open was rejoined, not restarted) is reopened), iOS keeps dr_mp3, `set_decoder` overrides either for a session, and `dump_audio_path`'s source stage reports `decoder` (`docs/audio-quality.md`). **FLAC files are decoded by dr_flac**, with no choice, since its decode is Apple's bit for bit: it takes under half the CPU, reads the file once, and seeks through the seek table and by bisection, where Apple's first seek into a part not yet read scans the file from its start, for seconds on a long mix. It also plays the legal FLACs Apple's codec refuses. CoreAudio's parser still answers the file's facts, then is closed; the vendored copy carries Vibe's fixes (`ThirdParty/AGENTS.md`). **WAV, W64, RF64 and AIFF files are decoded by dr_wav** the same way, with no choice, when they hold a coding it decodes (`ThirdParty/AGENTS.md`); anything else in those containers, MPEG or FLAC among them, stays Apple's, and no file the parser names another container is offered it (`VibeDrWAVReadsContainer`, inside `openWAV` beside the codings, tested by which decoder each container opens with), so a CAF, M4A or Ogg open never pays its read and failed parse. AAC decodes to float natively and is unaffected. Ogg is CoreAudio's own reader and decoders: Opus on both platforms, Vorbis on macOS only — iOS's CoreAudio has no Vorbis decoder and its Ogg reader refuses the file, so the open fails there (measured on the iOS 27 simulator). **TRAP: FLAC in Ogg is refused at the parser open**, since that reader reports it no length and decodes only its first page; the metadata fallback opens the same way, and the TagLib parse refuses it as FLAC, so its row fails to parse rather than look playable (`AudioFileHandle.m`, `AudioTrackMetadata.mm`, and the canary in `testFLACInOggIsRefused`). The source segment and conversions are described here; `audioPathSnapshot` reports the ones in force.

**Slots.** Eight, each a plain C struct with a power-of-two ring of at least one second at the bus rate, allocated once and freed only after the bus's pointer was withdrawn from the master bus and no render is inside it (`afterRenderLeavesOnQueue:`, `AudioPlayer+Pipeline.h`: a render not seen outside within the wait's bound keeps the bus alive until it is), so a late render touches valid memory. State `free → armed → live → dead → free`: the queue does free→armed and armed→dead, the decoder armed→live and armed→dead on failure before the first frame, **only the audio thread live→dead** (end of stream, or a retire landing; a kill is a retire of zero frames). An armed slot has never been rendered, so its death needs no render to retire. dead→free runs on the slot's own decode queue (**TRAP: on any other it disposes the converter under a turn still in flight**) after a render has completed past the death, so no reader or writer is inside the slot; the drain retires the record and lookup identities together before queuing it once per death. It cleans only the death it was queued for (the drain queues one per death) and clears the slot's generation, which every decode turn checks first, so a turn queued for the dead voice cannot enter the slot while the next voice binds it. Counters are never reset: `written` and `consumed` are monotonic for the slot's life, and the recycle aligns them. A bind records the two origins a snapshot subtracts as atomics, with the generation as the seqlock's version over every field it writes — zeroed by the recycle, a release fence before the writes, the identifier's release-store after them — so a snapshot polled across a slot's reuse retries instead of mixing two voices or racing the bind. A start always receives an identity: at two free slots the oldest retiring voice is cut, and a start that still finds no slot is **pending**, bound when the drain frees one. A pending start waits as long as that takes, an output stop included. A free slot guarantees a queue for its own file, and a successor never holds up binding. **Every started voice ends exactly once through the drain**, a pending one killed before it had a slot included.

**The audio thread** runs `VibeVoiceBusRender`, a C function under `CA_REALTIME_API` and `-Werror=function-effects`: a lock, allocation, Objective-C send or dispatch call is a build error. It reads plain memory and atomics, writes the buffers, takes its stamps from the block's timestamp, and never signals anyone. Per live voice: adopt a new ramp sequence from the current gain (adoption un-pauses; zero frames land at once); mix `min(frames, written − consumed)` at the per-frame gain — the ring read in at most two contiguous spans, each one vDSP operation per channel, a fade's gains computed once per frame and shared by the channels; zero-fill the rest as an underrun unless the end is known; land a pause or retire on its exact frame. `renderSequence` and `inRender` let the queue tell "no render is inside any slot" from "the output says it is stopped".

**Gain.** One 64-bit atomic per voice: target, frames, curve, action (none / pause / retire), sequence. The declick curve is **linear** — a 10 ms log ramp's last-sample slope fails the envelope bound at 44.1 kHz — and every longer fade is one side of an equal-power crossfade (`FadeMath.h`, tested per frame). **`rampOnQueueToGain:` is the one gain rule**: under bit-perfect output every ramp is clamped to the declick length — the crossfade setting is already held at the minimum under the mode, but a play submitted before the mode landed carries the length it was retired with — so the only gain the mode ever applies is 10 ms per edge; and with Declick off every declick-length ramp is zero frames, a cut, in either mode, while a longer one is the user's crossfade and fades. `bitPerfectOnQueue` is the mode itself, which the segment choice reads. Nothing renders under a stopped output, so retiring voices are killed there and a pause is a cut that lands at the first render after any restart.

**The decode pool** is eight serial queues per bus (`QOS_CLASS_USER_INTERACTIVE`), or the caller's thread under the frame-driven test pump. An unrelated file takes an unused queue, so a stalled old read cannot hold its start. Overlapping reads of one handle, a queued successor's included, stay on the same queue (`decodeQueueReadingFile:`). **TRAP: a voice has left its file only once its reads are stopped for good and its last turn is over** (`fillScheduled` clear): on `readsAllowed` alone a second queue takes the cursor with the old read still inside, and a withheld voice reads again. It need not have left its slot, where what it buffered still fades. A successor whose file another queue still reads is accepted into the record's `waitingSuccessor`, as a pending start's is, and handed to the decoder by the drain once that queue has left it (`publishWaitingSuccessorForSlot:`); cancelling it drops it, and a voice that ends first ends as an ordinary track end, the player having reported the successor armed. The decoder asks for a drain (`needsDrain`) when a voice goes live or fails before its first frame, when a stopped voice's last turn ends and when a slot frees, so these happen under a stopped output too, which has no drain timer. Queue ownership follows every occupied slot using it, not the slot that first took it: a seek can still use that reader after the old slot is freed. Turns are 4096 frames (live at one, filled to the ring, topped up below half). Only the assigned queue touches an `AudioFileHandle` after a start. **Clean EOF follows a successful read**: a short/empty read or reaching the declared end. A read error never becomes successful EOF. A refused seek or read ends with `VibeVoiceEndFailed`; the record retains the error, failing URL and exact handle identity until the drain consumes it. A failed read's valid buffer prefix is published once. The resampler cannot fail once made; a converter that cannot be made fails the prepare. A refused successor seek flushes the healthy shared converter’s predecessor tail before ending. An unheard successor cannot promote or reset the predecessor as a failed row. Retired/superseded voices cannot fail a newer submission. **The stream's end is declared** — a converter's tail pushed out and `endOfStream` published — only once the render is within two chunks of the file's last frame, or a successor needs a converter of its own; until then a converter stays open past its file, its filter primed for a successor named late. **TRAP: the tail is pushed out with silence and cut at the frames fed, at the bus rate (`streamEndForRecord:written:`)**: r8brain is never told the end, and a fill that stops at zero frames leaves its filter's length of the file inside it. A bus-format file has nothing to keep open and ends at once. A voice retired at declick length reads no more, so its file may be handed to the next voice at once. **A stopped voice's waiting read is woken**: the call that stops a voice's reads for good (`stopReadsOfSlot:`, behind `stopReadingForVoice:`, `stopReadingThen:`, a kill, and the drain that reports a death, a crossfade's end included) interrupts its file and successor (`AudioFileHandle interruptReads`), so a turn parked at a streaming file's edge returns at once instead of holding the queue, and the file, behind it; the transport also stops a dead voice's reads when it retires one. An interrupted read or seek of a stopped voice ends its turn and writes nothing, neither drained nor failed; one whose reads are still allowed was stopped by nobody and fails as any read error does. **TRAP: only the call that clears `readsAllowed` interrupts**, since a voice already stopped may have handed its file on. **TRAP: reads are allowed again only at a voice's positioning seek** (`decodeChunkForSlot:`, its first and each successor's), on its own decode queue: the voice before it was stopped and interrupted on the player queue before this one started, and a turn of it still in flight put this voice on its serial queue, so no other read is inside the file; a stop landing between the allow and the seek is caught by the re-check after it. Every interrupt is issued on the player queue before any newer voice on the file can start, which is what makes that ordering a property of where the calls sit rather than of timing. The frame-driven pump's inline fill would block on a waiting read, so the render suite plays streams on the decode pool.

**The successor.** `queueSuccessor:` hands the parked file to the current voice; the decoder continues into it at the file's end and publishes the **boundary** before the successor's first frames, so a render that sees them sees where they begin. The boundary is computed from the frames the converter was fed, at the bus rate, never read off `written`: the resampler holds its filter's length of the first file back until the successor's frames push it out, so a boundary read off `written` promotes the next track that long before its first frame sounds. A successor read the same way as the file before it — the same format and decode format — continues through the same converter, which is told nothing of the boundary (the pull that finds the file empty answers *no data now*, never *end of stream*), so a resampler's filter carries across it; any other successor gets a converter of its own after the first one's tail. The hand-off is one atomic `{none, queued, switching}`: the decoder wins queued→switching, an unqueue wins queued→none, and whoever loses has its answer — **the decoder's claim commits the voice from the moment it is made**, before the boundary is published, so an unqueue against it reports the switch and the transport re-voices. **A stream whose end was declared before its successor was named is reopened**: a live voice still takes a successor after its end, with a converter of its own — the decoder prepares it, withdraws the end, lets every render that could have loaded it finish, and reads the audio thread's verdict: a voice that reached the end died there and ends as it would have, the successor never begun; otherwise the boundary is published at the old end and the stream goes on. That wait is bounded (100 ms) and ends at once when the voice's reads are stopped, in both cases without a verdict: the end goes back, no boundary is published, and the voice ends there — a stuck render must never hold the assigned decode queue indefinitely. **One voice chains any number of successors**, so the drain reports boundaries by value, and the transport queues the next only after the previous one was reported.

**The drain** (`drainVoiceBusOnQueue`) binds pending voices, emits each voice's events in the order live → boundary → ended, tops up rings — never a voice that can write nothing, its reads stopped, its end published with no successor queued, or its stream held open past its file while the render is far from it, so a voice paused at its end asks for no decoder turn — and recycles dead slots. On hardware it is a timer on the player queue **iff the output is running and a slot is occupied** — nothing at idle, nothing while paused-and-idle-stopped — plus a hop from the decoder when a voice goes live, stopped reads leave a file, or a slot frees; under the debug pump it runs after every rendered slice. The timer is 10 ms while anything is due — a fade, deferred render-leave work, or the bus's `promptDrainDue` — and 100 ms otherwise, a tenth of the wakeups through steady playback. **What the bus has due is found by the drain's own pass**, slot by slot after each slot's work; a start or a kill raises it at once on the player queue. A boundary or an end the decoder publishes on reaching its file's end is at least half a ring ahead of the render, so the steady drain that first sees it is still in time. **A converted stream's end is not**: held open past its file for a late successor, it is declared only once the render is within `kOpenStreamReserveFrames` of it (85 ms at 96 kHz, under the steady interval), so the hold itself is prompt — while it is current, `heldOpenAt` still matching `written`, since a successor that continues the stream writes past it and the cadence returns to steady (`testAResampledStreamHeldOpenReturnsToSteadyOnceItsSuccessorContinues`). A path that makes something due without a drain (a start, a retire) re-evaluates the timer itself.

## Threading

**Every pipeline mutation runs on the serial player queue; the UI getters take the `os_unfair_lock` snapshot and compute off it**, never a queue round trip. The output unit's own HAL calls — bind (macOS), configure, start, stop, each of which waits on the hardware's IO thread — run on the unit's serial queue in the order the player queue issued them, so no device holds the player queue (`Mac/Devices/AGENTS.md`). **TRAP: so are the unit's own reads of its device.** Its latencies (after each bind, configure and start) and its channel map (after each landed bind and configure) are read on the unit's queue and answered from fields: read live, a hung device held the player queue 30 s per HAL call, and main with it through `dump_state`. **What the player queue still reads of a device is bounded**: the bound device's nominal rate after each bind (`followOutputDeviceRateOnQueue`, which the pipeline cannot be built without), the audio path's device stage and the bit-perfect report's device reads each go through `CoreAudioUtil.performBoundedRead:`, one serial reads queue the caller waits on for at most 0.5 s — a late rate is followed when it comes, as the rate listener's is, and while one read is overdue nothing waits. The bind and System Output's default ask the device manager's snapshot, never the HAL, and the rate listener reads off the queue. Still read on it, unbounded: the system default once, when the output unit is created, and bit-perfect's own — the stream reads that choose a file's format (the prepare and the gapless check), the format write and its confirmation, the hog, the prepared device's rate listener — which the mode blocks at by design (`Mac/Devices/AGENTS.md`); on iOS, the session's rate before every start and its latencies for the reports. The queue's QoS is Default, as the unit's queue it waits on in `waitUntilIdle` is; the latency-critical work — the open and the decode — runs on its own lanes. `make test-audio` checks the conversion through the production renderer against an off-bus reference, and reads the algorithm back through `debugCurrentConversion`.

**TRAP: every buffer the render is the first to write is written once where it is allocated** — a `calloc`'d page is mapped at its first write, and a fault on the audio thread waits for a free page. Use `vDSP_vclr`, not `memset`: clang drops a `memset(0)` after `calloc` (each allocation carries the `TRAP:`). **Nothing the render could be inside is freed or reset on a timeout.** A withdrawal publishes the pointer's removal and waits, bounded, for the render to be seen outside; when the bound runs out — a render stuck in the pipeline — the teardown is parked and run at the first later wait or drain that sees the render outside (`afterRenderLeavesOnQueue:`), and the player's dealloc leaks the pipeline rather than free under one. Each hosting is one allocation the render reads through one pointer: the varispeed's stage, and the FX chain's units and scratch. A re-host swaps the pointer, and a late render finishes inside the old hosting, which waits for it the same way. So does a varispeed kernel the fader replaced. **The pipeline admits one render at a time**: the master bus's `inRender` is a door the render takes at its entry and releases at its exit, so an output unit's callback that finds another render inside — a stuck one, outliving its output unit's bounded stop while the next output unit began — renders silence and touches nothing (`renderRefusals` in `debugRenderCounts` and `dump_health`, zero through every soak, and a `check_consistency` violation otherwise), the evidence the queue reads is the stuck render's own, and no two renders share the pipeline's state.

**There are no staleness counters for the audio; the voice's identity is that job.** A `VibeVoiceID` is minted once per start and is the slot's generation for as long as it holds one, so a stale id never addresses a reused slot, and a ramp or event for a voice the transport no longer publishes is simply not the current voice. Deliveries to main are guarded by submitted-play identity (below), not by any counter.

## Crossfades, pauses and seeks

**`VibeIncomingFadeMilliseconds` (`FadeMath.h`, tested) is the one place that decides a play's fade length.** `crossfadeMilliseconds` (default the 10 ms declick minimum, which Settings > Playback's slider shows as off; up to 3000 in steps of 100) applies only when a play replaces an audibly playing track; first plays, the pause/seek/stop declicks and `play:atPosition:startPaused:` force the minimum. **A track end crossfades from inside the player, since the shell's `play:` after it would find nothing audible**: each drain asks `maybeCrossfadeIntoParkOnQueue`, and once `VibeTrackEndCrossfadeMilliseconds` (tested) says it is due — the setting held to half of each track, outgoing and incoming, and to what remains, one steady drain interval early — the park starts as its own voice fading in while the current one fades out over the same length, and `adoptTrack:…`, which the gapless promote ends in too, makes it current and tells the shell through `didAutoAdvanceFromTrack:toTrack:`. Nothing parked (the playlist's end, Pause at track end), an estimated window, buffering and bit-perfect output each leave the ordinary track end. A track change is a true crossfade on one bus: the outgoing voice fades on its own ramp while the incoming one fades in beside it, and nothing is rewired. **With Declick off every declick-length edge cuts**, in either mode: `testBitPerfectTransportCutsWithoutChangingSamples` and `testDeclickOffCutsOrdinaryEdgesAndKeepsTheCrossfade` require every audible frame across seek, pause, resume, a track change and stop to continue an exact excerpt of the source, and the latter keeps a 2 s crossfade fading. With Declick on, the default, each of those edges is the 10 ms declick and nothing else is touched (`testBitPerfectDeclickRampsOnlyTheEdges`); the choice is `AudioPlayer.declick`, pushed from Settings > Audio.

**A voice that cannot be heard is killed, not faded** — not yet live, paused, cut with Declick off, or under a stopped output — and is silent at once, so only fading voices are `_retiringVoices` (`retiredFades` in the debug channel). Stop, pause, a parked play and the failure reset cut every retiring voice to the declick length; skips never do, so rapid skips keep the full fade-out.

**A seek is a new voice at the target with the old one fading out beside it**: one shape for playing and paused, nothing rescheduled, nothing to wait for; `didFinishSeeking:` settles every submitted seek intent, dropped ones included; it does not certify a decoder position. A refused decoder seek follows the current play's identity-guarded error/reset path. The old voice retires before the new one starts (`revoiceOnQueueAtPosition`): both read the same `AudioFileHandle`, and while a turn of the old voice is in flight the new voice takes that voice's decode queue, so its first decode turn positions the cursor after that read has returned, and a declick-length retire stops the old voice's further reads, so no turn of it can advance the cursor from under the new voice (`testSeekStopsTheOldVoiceReadingBeforeItsFileIsHandedOn`). **A pause fades the voice to silence and stops consuming on the exact landing frame**; a resume is a newer ramp, which un-pauses by being newer. The output's deferred idle stop (Stopped or Paused, ~6 s, releasing the output device) is cancelled by generation in `startOutputOnQueue:`, the one funnel every start and resume goes through. It waits past the 6 s while an FX send's tail still rings, re-checking each second for at most the chain's longest declared tail (`AudioFX.sendsActive`, `longestTailSeconds`): a reverb released before a pause rings out instead of being cut, and a send held through the pause cannot hold the device. **TRAP: only the manual pump may start without an output unit**; otherwise Playing and `didStartPlaying:` publish with no callback to advance the voice, and the shell gets neither audio nor an error. A missing output unit fails the start with `VibeAudioErrorEngineStartFailed`; a start the unit refuses later, after Playing was published, parks the voice Paused and reports the same error on either platform (`outputUnitFailedOnQueue:`; `VibeAudioErrorDeviceUnavailable` for a refused macOS bind). Voices keep their state across an output stop, so a paused idle stop, a device rebind that keeps the output's format and an iOS system stop reschedule nothing; a format change rebuilds the segment and re-voices.

**The source segment is built lazily** at the first settlement, and rebuilt only when the mode or the output's format differs — a rebuild kills every voice, so its callers guarantee nothing audible and re-voice at the retained intent (`Mac/Devices/AGENTS.md`). The re-voice takes the same `AudioFileHandle`, and the old bus's decoder may be inside a read of it — its queued turns retain the bus, not the player — so the rebuild stops the old bus's reading and is told once every one of its decode queues has left its turn (`stopReadingThen:`; one report for all eight, so a read stalled on any of them withholds every file the old bus held, the current track's included), withholding every file it may be inside from the new bus's voices until it has left them (`filesInUse`, read under the table lock the decoder writes a record's file pair under, so a handoff in flight lists the successor in one field or the other; `withholdReadsOfFile:` / `allowReadsOfFile:`, counted per retired bus in `_retiredDecoderFiles`): a voice started on a withheld file reads nothing until then, and a successor queued on one is refused and asked for again. **The player queue never joins the decoder**: a read on a stalled mount would hold it, and every transport command behind it, for the whole stall. **Pitch is the varispeed's ratio, 1 + pitch / 100, and whether it is in the chain at all.** **At zero pitch the varispeed is a bit-perfect pass-through**: the render plays the bus straight, with nothing converted, no delay and no copy (`testZeroPitchIsBitPerfectAndTogglesAreClickFree`). The varispeed is Vibe's own stage, `FX/AudioVarispeed`, not an Apple unit; its design, its edges and its tests are `FX/AGENTS.md`'s. The pipeline hosts one per source segment, at zero pitch. The render hands the stage each slice, and the stage pulls the bus the slice read through `VibeMasterBusSource`, at the slice's stamp. `applyPitchOnQueue:` sets the stage's pitch and frees the table it replaced through `afterRenderLeavesOnQueue:`, since only the pipeline knows when no render is inside. Bit-perfect output and iOS have no varispeed.

## Prefetch and gapless

**`prefetchTrack:` on every track start pre-opens what follows the track at its end — the playlist's `trackEndSuccessor`, through each shell's `successorPrefetchTrack` — and the next `play:` of that source consumes the parked `AudioFileHandle`.** The request state is `AudioPrefetchRules.h`: a prefetch of another source (`AudioTrack.sourceKey`, so another row of the file being opened too) requested while an open is in flight is retained and suppressed, resumed by that open's success and dropped by its failure, abandonment, a newer play (same-path rebind included) or a later prefetch; its identity also drops every late callback. There is no acknowledgement handshake with the loading side, because the foreground rule is derived inside the coordinator (`Loading/AGENTS.md`).

**At play submission an unrelated prefetch is cancelled before the foreground open starts**, so its transfer cannot compete with the file the user chose. A prefetch of the track being played stays alive: its handle run and the playback run ride one path claim, whichever succeeds first consumes the play request, and the winner retires the loser's park state so a play never leaves its own track parked; Repeat One parks it again on purpose, by the prefetch after its start.

**The successor is the park itself.** With the crossfade at its minimum, or the successor continuing the recording (`VibeGaplessArmAllowed`, below) — and, under bit-perfect output, the next file wanting the device's current rate and channels, since a continuation on one voice cannot switch the device — `maybeArmSuccessorOnQueue` queues the parked file on the current voice. No second handle exists: the bus's decoder is the only reader of a file after a voice starts. The boundary event promotes the successor in place — same voice, no stop, no fade — with the bus frames consumed before it as the new base for the position, fires `didAutoAdvanceFromTrack:toTrack:` so the controller advances the playlist without `play:`, and **consumes the park**, so a replay of the promoted row opens fresh. **Repeat One parks the playing track's own object**: a second handle on the same file, spliced into its own start like any successor (`testATrackSplicesIntoItselfGaplessly`; a cue row into its own window, `testACueRowSplicesIntoItsOwnWindow`). **A track's end fires exactly one of `didFinishPlaying:` or the auto-advance callback, never both.** When the track-end successor changes under a queued one — a playlist edit, or a change of shuffle, repeat or Pause at track end — or the crossfade setting is raised, `unqueueSuccessorOnQueue` drops it and the voice ends at its own file, or, when the decoder had already switched into it, re-voices the current file at its position so the buffered successor dies with the old voice; lowering the setting re-queues the park.

CoreAudio honors LAME/iTunes gapless metadata, so tagged MP3/AAC and all lossless files continue seamlessly; an untagged MP3's encoder padding is the one gap this cannot remove. `isGaplessArmed` surfaces the queued state to `dump_state`.

**A main-thread action snapshots its track at dispatch**, because a promote can land between the action and its queue block: `seekToPosition:` and `finishCurrentTrack` drop the request if the boundary advanced it.

## Cue rows play a window

**A cue row is a window of its file, and the player plays it as if it were the whole file**, so every track-end path — gapless promote, `didFinishPlaying:`, pause at track end — applies at the window's end unchanged. `VibeCueWindow` (`PlaybackIntent.h`, tested) turns the row's CD-frame window into file frames at the file's rate — the two rows meeting at a marker round the same point, so they stay contiguous at a rate 75 does not divide — at settlement (from the request's current track), at arm and at promote. The tuple publishes the window where it published the file's length, so `duration` is the window and `position` starts at its start, and `VibeClampedStartFrame` clamps every start and seek inside it. The bus takes an end frame per voice and per successor — the row's cue end in file frames, unclamped, or 0 for a window running to the file's end (`AudioTrack endFrameInFile:`), never derived from the length, so a whole file ends where its stream really ends, not at a lossy or streaming length's estimate, and an end past the stream's real end stops there — and stops reading there, counting from its own cursor: `framePosition` answers 0 when `ExtAudioFileTell` fails, and an end clamped against that reads into the next row.

**Opens and handles are per file; what sounds is per window.** The Loading rebind stays keyed by the file's path, so a pick of another row of a file being opened rebinds that open, and settlement reads the new row's window. The park, the prefetch and the successor compare `AudioTrack.sourceKey` (the path plus the window), so the next row of a file is a park of its own, and one prefetched while its file is still opening waits behind that open rather than joining it and never parking. **The next window of the same file continues gaplessly whatever the crossfade** (`VibeGaplessArmAllowed`'s second input, `AudioTrack isFollowedContiguouslyBy:`): one recording would otherwise crossfade with itself, or gap at every marker. **TRAP: that is the one successor a long crossfade can have**, so an explicit play of it must not take the park — the voice retiring over the crossfade keeps reading, and may be inside that very handle (`playOnQueue`). It opens its own.

## Settlement identity

**A settlement for a superseded submission is never delivered, and it is matched by submitted-play identity, never by track** (root `AGENTS.md`: a same-row replay is the same `AudioTrack` and the same URL). `didStartPlaying:` and the play-path errors are dropped on main by `submittedPlayIsCurrent:`, checked inside the delivery block because what matters is whether a newer play existed when the callback ran. The comparison is `PlaybackDeliveryRules.h` (tested): `VibePlaybackDeliveryIsCurrent`, and `VibePlaybackSubmissionStateIsUnchanged` for the media-services reset, where zero is a valid initial snapshot because a reset is not a play. The coordinator's own rule ("a superseded open's result is never consumed") cannot cover this: once a result is dispatched to main, nothing can retract it.

Track-end captures its track and submission before optional device recovery, since a failed bind resets the active identifier. A failure reset defers saved-device resolution until its caller has returned, and never schedules a retry from an already-active lookup.

**Resume and seek-restart failures use the same identity even though their errors carry no URL.** They belong to the sounding voice, whose `_activeSubmittedPlayIdentifier` survives gapless promotion; a same-row replay could otherwise make the shell accept the old generic error and tear down the newer play's state.

**TRAP: the counter is `_nextSubmittedPlayIdentifier`, which only increments, never `_lastSubmittedPlayIdentifier`**, the pre-Loading handoff cleared to 0 as soon as its play reaches Loading. Comparing against that one reports every settlement as superseded, delivers nothing, and strands whatever the shells release on settlement.

## Opening a file

**Files open off-queue against an abandon deadline, so a cloud placeholder cannot wedge playback**; a slow open surfaces Loading instead. `AudioFileOpenTimeoutMath.h` (`Loading/`) owns the monotonic deadline: a 60 s no-progress baseline both platforms share, extended (never shortened) to 60 s past each positive raw movement. `AudioLoadingConfiguration.openTimeouts` is snapshotted when the underlying open starts, so a mid-open configuration change cannot move its deadline.

The coordinator's accepted dataless verdict surfaces Loading for a provider file immediately once the probe settles; a probe still queued or running falls back to the half-second delay, as do local files and the remote streaming backend. Both paths match the open identifier and notify once. The player never repeats the filesystem probe on its control queue, where a stalled stat would also block stop and the open deadline.

**Progress is matched by the underlying open identifier, never by path or submitted-play identity.** A same-row replay changes submission identity but keeps the same `PlaybackRequestCoordinator.identifier`, transfer and timeout snapshot; a later retry of that URL gets a new identifier, so an older open's movement cannot extend it. `didBeginLoading:` carries the open identifier to both shells, its main-thread delivery guarded separately by submitted-play identity, and each forwards `CloudTransferRegistry`'s movement for that file under it (`Loading/AGENTS.md`). The player accepts `noteOpenProgressForOpenRequestIdentifier:` only while both the pending request and the playback-open slot carry the identifier. The claim, admission and transfer-publication machinery underneath is `Loading/AGENTS.md`'s.

**A file a remote transfer is still writing opens as a stream** (`CloudFileMaterializer availabilityForURL:`, `System/AGENTS.md`): the handle reads the part file at the final size, and every callback waits for the bytes it was asked for — never the rest of the block it fills, never a read at or past the size — before its `pread`, or copies them from the transfer's tail window when the wait says they are there and not on disk (a block fill takes one source, the window's bytes or the disk's, which stops short at the bytes written since the part is only appended to). A wait ends ready, failed or interrupted. A failed transfer is a read failure carrying the transfer's error, for good. **Interrupted is neither the end nor a failure**: `interruptReads` (any thread) or an open's `interrupted:` block ends a wait that would block, the read, seek or open answers NO with an error `isInterruption:` names, and reads are refused until a seek after `allowReads`. **A lost read returns no bytes**, so every decoder sees one clean end of data, and that seek restarts the two that keep state across it: **TRAP:** dr_flac's seek skips a target it believes it is at and moves inside the frame it holds without reading, so it is restarted at its first frame, and ExtAudioFile serves packets the lost read fetched after a later seek, so it is rebuilt over the same parser. dr_wav's cursor never moved, and dr_mp3's seek rebuilds its decoder anyway, provided a lost read never shortens the packet count. **TRAP:** an MP3 with no Xing, Info or VBRI frame states no packet count, and CoreAudio's parser counts one by reading every frame header to the end (the packet count and ExtAudioFile's length both ask it), so a stream of one would open only once downloaded. It opens uncounted instead, on a count from the frames of its head already on disk (`VibeUncountedMPEGPackets`): all at the first's bit rate, the count that rate gives the audio's bytes; otherwise the count at their average size. **Either is an estimate** (`lengthIsEstimated`): a head at one rate proves nothing of the rest, and a headerless file can change rate after its intro, so a constant-rate head's count is never taken as exact. It is kept ahead of the cursor, a seek past it included, so the bus never takes it for the end, and settled from the parser's count where the reads reach the stream's end, free by then, or at the first read once the download is complete, a count from disk on the decode queue, the parser being the reading thread's (tens of ms for a long mix). **TRAP:** the settle stores the length, then clears the flag with release ordering, and `lengthIsEstimated` reads it with acquire, so a reader that sees it settled reads the settled length; a reader of both takes them as one pair (`lengthEstimated:`), which reads the flag first. **TRAP:** while the length is an estimate the player's window follows it (`republishEstimatedWindowOnQueue`, at every drain, a seek and a stall): grown as the handle's length grows, never shrunk, so the position, which trails the reads, never freezes at a short estimate, and a stall saves the position heard, not the estimate's end. Settled, the window is republished once more, exactly — with `track.duration` and the optional `didSettleDurationOfTrack:` — leaving the position's origin, so only the total moves. Nothing is refused against an estimate: `VibeCueWindow` keeps a cue end past it and runs a window to the file's end at least a frame past its start, so a cue row beyond a short estimate plays, and a start past it (a stall's replay of audio heard past it) is kept, the settlement's window reaching it (`finishPlayOnQueueWithFile:`, **TRAP**). A voice's end is its row's cue end or 0, the file's own end (`AudioTrack endFrameInFile:`), never the length, so the bus needs nothing from the estimate. Seeks land exactly either way, since a packet is a fixed number of frames; a seek ahead waits for the parser to walk the frame headers up to it. A free-format stream, or a head that does not walk, is still counted at open, waiting for its download. **TRAP:** the transfer can finish and rename its part over the URL between the lookup and the open, so a missing part waits for the transfer's verdict and opens the URL. A whole file pays one lookup per open and one nil check per fill. The coordinator opens a readable transfer's file this way and interrupts the open when its run is cancelled, so the open's deadline, still fed by the part file's growth through the shell's monitor, stops a stalled stream and no longer applies once the handle is delivered (`Loading/AGENTS.md`); the voice bus interrupts the reads after it (the decode pool, above).

**A file on a network mount reads ahead** (`AudioFileHandle.m`, `VibeReadAheadRun`). The open decides before any I/O, and three things must hold. The opener passed an `interrupted` block, and it is not `VibeNeverWaitsForAStream`. No transfer's availability answers for the URL. The kernel's mount table says the file's mount is a network one. The handle reads that table with `getfsstat(MNT_NOWAIT)` and never stats the path, since a dead mount holds a stat for its timeout. `VibeMountReadsAhead` (`Loading/AudioFileOpenRules.h`) takes the mount with the longest prefix of the path. A mount without `MNT_LOCAL` is a network one. A table that cannot be read, or no mount holding the path, takes the direct road. The rule compares spellings only. It drops a leading `/System/Volumes/Data` and spells `/var` and `/tmp` under `/private`. A symlinked path, and any path the mount table cannot place, takes the direct road. The coordinator's opens and the waveform loader's read ahead. A nil block (the mac's converter, the debug BPM scan) and the metadata fallback read the file directly.

**The read-ahead is one thread per handle**, named `com.vibe.readahead`. Its QoS is fixed at User Initiated, since a prefetch opens at Utility and then becomes the voice the decode reads. It is the only thread that enters the kernel for the file. It opens the file with the direct road's flags, checks it, and notes its size in an availability with no part file (`System/AGENTS.md`). **TRAP:** both roads open the file nonblocking. A FIFO with no writer then returns at once for `fstat` to refuse, and never parks an uncancellable open worker. Then it fetches 256 KB blocks into it. A range a blocked wait wants comes first, rounded out to whole blocks and fetched whole, however wide. A partial read can leave bytes held inside that range. The fetch then starts where they end. Otherwise it fetches the next block after the reader's, up to 8 MB ahead. An open that succeeds puts the reader back at the head (`noteReaderPosition:`). An open that read the tail last would otherwise leave nothing fetched ahead of the audio until the first decode read. After each block it drops what lies more than one block behind the reader or past the 8 MB. The block behind is kept for an MP3 seek's preroll and dr_flac's bisection. Both read back over it. The drop also always keeps the file's first and last blocks. A handle holds about 9 MB this way. A track on a share has up to three: the playing handle, the parked prefetch and the waveform's.

**A read the server fails never fails the availability.** The thread logs the first failure in a row, waits about 1 s, reopens the file and tries again, until its handle is gone. A dead server is then a wait. The buffering hold and the stall below turn it into a pause with Connection lost. A server back within the stall's span plays on by itself. Only the open's own `open` and `fstat` fail the open, with the direct road's exact errors. A persistent read error on bytes the open itself needs is retried like any other. That open waits out the open deadline and fails as "Load timed out". The direct road fails it at once. A read of 0 bytes below the size is the file's end. The thread records it once (`noteShortenedEnd:`), and a wait at or past it is the end, as on the direct road. **Interrupted reads pause the fetch ahead**: `interruptReads` sets the availability's `readAheadPaused`, and the thread then fetches only a range a wait asks for. A voice skipped away from stops pulling its file over the network. `allowReads` clears it and wakes the thread.

**The handle holds no descriptor.** Every read waits for its bytes in the availability, as a stream's does, and is copied out of the blocks. A wait that is Ready with nothing copied is the end of the file. The open waits for byte 0, then takes the noted size. A cancel needs no wake. A wait with no part file asks its `interrupted` block again every 0.25 s. dr_flac and dr_wav read through the callbacks, as they do for a local file. `holdStream` does nothing, since there is no transfer to hold. The QuickTime reader has no callback open. Its URL road finishes the availability first, and that ends the thread. **An MP3 is counted exactly at open**, as a local file is, its frame headers read through the waits. A headerless MP3 whose frames take longer than the open deadline to cross the wire fails to open. **The hold and the stall below apply unchanged.** `waitingForBytes` rises while a read waits for a block. A parked prefetch reads ahead too, since the park is the gapless successor. Once it is queued on the voice, its progress joins the stall's sum, as a Dropbox prefetch's transfer does.

**The handle's dealloc finishes the availability first**, with a real "closed" error, and only then closes its decoders and parser. The thread exits once its current syscall returns, and closes its descriptor. A dead mount can hold that syscall for minutes. Until it returns the thread is an orphan, counted process-wide. A slow but healthy share can orphan a thread too, until its read returns. **Once eight are orphaned, a new read-ahead open waits** for one to exit before it makes a thread. It asks its `interrupted` block again every 0.25 s. The open deadline still bounds it. Live handles are bounded by their owners. `Read-ahead:` log lines mark each thread's start, its first failed read and its end. **TRAP: the thread retains the availability only, never the handle.** The handle's dealloc can then finish the availability and end the thread. **TRAP: the thread never holds the availability's lock across `open`, `pread` or `close`.** The player queue takes that lock through `interruptReads` and `wakeWaiters`. **TRAP: the thread never finishes its availability with nil.** Complete would send its readers to a disk it never wrote. **TRAP: the orphan count moves only under its own lock, at the handle's dealloc and at the thread's exit.** A thread still running at the dealloc is an orphan until it exits, and no other thread ever is. The two edges share one flags cell. The second edge frees it.

**A stream that falls behind buffers in place, and plays on once** (`updateBufferingOnQueue`, after every drain, from the current voice's snapshot; the render is untouched). The signal is the handle's own: `waitingForBytes`, raised only while a read is blocked at the edge, which the snapshot copies with `decoding` (a turn queued or running). The voice is **held** when its decode waits with less than a quarter second buffered (`kBufferingGraceSeconds`) — a grace in audio, not time, so the hold's fade has frames and a link trickling in many short waits holds as surely as one that stops — through the pause's own ramp and exact landing frame (a cut for a voice not yet heard), and **released** once half a ring is buffered, the end is known, or the decoder has nothing left to wait for (a stream held open past its file). A read waits for all it asks, so the decoder runs a chunk behind the download's edge. The transport stays Playing: `isBuffering` is the flag, `dump_state`'s `player.buffering`, the optional `didChangeBuffering:forTrack:` its edges for the current play, and a held voice is not `outputAudioActive`. A user pause during a hold is an ordinary pause and ends it (the hold is already that pause, so no second ramp), a seek or track change ends it with its voice, and a play resumes the model. **A hold with no progress for the open deadline's span is a stall** (`AudioFileOpenTimeoutMath.h` over `openTimeouts`, snapshotted at the hold). Progress is the handle's `bytesWritten`. That is its availability's `progressBytes`, whichever writer fills it: a transfer or a read-ahead. The stall sums the current file's and a queued successor's. A stall is Paused in place with `VibeAudioErrorConnectionLost`. The voice and its file are let go, and so is a park of the same file, Repeat One's. A transfer's claim then loses its last reader and is cancelled. A read-ahead's thread ends with its handle. A stall is never a track end. `stop`'s guarantee is the model for that. The stall's park is the one Paused with no voice or file (`publishPausedWithoutVoiceOnQueueAtSeconds:`), position and duration held for the getters; a seek moves its position, and resume replays the track from it through the ordinary play path, which fetches afresh. **TRAP: the replay runs in the resume's own queue turn** (`replayStalledTrackOnQueue`), under an identity minted only while the stalled play is still the newest submitted: queued as a fresh `play:` it landed behind a stop or play submitted after the resume, and undid it. There is no start threshold: a stream's voice goes live at its usual chunk, and a link slower than the bitrate buffers from there.

**`stop` (File > Close) unloads outright and fires no transport or track-end callback** (root `AGENTS.md`): it supersedes any in-flight open so a Loading track never starts and fades the voice to silence; the caller owns the UI reset. Every failure path lands in the same `resetToStoppedStateOnQueue`, which retires the current voice, invalidates the open, drops the park and the successor, and publishes Stopped.

## Live output levels

**`levelsEnabled` installs the demand-driven meter in `Levels/`**; `copyBandLevels:count:sequence:` reads the latest coherent snapshot without the player queue or an allocation. `outputAudioActive` is the shell-facing liveness fact: the output running and either the current voice playing or a voice still fading; Loading intent alone is not, a buffering hold is not, and a paused voice is inactive the moment the pause is published. **TRAP: it is not "the output has stopped"** — it excludes an FX tail on purpose. That fact is `outputIdle`: NO from every output start attempt, a failed one included, and YES again only when the idle stop has stopped the output (or the iOS media-services reset dropped the unit); on iOS the unit's queued stop has landed by then, on macOS it may still be queued, announced by `audioPlayerOutputDidBecomeIdle:`. The iOS shell releases its audio session on it (`iOS/AGENTS.md`); a receiver reads the property at the edge, since a newer start may have overtaken it.

**The meter is a stage of the render, not a node on a path.** The meter is created at the first demand and kept: `applyLevelMeterOnQueue` points the master bus at its meter and begins a publisher session while the equalizer or the beta probe wants levels, and withdraws the pointer and ends the session otherwise, live and without waiting; only a replacement — the output's rate or the debug normalization mode changing, the iOS media-services reset — frees it, once the render was seen outside it (`dropLevelMeterOnQueue`; a render stuck past the wait's bound keeps it alive until then). The ownership, render, FFT, publication, demand and `--silent` contracts are `Levels/AGENTS.md`'s.

## Error text

**`VibeAudioError*` descriptions are for logs and are never localized.** The one line a screen shows is `VibeStatusForPlayError` (`AudioErrorRules.h`, tested), mapping `VibeAudioErrorCode` to a `STR_ERROR_*` string; it lives beside the enum because both screens render the same wording.

## Beta diagnostics

With `VIBE_VERBOSE_LOGGING=1`, `Timeline:` joins input/submission, queue admission, open settlement, the voice start, the first render (the voice's live event and its start-of-consumption stamp), callback decisions/delivery and the first UI position **beyond the published playing position**. `Phase:` brackets the player queue's side of a device rebind (macOS), output preparation, format confirmation and ownership, each keeping the queue's stall watcher running over a stopped output; a bind (macOS), start or stop is the output unit's own `AudioOutputUnit:` line, timed on its queue. `Callback:` lines report every bus event with the voice's consumed and underrun counts; a stopped voice's read interrupted at a stream's edge logs one line from its decode queue, and each buffering episode one `Buffering:` line as it ends, with how long it held, how it ended (released, paused, replaced, stalled) and the bytes that arrived meanwhile; `Recovery:` explains device decisions. `Stall:` reports main-thread, player-queue and output-render-clock stalls, and the IO cycles the hosted output unit wrote as silence because the render returned an error (`outputDropouts`, cumulative, in `debugRenderCounts` and `dump_health`, beside the callback's cost, `renderMeanMicros` and `renderMaxMicros`): a stuck thread is sampled 250 ms in and every 500 ms after, six times at most, an unchanged sample in one line; the player queue through whichever pool thread `THREAD_IDENTIFIER_INFO` says is draining it, and a stalled render clock samples the output's IO thread (CoreAudio's `com.apple.audio.IOThread.client` on macOS, `AURemoteIO::IOThread` on iOS) — sampling runs on both platforms; `block_render` holds a real callback inside the pipeline to exercise that path, as `block_main_deep` and `block_player` do the other two. The main thread is watched by a run loop observer, not a ping: each pass stamps its start and the loop's wait clears it, a pass that ran long logs its length as it ends, and the watchdog that samples a pass older than 250 ms runs only while passes happen, parking after a quiet one, so a stalled main thread is sampled whether or not the player is busy. On iOS a main-thread stall while the scene is not foreground-active is marked as such (`noteSceneActive:`, fed by `PlaybackController`): leaving the app makes iOS have it redraw for the app-switcher snapshot, a ~250 ms stall on device that nobody sees. The player queue's watcher is a 100 ms ping that ticks only while the player has work that can stall — the output running, or a device phase in flight (`performDiagnosticPhase:`) — and is suspended otherwise, and the render clock is read at every drain, so its check has no timer of its own. An idle beta wakes nothing. **TRAP: the unified log truncates a message near 1 KB**, so a sample spans numbered `Stall stack:` lines; Vibe's frames are offsets into the executable: `atos -o <dSYM DWARF> -arch arm64 -l 0x100000000 <0x100000000 + offset>` names them, which is why each beta's dSYM is kept.

Every voice start, resume, seek, gapless promotion, output format change, device rebind, iOS output recovery and late meter installation arms the bounded `Signal:` probe described in `Levels/AGENTS.md`; it reads no file, and on hardware holds the meter only for each start's capture. The probe's origin is the pipeline's own clock at the start — the master bus's frames rendered plus the block in flight, under the output unit and the pump alike; its cutoff, when a track change is still fading out, is the render that saw the last fading voice die. **TRAP: the varispeed emits the bus's frames late by its kernel's half-width.** That is 64 input frames, and ceil(64 × ratio) above a ratio of 1, which play in half / ratio output frames. So that cutoff is padded by `varispeedLatencyOnQueue` while the pitch is off zero. It reads 0 otherwise, since at zero the render skips the converter. Unpadded, the probe reads the outgoing track's tail as the incoming one's first signal (`AudioPlayer+Diagnostics.m`).
