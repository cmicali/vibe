# Audio

The playback engine: `AudioPlayer` and its categories, `AudioVoiceBus`, `AudioFileHandle`, `AudioOutputUnit`, `AudioTrack`, `AudioError`, `MusicalKey.h` and the fade math. The file-open path beneath it is `Vibe/Loading/` (`Loading/AGENTS.md`). Every feature beside it has its own `AGENTS.md`: `FX/` (the DJ master-bus segment and the pitch stage), `Metadata/`, `Metadata/Artwork/FolderArt/`, `Waveform/` (data only; rendering is `Vibe/WaveformUI/`), `Analysis/`, `Levels/`, `Mac/Devices/` and `iOS/`. Convert to FLAC is the mac shell's (`Vibe/Mac/Convert/AGENTS.md`). `AudioFileFormat` names the codecs that the metadata display and the converter share. `PINCache+VibeAudioCache` is the disk-cache policy that the metadata and waveform caches share.

## The model in one page

Vibe owns playback and renders it. There is no `AVAudioEngine`. One C function, `VibeMasterBusRender` (`AudioMasterBus`), renders `voice bus → [varispeed] → [FX] → [meter] → [volume]` straight into the output's buffers, on both platforms.

- The **voice bus** (`AudioVoiceBus`) is decode → ring → per-frame gain → mix.
- The **varispeed** is Vibe's own converter (`FX/AGENTS.md`). The **FX** units are Apple's DSP, hosted through the AudioUnit C API. Both are skipped while they have nothing to do.
- The **meter** is the equalizer's analyzer (`Levels/AGENTS.md`).
- The **volume** is `AudioPlayer.volume`'s gain. It is skipped at exactly 1, so full volume leaves every sample untouched (`testVolumeIsExactAtFullAndSilentAtZero`). It ramps across one slice when it moves under a running output. It lands on its target directly at every output start, so a start plays at the volume it was left at. The debug `--silent` is its target of 0. Only the mac's Volume control moves it otherwise.

One **output unit** pulls the render on both platforms: `AudioOutputUnit`, an output audio unit Vibe hosts, calling the render through a C proc. On macOS it is HALOutput bound to the chosen device (`Mac/Devices/AGENTS.md`). On iOS it is RemoteIO, whose route is `AVAudioSession`'s (`iOS/AGENTS.md`).

- A **voice** is one file being rendered: a decoder filling a ring, a gain with at most one pending ramp, and an optional queued successor file. At most one voice is **current**, the transport's track. The rest are **retiring**, fading out after a track change, seek or stop, and dying on their own.
- The **transport** (`AudioPlayer.m`) is the state machine `{Stopped, Loading, Playing, Paused}`. State changes run on the player queue. Delegate events are dispatched to main. Nothing in the transport waits for a fade.
- The transport hears three things from the bus, through a **drain** it runs itself: a voice went **live** (its first frames are buffered); the current voice passed its **boundary** into the queued successor (promote, `didAutoAdvance`); a voice **ended**. A current voice's end is `didFinishPlaying` on clean EOF or an attributed error on failure. A retiring voice's end feeds the output-liveness fold.
- **Every fade exists only to keep the output click-free.** A ramp is evaluated per frame on the audio thread from the frame index, so it lands on its target exactly. **Declick** (Settings > Audio Output, default on) decides what a transport edge does: on, the ≤10 ms declick and every other frame untouched; off, a cut. A crossfade longer than the declick is the user's choice and fades either way. Bit-perfect output holds the crossfade at the declick, so with Declick off it applies no gain at all.

The reading path is `AudioPlayer.h` → `AudioPlayer.m` → `AudioVoiceBus.h` → `AudioPlayer+Pipeline.h` → `AudioMasterBus.h` → the platform half.

**`AudioPlayer.m` is the single writer of the state it publishes. Each category owns one vocabulary and declares its methods in its own header.** All of them share `AudioPlayerInternal.h`, which also declares `(PlatformOutput)` once for the two platform implementations. **That shared header is the cost of every split.** A category that pushes more state into it than it takes out of `AudioPlayer.m` is not worth making.

**Loading keeps its intent live, and buffering is a flag beside the state, never a state** (Opening a file, below). Loading covers the in-flight open and reports zero position and duration. Play/pause during it toggles whether the opened file starts or parks, and a seek replaces its start position. Exactly one of `isPlaying`/`isPaused`/`isStopped` is true, and `isLoading` is orthogonal. A pause reports Paused the moment it is requested. An action that must order itself after already-submitted transport commands calls `getPlaybackIntent:forTrack:` once. Loading answers the request's intent, otherwise `(position, state == Paused)`.

**`PlaybackRequestCoordinator` owns the in-flight open**: identity, row, intent, slow-load state and what the delegate must be told (Foundation-only, tested). An identifier is never reused, not even across `invalidate`, so a worker blocked on a dead mount cannot consume a later open of the same path. A re-drop or replay rebinds the row in place instead of starting a second open, re-firing `didBeginLoading:` when the request is already slow. A seek is accepted on either the row or the exact submitted play. The identifier covers a seek issued before its play reached the queue, and the row one issued after.

**The published tuple is written whole.** `publishState:voice:file:window:startSeconds:baseFrames:` writes state, voice, file (as its rate and the track's window, so a getter never messages an object) and the position's origin under `_stateLock` in one acquisition. The two `unpublishVoiceOnQueue…` variants and `republishEstimatedWindowOnQueue` write part of it and never move the origin. The terminal variant, at a track's natural end, drops the file with the voice, so a stream's last reader can go. The stall's park (`publishPausedWithoutVoiceOnQueueAtSeconds:`) moves the origin with the state, voice and file in one acquisition, and keeps the window. Position is `startSeconds + (consumed − promotedBaseFrames) / busRate`, read from the voice's own counter, so it holds across an output stop and needs no cache.

## The voice bus

`AudioVoiceBus.h` carries the contract. This is the shape.

| File | Owns |
| --- | --- |
| `AudioVoiceBus.h` | the contract: voices, ramps, events, the snapshot, and `VibeVoiceBusRender`'s signature |
| `AudioVoiceBusInternal.h` | the private header: the slot and mix structs, the record, every ivar, the `(Decoder)` methods the queue side calls, and the `(Testing)` seam |
| `AudioVoiceBus.m` | the queue side: identity, starts and binds, ramps, stopped and withheld reads, the successor hand-off, kills, snapshots, the drain and inline decoding |
| `AudioVoiceBusRender.m` | the audio thread: `VibeVoiceBusRender` and its mix helpers, in plain C |
| `AudioVoiceBus+Decoder.m` | the decode pool's side: the prepare and its conversion, failures, the recycle, the fills, the converter feed, the chunk decode, the stream's end and its reopen |

**Format.** The bus has one format for its life, the output's: float32 non-interleaved stereo at the output's rate. So a mode toggle or a device rate change rebuilds the segment, and the bus owns conversion. A file is read direct when its rate, sample format and channels are the bus's (`VibePCMFormatsMatch`, the one rule the bus, the pipeline and the bit-perfect splice share). Under bit-perfect output the prepare has moved the device and the output to the file's rate first, so a stereo file at that rate is the bitstream unchanged.

Otherwise it is converted on the decode queue by a `VibeResampler` (`AudioResampler.h`), which also serves a device that refuses a file's rate. That is **r8brain-free-src on both platforms**: linear phase, its 24-bit preset, a 1% transition band, with no quality setting (`docs/audio-quality.md`; `Tests/Audio/ResamplerQualityTests.m` holds it to its bounds). It pulls float32 through a `VibeResamplerInputProc`. A file that runs dry with the stream open answers no frames, which ends that fill and keeps the filter primed for a successor. r8brain has no end-of-stream call, so the stream's end is the silence flush below.

A channel difference is mixed first, at the file's own rate, through AudioToolbox's standard matrix (`kAudioFormatProperty_MatrixMixMap`). A 5.1 file's center and surrounds fold into stereo at −3 dB and its LFE is dropped. Mono goes into every channel, and a file with no standard layout keeps its first channels. So a converter carries only the bus's channels. Every file reaches the bus as the float32 its reader decodes. A lossy one's precision is left to the output's one rounding into the device.

**The decoders** (`docs/audio-quality.md` has the measurements for each):

- **MPEG files (MP1, MP2, MP3) are decoded by dr_mp3 by default.** Apple's MPEG decoder offers only 16-bit integer output on arm64, which rounds without dither and clips a master's overs. Its x86_64 build outputs float32 and still clips them. The mac's Settings > Advanced > MP3 decoder chooses Apple's instead (`AppSettings.appleMPEGDecoder`, applied through `VibeSettingsLiveEffectMP3Decoder`). iOS keeps dr_mp3. A handle keeps the decoder it opened with, and a prefetch opened under a since-changed choice is reopened (`decoderChoiceIsStale`). `set_decoder` overrides the choice for a session, and `dump_audio_path` reports `decoder`.
- **FLAC files are decoded by dr_flac**, with no choice. Its output is Apple's bit for bit at under half the CPU, it seeks without scanning from the start, and it plays legal FLACs Apple's codec refuses. dr_flac answers the file's facts too. CoreAudio's parser never opens a FLAC.
- **WAV, W64, RF64 and AIFF files are decoded by dr_wav** the same way, when they hold a coding it decodes. Anything else in those containers stays Apple's.
- **The open picks one of Vibe's own decoders by the file's first bytes, before any parser** (`openOwnDecoderInterleaved:mpeg:`). A RIFF, RF64, Wave64 or FORM header goes to dr_wav. `fLaC`, after any ID3v2 tags, goes to dr_flac. An Ogg page whose first packet is Vorbis goes to libvorbis. Under the dr_mp3 choice, MPEG frames go to dr_mp3, after any ID3v2 tags or in a WAV's data chunk. In a file named as MPEG, the first frame may lie up to 64 KB past the tags, as CoreAudio allows. A file the decoder refuses goes to CoreAudio's parser. A WAV above 384 kHz is one. The check reads the file's first bytes, and the ten after each ID3v2 tag. The parser reads them anyway.
- **dr_flac and dr_wav state a file's facts as CoreAudio's parser does**: the format's flags, its packet and frame sizes, its width and its channel layout. A PCM sample narrower than its bytes keeps its width, high-aligned: 20 bits in 3. dr_wav rounds an AIFF's up to whole bytes, so the open reads the COMM chunk's width itself. An AIFF's layout is its CHAN chunk (`VibeReadAIFFChunk` reads both). A WAV's is its channel mask. A FLAC's follows from its channel count. There are two known differences, and in each Vibe states the true width. The parser calls a sowt AIFF-C's 24 and 32-bit samples 16-bit. It also ignores an extensible WAV's valid bits and calls 24 bits in 32-bit containers 32-bit. Vibe states 24, and a 24-bit device format then carries the file. `testOwnDecodersStateWhatCoreAudioStates` compares every field and every surround layout's stereo fold on each fixture. Without the parser, a FLAC opens 3 to 14 times faster and a WAV or AIFF twice as fast (`open.*` in VibeBenchComponents).
- **The MPEG open frames the stream itself** (`AudioFileHandle+MPEG.m`). It states CoreAudio's facts and length, except where CoreAudio is wrong. CoreAudio misses the Info frame of a mono or an MPEG-2 file, so it plays their encoder padding. It stops counting packets where a stream changes mode. It refuses free format. The walk takes the priming and remainder from the LAME tag, or from iTunes' iTunSMPB comment. It trusts a Xing, VBRI or iTunes count only when the count fits the file. The stated bytes must cover the audio, apart from tags after it, and each frame must get a size the stream's frames can have. The remainder is trimmed only when the stream holds exactly the packets stated. A frame that states no bit rate is free format. It runs to the next header of the stream, never past dr_mp3's largest free format frame, whether the whole stream is free format or damage left one frame so. The channels are the most any frame walked at open holds. A mono frame plays in both channels, and a stereo frame is mixed down. MPEG in another container, such as CAF, is Apple's. `testOwnDecodersStateWhatCoreAudioStates` compares every MPEG fixture.
- The vendored dr_ copies carry Vibe's fixes (`ThirdParty/AGENTS.md`). AAC decodes to float natively.
- **Ogg Vorbis is decoded by libvorbis on both platforms.** CoreAudio's parser sees only a Vorbis file libvorbis refuses. The handle's open reads the first Ogg packet and gives a Vorbis stream to vorbisfile (`openVorbisInterleaved:`). iOS's Ogg reader refuses Vorbis. macOS's decoder starts each file 128 frames late, and `testOggVorbis` fails once it no longer does. vorbisfile answers the facts: the rate, the channels in Vorbis I's order as labels, and the length from the last page's granule position. A chained file whose streams change rate or channels goes to the parser. The handle counts the position itself, since vorbisfile's follows granule positions, which damage can set to anything.
- **TRAP: a Vorbis open reads the first audio page and the last 64 KB.** A file still downloading needs both, so the tail window for Ogg must hold 64 KB (`VibeAudioFileTailWindowBytes`).
- Ogg Opus is CoreAudio's own reader and decoder, on both platforms.
- **Apple's Opus decoder ignores the header's output gain**, which RFC 7845 says a player must apply. The handle reads it from the `OpusHead` packet at open and scales Apple's decode by it (`VibeOpusOutputGain`). The samples are then bounded at +12 dBFS, as a damaged MP3 frame's are (`kVibeDecodeSampleBound`). `testOpusPlaysAtItsHeaderOutputGain` ends with a canary that fails once Apple applies the gain itself.
- **TRAP: FLAC in Ogg is refused at the parser open**, since that reader reports it no length and decodes only its first page. The metadata fallback opens the same way. The TagLib parse refuses it as FLAC. Its row then fails to parse rather than look playable (`AudioFileHandle.m`, `TagLibAudioFile.mm`, `testFLACInOggIsRefused`).
- **TRAP: a CAF's info chunk is checked before CoreAudio's info parser reads it**, since a forged entry count makes that parser loop forever. `infoDictionaryReadable` walks the chunk headers through the handle's own reads. It answers NO when the info chunk is absent, runs past the file, or declares more entries than its body could hold. A streamed CAF's data chunk of size -1 ends the walk, so an info chunk after it is dropped.

`audioPathSnapshot` reports the source segment and the conversions in force.

**Slots.** Eight, each a plain C struct with a power-of-two ring of at least one second at the bus rate. A slot is allocated once and freed only after the bus's pointer was withdrawn from the master bus and no render is inside it (`afterRenderLeavesOnQueue:`). A render not seen outside within the wait's bound keeps the bus alive until it is. State runs `free → armed → live → dead → free`:

- The queue does free→armed and armed→dead. The decoder does armed→live, and armed→dead on failure before the first frame. **Only the audio thread does live→dead** (end of stream, or a retire landing; a kill is a retire of zero frames). An armed slot was never rendered, so its death needs no render to retire.
- dead→free runs on the slot's own decode queue after a render has completed past the death. **TRAP: on any other queue it disposes the converter under a turn still in flight.** It cleans only the death it was queued for and clears the slot's generation. Every decode turn checks that generation first, so a turn queued for the dead voice cannot enter the slot while the next voice binds it.
- Counters are never reset. `written` and `consumed` are monotonic for the slot's life, and the recycle aligns them. A bind records the two origins a snapshot subtracts as atomics, with the generation as the seqlock's version. So a snapshot polled across a slot's reuse retries instead of mixing two voices.
- A start always receives an identity. At two free slots the oldest retiring voice is cut. A start that still finds no slot is **pending**, bound when the drain frees one, however long that takes. A free slot guarantees a queue for its own file, and a successor never holds up binding.
- **Every started voice ends exactly once through the drain**, a pending one killed before it had a slot included.

**The audio thread** runs `VibeVoiceBusRender`, a C function under `CA_REALTIME_API` and `-Werror=function-effects`: a lock, allocation, Objective-C send or dispatch call is a build error. It reads plain memory and atomics, writes the buffers, stamps from the block's timestamp, and never signals anyone. Per live voice it adopts a new ramp sequence from the current gain (adoption un-pauses). It mixes `min(frames, written − consumed)` at the per-frame gain, in at most two contiguous spans, one vDSP operation per channel each. It zero-fills the rest as an underrun unless the end is known, and lands a pause or retire on its exact frame. `renderSequence` and `inRender` let the queue tell "no render is inside any slot" from "the output says it is stopped".

**Gain.** One 64-bit atomic per voice: target, frames, curve, action (none, pause, retire) and sequence. The declick curve is **linear**, since a 10 ms log ramp's last-sample slope fails the envelope bound at 44.1 kHz. Every longer fade is one side of an equal-power crossfade (`FadeMath.h`, tested per frame). **`rampOnQueueToGain:` is the one gain rule.** Under bit-perfect output every ramp is clamped to the declick length, since a play submitted before the mode landed carries the length it was retired with. With Declick off every declick-length ramp is zero frames, a cut, in either mode, while a longer one is the user's crossfade. `bitPerfectOnQueue` is the mode itself, which the segment choice reads. Nothing renders under a stopped output, so retiring voices are killed there, and a pause is a cut that lands at the first render after any restart.

**The decode pool** is eight serial queues per bus (`QOS_CLASS_USER_INTERACTIVE`), or the caller's thread under the frame-driven test pump. An unrelated file takes an unused queue, so a stalled old read cannot hold its start. Overlapping reads of one handle, a queued successor's included, stay on one queue (`decodeQueueReadingFile:`). Queue ownership follows every occupied slot using it, since a seek can still use that reader after the old slot is freed. Only the assigned queue touches an `AudioFileHandle` after a start.

- **TRAP: a voice has left its file only once its reads are stopped for good and its last turn is over** (`fillScheduled` clear). On `readsAllowed` alone a second queue takes the cursor with the old read still inside, and a withheld voice reads again. A voice may still occupy its slot, fading what it buffered.
- A successor whose file another queue still reads waits in the record's `waitingSuccessor`. The drain hands it to the decoder once that queue has left the file (`publishWaitingSuccessorForSlot:`). A voice that ends first ends as an ordinary track end.
- The decoder asks for a drain (`needsDrain`) when a voice goes live or fails before its first frame, when a stopped voice's last turn ends, and when a slot frees. So these happen under a stopped output too, which has no drain timer.
- Turns are 4096 frames: live at one, filled to the ring, topped up below half.
- **Clean EOF follows a successful read**: a short or empty read, or reaching the declared end. A read error never becomes EOF. A refused seek or read ends with `VibeVoiceEndFailed`. The record keeps the error, the failing URL and the exact handle until the drain consumes it. A failed read's valid prefix is published once. A converter that cannot be made fails the prepare, and a made one cannot fail. A refused successor seek flushes the shared converter's predecessor tail before ending. An unheard successor cannot fail the predecessor's row. Retired and superseded voices cannot fail a newer submission.
- **The stream's end is declared** (the converter's tail pushed out and `endOfStream` published) only once the render is within two chunks of the file's last frame, or a successor needs a converter of its own. Until then a converter stays open past its file, primed for a successor named late. **TRAP: the tail is pushed out with silence and cut at the frames fed, at the bus rate (`streamEndForRecord:written:`).** r8brain is never told the end, and a fill that stops at zero frames leaves its filter's length of the file inside it. A bus-format file has nothing to keep open and ends at once.

**A stopped voice's waiting read is woken.** The call that stops a voice's reads for good (`stopReadsOfSlot:`, behind `stopReadingForVoice:`, `stopReadingThen:`, a kill, and the drain that reports a death) interrupts its file and successor (`AudioFileHandle interruptReads`). A turn parked at a streaming file's edge then returns at once instead of holding the queue and the file. The transport also stops a dead voice's reads when it retires one. A voice retired at declick length reads no more, so its file may be handed to the next voice at once. An interrupted read or seek of a stopped voice ends its turn and writes nothing. One whose reads are still allowed fails as any read error does.

- **TRAP: only the call that clears `readsAllowed` interrupts**, since a voice already stopped may have handed its file on.
- **TRAP: reads are allowed again only at a voice's positioning seek** (`decodeChunkForSlot:`, its first and each successor's), on its own decode queue. The voice before it was stopped and interrupted on the player queue before this one started. A turn of it still in flight put this voice on its serial queue. So no other read is inside the file. A stop between the allow and the seek is caught by the re-check after it.

Every interrupt is issued on the player queue before any newer voice on the file can start, so that ordering follows from where the calls sit, not from timing. The frame-driven pump's inline fill would block on a waiting read, so the render suite plays streams on the decode pool.

**The successor.** `queueSuccessor:` hands the parked file to the current voice. The decoder continues into it at the file's end and publishes the **boundary** before the successor's first frames, so a render that sees them sees where they begin. The boundary is computed from the frames the converter was fed, at the bus rate, never read off `written`. The resampler holds its filter's length of the first file back until the successor pushes it out, so a boundary read off `written` promotes the next track early. A successor in the same format and decode format continues through the same converter, which is told nothing of the boundary, so the filter carries across it. Any other successor gets a converter of its own after the first one's tail.

- The hand-off is one atomic `{none, queued, switching}`. The decoder wins queued→switching and an unqueue wins queued→none. **The decoder's claim commits the voice from the moment it is made**, before the boundary is published. So an unqueue against it reports the switch and the transport re-voices.
- **A stream whose end was declared before its successor was named is reopened**, with a converter of its own. **TRAP:** only the audio thread decides the voice reached its end. The decoder withdraws the end, waits out every render that could have loaded it, and reads the verdict. A voice that reached the end died there and ends as it would have. Otherwise the boundary is published at the old end and the stream goes on. **TRAP:** that wait is bounded (100 ms) and yields to stopped reads. With no verdict the end goes back and the voice ends there. A stuck render must never hold the decode queue.
- **One voice chains any number of successors.** The drain reports boundaries by value, and the transport queues the next only after the previous one was reported.

**The drain** (`drainVoiceBusOnQueue`) binds pending voices, emits each voice's events in the order live → boundary → ended, tops up rings, and recycles dead slots. It tops up no voice that can write nothing: reads stopped, end published with no successor, or a stream held open past its file while the render is far from it. On hardware it is a timer on the player queue **only while the output is running and a slot is occupied**, plus a hop from the decoder. Under the debug pump it runs after every rendered slice. The timer is 10 ms while anything is due (a fade, deferred render-leave work, or the bus's `promptDrainDue`) and 100 ms otherwise. **What the bus has due is found by the drain's own pass**, slot by slot. A start or a kill raises it at once on the player queue. A boundary or end the decoder publishes at its file's end is at least half a ring ahead of the render, so the steady drain is in time. **A converted stream's end is not.** Held open for a late successor, it is declared only within `kOpenStreamReserveFrames` of the render (85 ms at 96 kHz). So the hold is prompt while it is current (`heldOpenAt` still matching `written`). A successor that continues the stream writes past it, and the cadence returns to steady. A path that makes something due without a drain re-evaluates the timer itself.

## Threading

**Every pipeline mutation runs on the serial player queue. The UI getters take the `os_unfair_lock` snapshot and compute off it**, never a queue round trip. The output unit's HAL calls (bind on macOS, configure, start, stop) each wait on the hardware's IO thread. They run on the unit's own serial queue in the order the player queue issued them, so no device holds the player queue. What the player queue still reads of a macOS device is bounded, with stated exceptions (`Mac/Devices/AGENTS.md`). On iOS it reads the session's rate before every start and its latencies for the reports. The queue's QoS is Default, as is the unit's queue it waits on in `waitUntilIdle`. The latency-critical work, the open and the decode, runs on its own lanes. `make test-audio` checks the conversion through the production renderer against an off-bus reference, and reads the algorithm back through `debugCurrentConversion`.

**TRAP: a handle's last release never closes its file on the thread it lands on** (`AudioFileHandle`'s dealloc). On a network share a close can wait on the server. Handles are let go where main waits: under `_stateLock` at a gapless promote, under `_tableLock` at a recycle, on the player queue when the park is dropped, and on the coordinator's state queue when an open is abandoned. So a handle opened with an `interrupted` block hands its descriptor, or its parser opened by URL, to a serial close queue of its own. Its decoders and a callback parser still close in the dealloc, since they read through the handle. No call site needs to carry a release past a lock. A close stuck on a dead server holds the later ones. The converter opens with no block. Its files close at their release, before it moves them.

**TRAP: every buffer the render is the first to write is written once where it is allocated.** A `calloc`'d page is mapped at its first write, and a fault on the audio thread waits for a free page. Use `vDSP_vclr`, not `memset`: clang drops a `memset(0)` after `calloc` (each allocation carries the `TRAP:`).

**Nothing the render could be inside is freed or reset on a timeout.** A withdrawal publishes the pointer's removal and waits, bounded, for the render to be seen outside. When the bound runs out, the teardown is parked and run at the first later wait or drain that sees the render outside (`afterRenderLeavesOnQueue:`). The player's dealloc leaks the pipeline rather than free under a render. Each hosting is one allocation the render reads through one pointer: the varispeed's stage, and the FX chain's units and scratch. A re-host swaps the pointer, and the old hosting waits for a late render the same way, as does a varispeed kernel the fader replaced.

**The pipeline admits one render at a time.** The master bus's `inRender` is a door the render takes at entry and releases at exit. An output unit's callback that finds another render inside renders silence and touches nothing. That is a stuck render outliving its unit's bounded stop while the next unit began. `renderRefusals` (`debugRenderCounts`, `dump_health`) counts it, and `check_consistency` flags any.

**A voice's identity rejects stale voice work.** A `VibeVoiceID` is minted once per start and is the slot's generation while it holds one. A stale id never addresses a reused slot. A ramp or event for a voice the transport no longer publishes is simply not the current voice. Deliveries to main are guarded by submitted-play identity (below).

## Crossfades, pauses and seeks

**`VibeIncomingFadeMilliseconds` (`FadeMath.h`, tested) is the one place that decides a play's fade length.** `crossfadeMilliseconds` defaults to the 10 ms declick minimum, which Settings > Playback's slider shows as off, up to `kVibeCrossfadeMaxMilliseconds`. It applies only when a play replaces an audibly playing track. First plays, the pause/seek/stop declicks and `play:atPosition:startPaused:` force the minimum. A track change is a true crossfade on one bus: the outgoing voice fades on its own ramp while the incoming one fades in beside it, and nothing is rewired.

**A track end crossfades from inside the player**, since the shell's `play:` after it would find nothing audible. Each drain asks `maybeCrossfadeIntoParkOnQueue`. `VibeTrackEndCrossfadeMilliseconds` (tested) says when it is due: the setting held to half of each track and to what remains, one steady drain early. The park then starts as its own voice fading in while the current one fades out. `adoptTrack:…`, which the gapless promote ends in too, makes it current and tells the shell through `didAutoAdvanceFromTrack:toTrack:`. Nothing parked (the playlist's end, Pause at track end), an estimated window, buffering and bit-perfect output each leave the ordinary track end.

**With Declick off every declick-length edge cuts**, in either mode. `testBitPerfectTransportCutsWithoutChangingSamples` and `testDeclickOffCutsOrdinaryEdgesAndKeepsTheCrossfade` require every audible frame across seek, pause, resume, a track change and stop to continue an exact excerpt of the source. The second keeps a 2 s crossfade fading. With Declick on, each edge is the 10 ms declick and nothing else is touched (`testBitPerfectDeclickRampsOnlyTheEdges`). The choice is `AudioPlayer.declick`, pushed from Settings > Audio.

**A voice that cannot be heard is killed, not faded**: not yet live, paused, cut with Declick off, or under a stopped output. So only fading voices are `_retiringVoices` (`retiredFades` in the debug channel). Stop, pause, a parked play and the failure reset cut every retiring voice to the declick length. Skips never do, so rapid skips keep the full fade-out.

**A seek is a new voice at the target with the old one fading out beside it.** It is one shape for playing and paused, with nothing rescheduled. `didFinishSeeking:` settles every submitted seek intent, dropped ones included. It does not certify a decoder position. A refused decoder seek takes the current play's identity-guarded error path. **TRAP: the old voice retires before the new one starts** (`revoiceOnQueueAtPosition`). Both read the same `AudioFileHandle`. While a turn of the old voice is in flight, the new voice takes its decode queue, so its first turn positions the cursor after that read returned. A declick-length retire stops the old voice's further reads (`testSeekStopsTheOldVoiceReadingBeforeItsFileIsHandedOn`).

**A pause fades the voice to silence and stops consuming on the exact landing frame.** A resume is a newer ramp, which un-pauses by being newer.

**The deferred idle stop** (Stopped or Paused for 6 s) releases the output device. It is cancelled by generation in `startOutputOnQueue:`, the one funnel every start and resume goes through. It waits past the 6 s while an FX send's tail still rings, re-checking each second for at most the chain's longest declared tail (`AudioFX.sendsActive`, `longestTailSeconds`). A reverb released before a pause rings out, and a send held through the pause cannot hold the device.

**TRAP: only the manual pump may start without an output unit.** Otherwise Playing and `didStartPlaying:` publish with no callback to advance the voice, and the shell gets neither audio nor an error. A missing output unit fails the start with `VibeAudioErrorEngineStartFailed`. A start the unit refuses later, after Playing was published, parks the voice Paused and reports the same error on either platform (`outputUnitFailedOnQueue:`). A refused macOS bind reports `VibeAudioErrorDeviceUnavailable`. Voices keep their state across an output stop. A paused idle stop, a rebind that keeps the output's format and an iOS system stop reschedule nothing. A format change rebuilds the segment and re-voices.

**The source segment is built lazily** at the first settlement, and rebuilt only when the mode or the output's format differs. A rebuild kills every voice, so its callers guarantee nothing audible and re-voice at the retained intent (`Mac/Devices/AGENTS.md`). **TRAP: the re-voice takes the same `AudioFileHandle`, and the old bus's decoder may be inside a read of it**, since its queued turns retain the bus. So the rebuild stops the old bus's reading and is told once every one of its decode queues has left its turn (`stopReadingThen:`). Until then it withholds every file the old bus may be inside from the new bus's voices (`filesInUse`, `withholdReadsOfFile:` / `allowReadsOfFile:`, counted per retired bus in `_retiredDecoderFiles`). One report covers all eight queues, so a read stalled on any of them withholds every file the old bus held. `filesInUse` is read under the table lock the decoder writes a record's file pair under, so a handoff in flight lists the successor in one field or the other. A voice started on a withheld file reads nothing until then. A successor queued on one is refused and asked for again. **The player queue never joins the decoder**, since a read on a stalled mount would hold every transport command behind it.

**Pitch is the varispeed's ratio, 1 + pitch / 100, and whether it is in the chain at all.** At zero pitch the render plays the bus straight, with nothing converted, delayed or copied (`testZeroPitchIsBitPerfectAndTogglesAreClickFree`). The pipeline hosts one `FX/AudioVarispeed` per source segment, at zero pitch. The render hands the stage each slice, and the stage pulls the bus through `VibeMasterBusSource` at the slice's stamp. `applyPitchOnQueue:` sets the stage's pitch and frees the table it replaced through `afterRenderLeavesOnQueue:`. Bit-perfect output and iOS have no varispeed. The stage's design and tests are `FX/AGENTS.md`'s.

## Prefetch and gapless

**`prefetchTrack:` on every track start pre-opens what follows the track at its end**, through the playlist's `trackEndSuccessor` (root `AGENTS.md`). The next `play:` of that source consumes the parked `AudioFileHandle`. The request state is `AudioPrefetchRules.h`. A prefetch of another source (`AudioTrack.sourceKey`, so another row of the file being opened too) requested during an open is retained and suppressed. That open's success resumes it. Its failure, abandonment, a newer play (same-path rebind included) or a later prefetch drops it. Its identity also drops every late callback. There is no handshake with the loading side, because the foreground rule is derived inside the coordinator (`Loading/AGENTS.md`).

**At play submission an unrelated prefetch is cancelled before the foreground open starts**, so its transfer cannot compete with the file the user chose. A prefetch of the track being played stays alive. Its handle run and the playback run ride one path claim, and whichever succeeds first consumes the play request. The winner retires the loser's park state, so a play never leaves its own track parked. Repeat One parks it again on purpose, by the prefetch after its start.

**The successor is the park itself.** `maybeArmSuccessorOnQueue` queues the parked file on the current voice when the crossfade is at its minimum or the successor continues the recording (`VibeGaplessArmAllowed`). Under bit-perfect output the next file must also want the device's current rate and channels, since one voice cannot switch the device. No second handle exists. The boundary event promotes the successor in place, with no stop and no fade. The bus frames consumed before it become the new base for the position. It fires `didAutoAdvanceFromTrack:toTrack:`, so the controller advances the playlist without `play:`. It **consumes the park**, so a replay of the promoted row opens fresh.

- **Repeat One parks the playing track's own object**: a second handle on the same file, spliced into its own start like any successor (`testATrackSplicesIntoItselfGaplessly`; `testACueRowSplicesIntoItsOwnWindow`).
- **A track's end fires exactly one of `didFinishPlaying:` or the auto-advance callback, never both.**
- When the track-end successor changes under a queued one (a playlist edit, or a change of shuffle, repeat or Pause at track end), or the crossfade setting is raised, `unqueueSuccessorOnQueue` drops it. The voice then ends at its own file. If the decoder had already switched into it, the current file is re-voiced at its position, so the buffered successor dies with the old voice. Lowering the setting re-queues the park.

The MPEG open honors LAME and iTunes gapless metadata. CoreAudio honors iTunes' for AAC. So tagged MP3 and AAC files continue seamlessly, as all lossless files do. An untagged MP3's encoder padding is the one gap this cannot remove. `isGaplessArmed` surfaces the queued state to `dump_state`.

**A main-thread action snapshots its track at dispatch**, because a promote can land between the action and its queue block. `seekToPosition:` and `finishCurrentTrack` drop the request if the boundary advanced it.

## Cue rows play a window

**A cue row is a window of its file, and the player plays it as if it were the whole file.** So every track-end path (gapless promote, `didFinishPlaying:`, pause at track end) applies at the window's end unchanged. `VibeCueWindow` (`PlaybackIntent.h`, tested) turns the row's CD-frame window into file frames at the file's rate, at settlement, at arm and at promote. Two rows meeting at a marker round to the same point, so they stay contiguous at a rate 75 does not divide. The tuple publishes the window as the length, so `duration` is the window and `position` starts at its start. `VibeClampedStartFrame` clamps every start and seek inside it.

The bus takes an end frame per voice and per successor: the row's cue end in file frames, unclamped, or 0 for a window running to the file's end (`AudioTrack endFrameInFile:`). It is never derived from the length. So a whole file ends where its stream really ends, not at a lossy or streaming length's estimate. The bus stops reading at the end frame, counting from its own cursor, never from `framePosition`. That answers 0 when `ExtAudioFileTell` fails, and an end clamped against it reads into the next row.

**Opens and handles are per file; what sounds is per window.** The open's rebind stays keyed by the file's path, so a pick of another row of a file being opened rebinds that open, and settlement reads the new row's window. The park, the prefetch and the successor compare `AudioTrack.sourceKey` (the path plus the window). So the next row of a file is a park of its own, and one prefetched while its file is still opening waits behind that open. **The next window of the same file continues gaplessly whatever the crossfade** (`AudioTrack isFollowedContiguouslyBy:`, `VibeGaplessArmAllowed`'s second input). Otherwise one recording would crossfade with itself, or gap at every marker. **TRAP: that is the one successor a long crossfade can have, so an explicit play of it must not take the park** (`playOnQueue`). The voice retiring over the crossfade keeps reading, and may be inside that very handle. The play opens its own.

## Settlement identity

**A settlement for a superseded submission is never delivered, and it is matched by submitted-play identity, never by track** (root `AGENTS.md`). `didStartPlaying:` and the play-path errors are dropped on main by `submittedPlayIsCurrent:`. It is checked inside the delivery block, because what matters is whether a newer play existed when the callback ran. The comparison is `PlaybackDeliveryRules.h` (tested): `VibePlaybackDeliveryIsCurrent`, and `VibePlaybackSubmissionStateIsUnchanged` for the media-services reset, where zero is a valid initial snapshot. The coordinator's own rule cannot cover this: once a result is dispatched to main, nothing can retract it.

**Resume and seek-restart failures use the same identity, though their errors carry no URL.** They belong to the sounding voice, whose `_activeSubmittedPlayIdentifier` survives gapless promotion. A same-row replay could otherwise make the shell accept the old error and tear down the newer play's state.

**TRAP: the counter is `_nextSubmittedPlayIdentifier`, which only increments, never `_lastSubmittedPlayIdentifier`.** That one is the pre-Loading handoff, cleared to 0 once its play reaches Loading. Compared against it, every settlement reads as superseded, nothing is delivered, and whatever the shells release on settlement is stranded.

## Opening a file

**Files open off-queue against an abandon deadline, so a cloud placeholder cannot wedge playback.** A slow open surfaces Loading instead. `AudioFileOpenTimeoutMath.h` (`Loading/Claim/`) owns the monotonic deadline: a 60 s no-progress baseline, extended (never shortened) to 60 s past each positive raw movement. `AudioLoadingConfiguration.openTimeouts` is snapshotted when the open starts, so a configuration change cannot move its deadline. The claim, admission and transfer machinery underneath is `Loading/AGENTS.md`'s.

The coordinator's accepted dataless verdict surfaces Loading for a provider file as soon as the probe settles. A probe still running falls back to the half-second delay, as do local files and the remote streaming backend. Both paths match the open identifier and notify once. The player never repeats the filesystem probe on its queue, where a stalled stat would also block stop and the deadline.

**Progress is matched by the underlying open identifier, never by path or submitted-play identity.** A same-row replay changes submission identity but keeps the same `PlaybackRequestCoordinator.identifier`, transfer and timeout snapshot. A later retry of that URL gets a new identifier, so an older open's movement cannot extend it. `didBeginLoading:` carries the open identifier to both shells. Each forwards `CloudTransferRegistry`'s movement for that file under it (`Loading/AGENTS.md`). The player accepts `noteOpenProgressForOpenRequestIdentifier:` only while both the pending request and the playback-open slot carry the identifier.

### Reading a file still downloading

**A file a remote transfer is still writing opens as a stream** (`CloudFileMaterializer availabilityForURL:`, `Loading/AGENTS.md`). The handle reads the part file at the final size. Every callback waits for the bytes it was asked for before its `pread`, never the rest of the block and never a read at or past the size. Or it copies them from the transfer's tail window when the wait says they are there and not on disk. A block fill takes one source, the window's bytes or the disk's. **TRAP:** the transfer can finish and rename its part over the URL between the lookup and the open, so a missing part waits for the transfer's verdict and opens the URL.

A wait ends ready, failed or interrupted. A failed transfer is a read failure carrying the transfer's error, for good. **Interrupted is neither the end nor a failure.** `interruptReads` (any thread) or an open's `interrupted:` block ends a wait that would block. The read, seek or open answers NO with an error `isInterruption:` names, and reads are refused until a seek after `allowReads`. **A lost read returns no bytes**, so every decoder sees one clean end of data. That seek restarts the two decoders that keep state across it:

- **TRAP:** dr_flac's seek skips a target it believes it is at and moves inside the frame it holds without reading, so it is restarted at its first frame.
- **TRAP:** ExtAudioFile serves packets the lost read fetched after a later seek, so it is rebuilt over the same parser.
- dr_wav's cursor never moved, and dr_mp3's seek rebuilds its decoder anyway, provided a lost read never shortens the packet count. vorbisfile's seek finds its page again from the bytes. A lost read reaches vorbisfile as a failed read, never as the stream's end (`VibeVorbisRead`).

**TRAP: an MP3 with no Xing, Info or VBRI frame states no packet count.** The walk counts one by reading every frame header to the end, so a stream of one would open only once downloaded. It opens uncounted instead, on a count from the frames of its head (`estimateMPEGPackets:`, `AudioFileHandle+MPEG.m`). The open waits for 16 frames and walks on through those already on disk. If they share the first's bit rate, the count is what that rate gives the audio's bytes. Otherwise it is the count at their average size. **Either is an estimate** (`lengthIsEstimated`), since a headerless file can change rate after its intro. It is kept ahead of the cursor, so the bus never takes it for the end. It is settled from the walk's count when the reads reach the stream's end, or at the first read once the download is complete, on the decode queue. Seeks land exactly either way, since a packet is a fixed number of frames. A head that does not walk is still counted at open, waiting for its download.

- **TRAP:** the settle stores the length, then clears the flag with release ordering, and `lengthIsEstimated` reads it with acquire. A reader of both takes them as one pair (`lengthEstimated:`).
- **TRAP:** while the length is an estimate the player's window follows it (`republishEstimatedWindowOnQueue`, at every drain, a seek and a stall). It grows with the length and never shrinks, so the position never freezes at a short estimate, and a stall saves the position heard. Settled, the window is republished once more, exactly, with `track.duration` and the optional `didSettleDurationOfTrack:`. The origin is untouched, so only the total moves.
- **TRAP:** nothing is refused against an estimate. `VibeCueWindow` keeps a cue end past it and runs a window to the file's end at least a frame past its start. A start past it (a stall's replay of audio heard past it) is kept, the settlement's window reaching it (`finishPlayOnQueueWithFile:`).

A whole file pays one lookup per open and one nil check per fill. The coordinator opens a readable transfer's file this way and interrupts the open when its run is cancelled. The open's deadline, fed by the part file's growth, stops a stalled stream, and no longer applies once the handle is delivered. The voice bus interrupts the reads after it (the decode pool, above).

### Reading ahead on a network mount

**A file on a network mount reads ahead** (`Loading/Transfer/NetworkReadAhead`). The read-ahead itself is `Loading/AGENTS.md`'s: its thread, its blocks, its retries and its orphans. The open decides before any I/O, and three things must hold:

- The opener passed an `interrupted` block, and it is not `VibeNeverWaitsForAStream`.
- No transfer's availability answers for the URL.
- The kernel's mount table says the file's mount is a network one (`VibeNetworkReadAheadApplies`).

The coordinator's opens and the waveform loader's read ahead. A nil block (the mac's converter, the debug BPM scan) and the metadata fallback read directly. **TRAP: both roads open the file nonblocking.** A FIFO with no writer then returns at once for `fstat` to refuse, and never parks an uncancellable open worker.

**A read the server fails never fails the open.** The read-ahead retries it until its handle is gone. Only the read-ahead's own `open` and `fstat` fail the open, with the direct road's errors. A persistent read error on bytes the open needs is retried, so that open fails at the open deadline as "Load timed out". A dead server is then a wait, which the buffering hold and the stall below turn into a pause with Connection lost.

**Interrupted reads pause the fetch ahead.** `interruptReads` sets the availability's `readAheadPaused`, and the thread then fetches only a range a wait asks for. So a voice skipped away from stops pulling its file over the network. `allowReads` clears it and wakes the thread.

**The handle holds no descriptor.** Every read waits for its bytes in the availability, as a stream's does, and is copied out of the blocks. A wait that is Ready with nothing copied is the end of the file. The open waits for byte 0, then takes the noted size. The open's canceller wakes only a transfer, so the open's waits ask its `interrupted` block again every 0.25 s. Every later interrupt comes with a wake from `interruptReads`. `holdStream` has no transfer to hold. On a play's handle it raises the read-ahead's span to about 30 s of the file instead. That read-ahead then fetches ahead only while the plays' read-aheads sized before it are idle. The playing file's goes before the prefetch's (`Loading/AGENTS.md`). The QuickTime reader has no callback open, so its URL road finishes the availability first, which ends the thread. **An MP3 is counted exactly at open**, as a local file is. A headerless MP3 whose frames take longer than the open deadline to cross the wire fails to open. **The hold and the stall below apply unchanged.** `waitingForBytes` rises while a read waits for a block. A parked prefetch reads ahead too, and its progress counts against a stall only once the decoder reads it.

**The handle's dealloc finishes the availability first**, with a "closed" error, and only then closes its decoders and parser. The thread exits once its current syscall returns. Until then it is an orphan (`Loading/AGENTS.md`). An open waits while eight are orphaned, and the open deadline still bounds that wait.

### Buffering and the stall

**A stream that falls behind buffers in place, and plays on once** (`updateBufferingOnQueue`, after every drain, from the current voice's snapshot; the render is untouched). The signal is the handle's own `waitingForBytes`, raised only while a read is blocked at the edge. The snapshot copies it with `decoding`. The voice is **held** when its decode waits with less than a quarter second buffered (`kBufferingGraceSeconds`). The grace is in audio, not time, so the hold's fade has frames and a link trickling in many short waits holds as surely as one that stops. The hold is the pause's own ramp and exact landing frame (a cut for a voice not yet heard). It is **released** once half a ring is buffered, the end is known, or the decoder has nothing left to wait for. A read waits for all it asks, so the decoder runs a chunk behind the download's edge.

The transport stays Playing. `isBuffering` is the flag (`dump_state`'s `player.buffering`), and the optional `didChangeBuffering:forTrack:` reports its edges for the current play. A held voice is not `outputAudioActive`. A user pause during a hold is an ordinary pause and ends it. A seek or track change ends it with its voice, and a play resumes the model. There is no start threshold: a stream's voice goes live at its usual chunk.

**The player decides when the shells show Buffering…** (`bufferingNoticeShown`, `ui.bufferingShown`). The drain raises it once a hold has lasted `kWaitIndicatorDelaySeconds`, the delay a slow open waits too. So a shorter hold never flashes it. It ends at the first drain that finds the voice playing and not held, after a release or a re-voice. A stall and leaving Playing or the file end it too. **A new voice for the same file keeps it** (`publishState:…`). So a seek during a shown hold keeps it up. The old voice's hold ends with it. If the new voice holds, the notice stays. If the new voice plays, the next drain ends it. The optional `didChangeBufferingNotice:forTrack:` reports its edges for the current play. It is apart from `didChangeBuffering:forTrack:` because the two move apart and drive different work. The notice rises during a hold, and after a seek it can fall with no hold edge. A hold edge moves the Now Playing clock, and a notice edge redraws the header.

**A hold with no progress for the open deadline's span is a stall** (`AudioFileOpenTimeoutMath.h` over `openTimeouts`, snapshotted at the hold). Progress is the `bytesWritten` of the file the voice's decoder reads: its availability's `progressBytes`, from a transfer or a read-ahead. That is the voice's own file, or the queued successor once the decoder switched into it. A successor not yet read never counts, since its download would hide a stall of the file the voice waits on. A stall is Paused in place with `VibeAudioErrorConnectionLost`. The voice and its file are let go, and so is a park of the same file (Repeat One's). A transfer's claim then loses its last reader and is cancelled, and a read-ahead's thread ends with its handle. A stall is never a track end. The stall's park is the one Paused with no voice or file (`publishPausedWithoutVoiceOnQueueAtSeconds:`), with position and duration held for the getters. A seek moves its position. Resume replays the track from it through the ordinary play path, which fetches afresh. **TRAP: the replay runs in the resume's own queue turn** (`replayStalledTrackOnQueue`), under an identity minted only while the stalled play is still the newest submitted. Queued as a fresh `play:`, it would land behind a stop or play submitted after the resume, and undo it.

**`stop` (File > Close) unloads outright and fires no transport or track-end callback** (root `AGENTS.md`). It supersedes any in-flight open, so a Loading track never starts, and fades the voice to silence. The caller owns the UI reset. `resetToStoppedStateOnQueue` handles stop and failures that unload playback. It retires the current voice, invalidates the open, drops the park and successor, and publishes Stopped. A stream stall or output restart failure can instead leave playback Paused.

## Live output levels

`levelsEnabled` installs the demand-driven meter, and `copyBandLevels:count:sequence:` reads its latest snapshot without the player queue or an allocation. The meter's ownership, render, publication, demand and `--silent` contracts are `Levels/AGENTS.md`'s.

**`outputAudioActive` is the shell-facing liveness fact**: the output running, and either the current voice playing or a voice still fading. Loading intent alone is not, a buffering hold is not, and a paused voice is inactive the moment the pause is published. Its delegate edge is delivered on main only when the value changes. **TRAP: it is not "the output has stopped".** It excludes an FX tail on purpose, and no "tail is now silent" fact is modeled with a timer. That fact is `outputIdle`. It is NO from every output start attempt, a failed one included. It is YES again only when the idle stop has stopped the output, or the iOS media-services reset dropped the unit. On iOS the unit's queued stop has landed by then. On macOS it may still be queued. `audioPlayerOutputDidBecomeIdle:` announces it, and the iOS shell releases its audio session on it (`iOS/AGENTS.md`). A receiver reads the property at the edge, since a newer start may have overtaken it.

## Error text

**`VibeAudioError*` descriptions are for logs and are never localized.** The one line a screen shows is `VibeStatusForPlayError` (`AudioErrorRules.h`, tested), mapping `VibeAudioErrorCode` to a `STR_ERROR_*` string. It lives beside the enum because both screens render the same wording.

## Beta diagnostics

With `VIBE_VERBOSE_LOGGING=1` the player logs:

- `Timeline:` joins input and submission, queue admission, open settlement, the voice start, the first render (the live event and its start-of-consumption stamp), callback decisions and delivery, and the first UI position beyond the published playing position.
- `Phase:` brackets the player queue's side of a device rebind (macOS), output preparation, format confirmation and ownership. A bind, start or stop is the output unit's own `AudioOutputUnit:` line, timed on its queue.
- `Callback:` reports every bus event with the voice's consumed and underrun counts. A stopped voice's read interrupted at a stream's edge logs one line from its decode queue.
- `Buffering:` is one line per episode as it ends: how long it held, how it ended (released, paused, replaced, stalled) and the bytes that arrived meanwhile.
- `Recovery:` explains device decisions.
- `Stall:` reports main-thread, player-queue and output-render-clock stalls. It also reports IO cycles the output unit wrote as silence because the render returned an error (`outputDropouts`, cumulative, in `debugRenderCounts` and `dump_health`, beside `renderMeanMicros` and `renderMaxMicros`).

The main-thread and queue watchers sample at 250 ms and every 500 ms after, six times at most. The player queue is sampled through its current pool thread (`THREAD_IDENTIFIER_INFO`). The render-clock check samples the output's IO thread once per stall, after more than 200 ms without progress. `block_render`, `block_main_deep` and `block_player` exercise the three paths. **An idle beta wakes nothing.** The main thread is watched by a run loop observer whose watchdog runs only while passes happen. The player queue's 100 ms ping ticks only while the output runs or a device phase is in flight (`performDiagnosticPhase:`). The render clock is read at every drain. On iOS a main-thread stall while the scene is not foreground-active is marked as such (`VibeStallWatchNoteSceneActive`), since iOS redraws a leaving app for the app-switcher snapshot.

**TRAP: the unified log truncates a message near 1 KB**, so a sample spans numbered `Stall stack:` lines. Vibe's frames are offsets into the executable. `atos -o <dSYM DWARF> -arch arm64 -l 0x100000000 <0x100000000 + offset>` names them, which is why each beta's dSYM is kept.

Every voice start, resume, seek, gapless promotion, output format change, device rebind, iOS output recovery and late meter installation arms the bounded `Signal:` probe (`Levels/AGENTS.md`). It reads no file, and on hardware holds the meter only for each start's capture. The probe's origin is the pipeline's own clock at the start: the master bus's frames rendered plus the block in flight. Its cutoff, when a track change is still fading out, is the render that saw the last fading voice die. **TRAP: the varispeed emits the bus's frames late by its kernel's half-width.** That is 64 input frames, and ceil(64 × ratio) above a ratio of 1, which play in half / ratio output frames. So the cutoff is padded by `varispeedLatencyOnQueue`, which is 0 at zero pitch, where the render skips the converter. Unpadded, the probe reads the outgoing track's tail as the incoming one's first signal (`AudioPlayer+Diagnostics.m`).
