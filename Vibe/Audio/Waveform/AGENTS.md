# Waveform data

This directory owns the waveform *data*: generation, chunking and persistence. Rendering is `Vibe/WaveformUI/`; the BPM and key analyzers that ride this decode pass are `Vibe/Audio/Analysis/`.

`AudioWaveform` is a C++ structure storing, per chunk, the peak min/max and the energy — a sum of squared samples plus a frame count, so merging partial buffers into a chunk and chunks into a drawn column stays exact in any order — and, when its decode was asked for them, the same sum for each of three frequency bands. `AudioWaveformLoader`, reading through `AudioFileHandle` (`Audio/`), generates the data asynchronously and hands immutable snapshots to the main thread for progressive rendering. `AudioWaveformCache` owns the loader lifecycle and persists through PINCache.

## Two reads: a column's peaks, and a bar's energy

`getChunkAtIndex` combines a column's chunks — whole chunks, columns tiling the track — and is what a shape is drawn from: a peak must never fall between two columns. **A level is never read from it.** `getBarMeanSquares` answers every bar's mean square at once, the mix's or the bands', in one pass over the chunks. A bar's own window is the bar exactly, its edges anywhere in a chunk and a chunk counted by the part the bar covers. **With reach, the bar takes the loudest of the five bar-long windows a quarter bar apart around its own**: whichever way a kick falls against the bars, one of those holds it whole, so its height does not depend on where a resize has put the edges. Reach is the caller's, 0 to 1, blending the two; when to fade it out is the renderers' rule (`WaveformUI/Renderers/AGENTS.md`). The frames are windowed as the sums are, so a window past the track's edge, or past the front of a load still streaming, is the mean of the frames it covers.

## What rides the pass is an input, not a setting this layer reads

`AudioWaveformLoader` takes a `VibeWaveformAnalysis` value — the tempo and key analyzers and the bands — set before `load:`. `AudioWaveformCache` holds the `VibeWaveformAnalysisProvider` block and asks it **once per request, on main**, handing that answer to the request's loader, so the cache lookup and the decode agree on the bands, a provider reading main-confined settings is never called off main, and a settings change lands on the next request. The cache's owner installs it — `MainPlayerController` from the two analysis settings and the theme's style, `PlayerViewController` on iOS from `analyzeBPM` and the card's and widget's styles. An unset provider means none of it runs, which is what the tests get.

Same shape as `FolderArtResolver`'s enabled provider, for the same reason: a decode pass that reached into a settings singleton could not be tested without one.

## The decode pass

The pass is decode-bound — the file's decoder (`Audio/AGENTS.md`: dr_mp3, dr_flac and dr_wav by default, CoreAudio's codecs otherwise) is the floor of its cost, and the read block size does not move that floor — so the loader **pipelines the decode against the processing**: block N+1 decodes while block N runs through the mono mix, the analyzers and the chunker on a serial queue one block behind, ping-ponging two buffers whose reuse per-slot semaphores gate. Wall time comes down to roughly the decode alone, and the output is exact, because the serial queue preserves the stream order the analyzers' framing depends on.

All processing-side state — the progress throttle and its snapshots included — lives on that queue and is read only after the final drain.

## The three bands are decoded only when asked for

3-Band (`WaveformUI/Renderers/AGENTS.md`) is their only reader, so **a decode splits them only when its request asks** (`VibeWaveformAnalysis.bands`, from `WaveformRendererRegistry readsBandsForIdentifier:`). They cost four biquad sections a sample on the processing side, about four times the rest of it, which the decode hides for a compressed format but not for a cheap WAV. When asked, the chunker hands each chunk's slice of the mono stream to the split (`AudioWaveformBandSplit`), which filters it into low, mid and high and adds each band's energy beside the broadband one. **The four sections run side by side in one double-precision vector**, mid's lowpass fed its highpass's output from two samples back so no lane waits on another within a sample: a sample costs one section's latency, where `vDSP_biquad` runs a setup's sections one after another at 2.6x the cost. The mid band lags the others by two samples, which no chunk can see. The crossovers are 2nd-order Butterworth at 300 Hz and 2.5 kHz, mid the one's highpass into the other's lowpass: the handovers cdj3k-mods measured off a CDJ-3000's 3-band display, wide enough that a shallow filter is the right shape rather than a compromise. **One split per decode, on the serial queue**, since its filters carry state across slices and must see them in stream order.

**The band sums are an allocation and an archive key of their own**, read apart from the chunks (`getBandMeanSquares` a chunk's, for the archive, `getBarMeanSquares` every bar's): widening the chunk instead made every style's per-bar merges dearer for data only one style reads (+73% instructions on a Detailed resize). In memory they are float32 sums, like the chunk's energy; **the archive keeps each chunk's mean squares in float16**, 48 KB rather than 96, and multiplies them back by the chunk's frames on read. Means because a long chunk's sum overflows float16, and an entry whose bands fail the finite check would decode again on every play. The optional `bands` key needs no version bump: an entry without it is a waveform no band request has decoded, which is every entry written before them, and whole bands are read back or dropped, never a reason to reject the waveform.

**An entry without the bands is a miss for a request that wants them, and only for it** — and likewise an entry decoded without the tempo or key analyzer for a request that runs it (`CodableAudioWaveform.bpmAnalyzed`/`keyAnalyzed`, archived beside the values; an archive from before the flags reads analyzed, keeping its old meaning rather than re-decoding every cached file). That decode replaces the entry with one holding them, which every later request takes, so nothing is ever invalidated for what it lacks. A request finding a decode without what it asks for in flight for the same window cancels it and waits on its claim like any parked request, restarting once the worker returns. **An owner that comes to want more asks for its track again**: the mac when the theme's style becomes 3-Band or a detection switch turns on (`VibeSettingsLiveEffectWaveformStyle`, `VibeSettingsLiveEffectTrackAnalysis`), the iOS card when the card's or the widget's style does, forgetting its pages' snapshots so each is asked for again as it comes up.

## A cue row's waveform is its window

The loader decodes only the row's window (`cueStart`/`cueEnd`, stamped by the cache from the track, through `VibeCueWindow` on its own handle: seek to the start, length the window), at the full chunk resolution, and the analyzers ride the same window — so a DJ mix gets a waveform, a tempo and a key per track. Everything keyed per file is keyed per window here: the claim (`AudioTrack.standardizedSourceKey`), the detached-loader pool and the disk entry all append the window to the file's key through `AudioTrack.keyByAppendingWindowTo:`, which is the key itself for a whole file. Metadata stays the file's, shared by every row. Slicing one whole-file waveform was rejected: at 8192 chunks per file, a four-minute row of a 74-minute image would get about 440.

Cache-key stats, serial cache lookups, opens and decodes pass through **fixed-slot utility `AudioWorkScheduler`s**. `stat` and the handle open have no cancellation point and can block for minutes on a cloud placeholder, so fixed admission slots are the resource bound. A stat keeps its slot while it synchronously visits the serial cache queue, so a wedged cache lookup cannot grow a tail there either. Playback opens do not pass through these schedulers at all: they run on the coordinator's own user-initiated queue under the handle ceiling, so background analysis cannot starve the open the user is waiting on.

**Two lanes here, not one, because the stages block on different things.** The lookup lane (cache-key stat plus the serial cache lookup) runs two; the decode lane (open plus decode) runs `kMaxDetachedWaveformLoads + 1` = three. They must be independent because a decode is submitted from *inside* a lookup that still holds its slot: sharing one scheduler would let a burst of lookups fill the pending list and then reject or expire the very decode they had asked for — delivered to the delegate as that file failing to load, when nothing about the file had failed.

Pending work is held inside the scheduler — never submitted to `NSOperationQueue` or libdispatch until a slot is free — with an explicit four-item memory bound. Cancellation removes a still-pending block synchronously. If a lane's slots remain occupied for ten seconds, the pending request fails *admission* without pretending its file open ran and timed out. A truly never-returning OS call still owns its fixed slot for the process lifetime; only process restart (or future killable helper-process isolation) can reclaim that thread, but retries cannot multiply it.

## A superseded load is detached, not aborted

`cancelLoad` and a new `loadWaveformForTrack:` stop the old load's *deliveries* but let its decode run to completion and persist, so a skip-ahead — or the iOS pager peeking at a neighbour — turns the next request for that file into a disk hit instead of throwing the work away.

Up to `kMaxDetachedWaveformLoads` (2) detached decodes run behind the active one, bounding concurrent decodes at three; beyond that the oldest loader is cancelled outright. Standardized-path claims are independent of that UI lifecycle: a claim stays registered until its executing stat/open/decode worker really returns. Re-requesting a file whose live detached decode is still running **reattaches** it; re-requesting one whose loader was permanently cancelled parks one replacement and starts it only after the old worker settles. Work which has not acquired a scheduler slot is removed with its claim immediately, so skip storms cannot build an unbounded queue behind wedged workers.

**The claim is unbounded; the wait on it is not.** A worker blocked in an uncancellable stat or open keeps its slot and its path claim until it returns, which nothing in-process can force — but a request parked behind it would otherwise hold the waveform view in its loading state forever, since this path has no equivalent of the player's per-file open timeout. After `kWaveformClaimWaitSeconds` (20) the *wait* is given up rather than the claim: the parked request is dropped and the delegate gets its ordinary terminal failure, while the worker keeps its slot and settles normally whenever it returns. The parked request is dropped rather than left armed deliberately — a retry starting later would deliver a waveform for a track the user has already been told has none.

**TRAP: `detachCurrentLoader` must detach even a loader that reads `isComplete`.** `isComplete` is set on the decode thread *before* its final delivery block reaches the main queue, so "complete" can still have a live delivery in flight, and the detach flag is what `deliverCompleteWaveform` checks on main. Skipping the detach there would let that delivery land as a live one on whatever track had superseded it. (The completed loader is then not pooled — its decode is done and persists on its own.)

The flag is checked **on delivery, not at enqueue**, so a reattach landing first correctly turns it back into a live one.

Progress is different: detached and cancelled loaders do not construct or enqueue progressive snapshots. A reattached loader resumes them from its live decode position. The final waveform still persists while detached.

## A file still downloading rides the play's stream

**The loader opens a streaming file like any other** (`AudioFileHandle` finds the transfer's `CloudFileAvailability`), so its decode fills in as the bytes arrive, at the download's pace, and reaches `isComplete` only once the last byte has; persistence is unchanged. The key it is filed under is the placeholder's size and mtime, which the install keeps, so a later lookup of the installed file finds it. A download that installed another version re-keys every track instead (`Loading/AGENTS.md`); until that install, a cached entry of the placeholder's version is what a lookup finds.

**A guessed length is never persisted against.** A streaming MP3 with no VBR header, constant rate or not, opens on an estimated length (`AudioFileHandle.lengthIsEstimated`, `Audio/AGENTS.md`). The loader decodes on it anyway, so the waveform fills in as the bytes arrive, but a pass sized by an estimate is complete only once the length is exact and within one chunk of the total it sized its chunks by: at its end it waits in `awaitExactLength:` — for the download to complete, interrupted by `cancel` like any wait, then a count from disk — and if the two differ by a chunk or more it decodes the file again, from disk, on the exact length (`load:`, **TRAP**). Under a chunk the estimate moved no boundary but the last chunk's, which a decode ending short already tolerates, so the pass is kept: a two-hour mix whose packet estimate was exactly right was decoded twice because the estimated length counted the priming frames the settled one excludes, which it no longer does (`AudioFileHandle.openMPEGWithDescription:`). Nothing sized by an estimate is ever delivered complete or filed under the file's key: a progress tick stays short of 1 even with every chunk filled, since a receiver reads 1 as the completion (the iOS pager's `isCompleteAtIndex:`), which only the cache delivers. Measured on a real library every headerless file settled at exactly its head's count, so the common case is one pass that filled in early; an estimate that was off costs one more decode from disk, which is what waiting for the exact length cost every file before. A cue row past a short estimate is sized by `VibeCueWindow`'s rule and decoded again the same way.

**A waveform never holds a stream: the play's handles do** (`holdStream`, sent only to handles the coordinator serves). So a stream lives exactly as long as the play wants it, and when the play lets it go — a skip, a stall's pause (`Audio/AGENTS.md`), a failed open — the coordinator cancels the transfer and the decode's next wait fails with it: a read error, never complete, never persisted, delivered as a failure only while that load is still current. This is why a detached streaming decode needs no rule of its own: detached, it still runs only while the stream does, so a pager peek reattaches it and a skip ends it.

**A cancel ends a streaming decode's wait at once** (`cancel`: `interruptReads` on the handle, and the open's `interrupted` block with the file's waiters woken), so the cap's eviction frees its decode slot now, not when the download gets there, and an interruption is logged as nothing. **TRAP: the loader holds that handle only until its load ends** (`load:`): a loader outlives its load, held by its claim and the claim's wait timer, and one that kept the handle kept the file open, a descriptor and its read block per replay of a row on a cold cache. **The 20 s claim wait is not a streaming timeout**: it bounds a request parked behind a cancelled worker, which the interrupt now returns promptly. A streaming decode has no deadline of its own; the play's stall deadline and the transfer's own request timeout bound it, through the cancel above.

**One decode slot per window for the length of the download**: the claim and reattachment keep a second request for the same window off a second slot, so one playing track holds one of the three. Each cue row is its own window, so rows of one streaming image each take one.

## Every delivery carries the track it was loaded for

`audioWaveform:didLoadData:forTrack:`, the terminal-failure callback, and the BPM and key twins — because a delivery can land after the track has changed. Receivers match its `sourceKey` against their current track's, not its URL, since rows of one file share the URL and each has its own waveform and tempo: `MainPlayerController+Delivery` and the iOS `PageWaveformCoordinator` each do, and a tempo or key is stamped on every row sounding that window (`Playlist.stampTracksSounding:usingBlock:`). Failure is delivered only while that loader is still current; it makes the attempt terminal before delivery so a same-window request starts fresh. The BPM and key twins are optional; iOS implements the BPM one (the coordinator forwards it through `PlayerViewController` to `PlaybackController.noteDetectedBPM:forTrack:`) and not the key, since key analysis is macOS-only.

The cache captures that track when the load starts (`_currentLoadTrack`) rather than reading it back at delivery time, and **the reattach path must set it too**, or a resumed decode would deliver under the track it was detached from.

## Language

`.mm` files, because the waveform types are C++. Keep them out of ObjC headers: `AudioWaveformCache.h` forward-declares `CodableAudioWaveform` rather than importing `AudioWaveform.h`, so the UI layer compiles as plain ObjC.
