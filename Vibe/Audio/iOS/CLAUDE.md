# Audio session and output recovery (iOS only)

The files here are the iOS counterpart of `Audio/Mac/Devices/`: what answers when the system moves the audio out from under the output. The carrier of Vibe's render pipeline is the same `AudioOutputUnit` on both platforms (`Audio/CLAUDE.md`), a RemoteIO unit here; the shared `(Carrier)` declaration in `AudioPlayerInternal.h` is implemented here for iOS, and `(Recovery)` receives the session controller’s recovery verdicts.

## AudioSessionController

The `AVAudioSession` half of what `AudioPlayer+Devices` is on macOS.

**The playback category is activated before every play, never at launch**, and released with `NotifyOthersOnDeactivation` once playback sits idle past the player's own idle stop — so an interrupted podcast gets its resume hint.

**No audio object exists before the first play.** Launch sets the pipeline's format from the session's rate, which claims nothing; the RemoteIO unit is made at the first output start, after the play activated the session. `AVAudioEngine` used to instantiate its output unit while its source node was wired at launch, against whatever category the session carried — SoloAmbient by default, which is not mixable — and that alone stopped whatever else the device was playing. Making the unit at launch again would bring that back.

Four events, four delegate verdicts, each mapped onto the player's public API:

| Event | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| Interruption Began / Ended | pause, then resume |
| Route loss (`OldDeviceUnavailable`) | pause |
| Any other route change | classify the output transition, then pause or `recoverOutput` |
| Media services reset | `beginMediaServicesResetWithCompletion:` at notification receipt |

**Was-playing is captured at the interruption's Began edge *only*.** The route-loss pauses that pile up mid-interruption must not overwrite it, or the resume decides from the wrong state.

**Interruption-ended automatic activation is not user intent.** It preserves and checks the route-loss and media-reset blockers before and after the synchronous session activation, so unplugging headphones during a call cannot turn the system's `ShouldResume` hint into playback on the speaker. Only explicit user activation clears those persistent blockers.

**The route-change notification is the only output trigger.** RemoteIO has no configuration-change notification and keeps running across a route change that brings a device in, converting from the pipeline's format to the new hardware rate itself, so connecting AirPods or CarPlay keeps playing. **A route that loses its device is different: iOS stops the unit itself**, about 0.75 s after the change, observed on an iPhone 17 Pro at every Bluetooth disconnect. That stop reaches the player through the system-stop path below, after the route-loss verdict has already paused, so it changes nothing audible — but it means the unit is not guaranteed to survive a route change. For a device coming in, the recovery verdict has the pipeline follow the route's rate, so the bus's conversion stays the only one. The controller records whether the last and current outputs are built-in, external or absent, and an external-to-built-in transition is headphone loss and pauses whatever reason the notification carried; an already recorded route loss, interruption or media reset blocks recovery until a successful explicit session activation. Ordinary recoveries are generation-coalesced before main delivery.

**The three verdicts above are decided in a header, not in the controller.** `AudioSessionRecoveryRules.h` — header-only, Foundation-only, tested — holds `VibeAudioSessionConfigurationAction` (recover on the new route, or park because the output is disappearing), `VibeAudioSessionMayDeliverConfigurationRecovery` (a later route change coalesces an earlier ordinary recovery; any safety verdict received before main delivery blocks it) and `VibeAudioSessionMayAutomaticallyResume` (interruption-ended is a system suggestion, not a fresh user play). Existing interruption, route-loss and reset ownership always wins in all three, which is what makes the rules fail-closed.

**One scan classifies a route, and two things read the result.** `OutputRouteRules.h` — header-only, Foundation-only, tested — holds the fine kind the card's indicator draws (speaker, receiver, wired, Bluetooth, AirPlay, CarPlay, other, none) and the fold onto the coarse none/built-in/external kind the pause/recover decision above is written in terms of. Deriving one from the other is what stops a newly handled port from being external to the indicator and built-in to the unplugged-headphones rule. The `portType` mapping stays in `AudioSessionController.m`: the `AVAudioSessionPort*` constants are AVFoundation's API, and re-spelling their values into the header would depend on their contents. **A Bluetooth route's glyph is guessed from its device name**, the one row that reads the name, because no public API says what the device is (`docs/not-doing/rejected-alternatives.md`). An unrecognised name draws the generic glyph.

**The route pair — kind and the system's device name — is published on a real change and nowhere else.** `recordOutputRoute:name:` writes both under the one lock, so the pair never describes two different routes — and the pause/recover decision reads the coarse fold of the same field — and returns whether anything moved. Init records without publishing (the delegate is set, but `PlaybackController` is still inside its own init); `activateSession` publishes on change, which is the edge where a destination picked against an inactive session — that posts no route notification of its own — first becomes visible; `handleRouteChange:` publishes whatever its verdict, because a new device, an override and a category change are exactly what an indicator exists for. The hop is an unconditional `dispatch_async`, not `onMain:`: this edge fans out to every `PlaybackObserver`, and `activate` calls it from inside an activation the play path is waiting on. **Before the first activation the session can report no outputs at all**, which is `None` — a real answer, not an error.

**A pause verdict during Loading parks the landing** rather than being dropped. The player's explicit `pause`/`resume` methods update the pending request to the requested state, so duplicate system verdicts are no-ops instead of toggles.

## AudioPlayer+Recovery

The player's output-recovery category, on `AudioPlayerInternal.h`'s shared private surface exactly as the mac's `AudioPlayer+Devices` is. It lives here so the mac target never compiles or even sees the API, and `AudioSessionController`'s verdicts are the only callers.

**The system stops the unit under the app, and says so only through the unit.** An interruption stops RemoteIO without asking. `AudioOutputUnit`'s `IsRunning` listener reports a stop it sees with the gate open — the player's own stops close the gate first — through `failureHandler` without an error, and the player stops its output at that edge (`outputUnitFailedOnQueue:`), so the output-liveness fold and the drain timer follow at once and the next start is an ordinary one; what the transport does is the session verdict's. Without it, a resume after a short interruption would find the output "running" and play nothing.

- `recoverOutput` — for a playing voice only (every start follows the route itself), follows the route's rate, then restarts a unit the system stopped. A route at **another sample rate** is followed through `followOutputFormatOnQueue:` (`Audio/CLAUDE.md`): the unit and the bus are rebuilt at it and the track kept at its position and state, so the bus converts once, at the route's rate, and RemoteIO converts nothing; the follow restarts a playing output itself. Otherwise a stopped unit under a Playing voice restarts in place: the voice kept its ring and gain across the stop, so nothing is rescheduled and the position is the voice's own consumed count. **The same follow runs before every output start** — a resume, a play's settlement — through `followCarrierRateOnQueue`, because a route loss or an interruption blocks this recovery until an explicit activation, and the next start would otherwise run on the stale rate with RemoteIO resampling. A restart with no unit to make parks the track Paused (`didPausePlaying:`); a start the unit refuses later parks it too and reports the error, through the failure path both platforms share (`outputUnitFailedOnQueue:`).
- `beginMediaServicesResetWithCompletion:` — **every audio object is dead per Apple's contract**, so this drops them without messaging them (`dropOutputBoundStateOnQueue`: the master bus's gate closes, the bus and the meter are retired through `afterRenderLeavesOnQueue:`, and the unit is released unstopped — its dealloc disposes the instance, which is what the contract asks of an orphaned audio object; a late failure from it never matches the next unit's run generation) and `createOutputOnQueue` recreates the carrier as launch does — the pipeline at the route's rate, the unit made at the next start. It is called synchronously on the notification thread: reset receipt and play submission each enqueue under the same state lock, so a play received after reset runs on the new output instead of being destroyed by a later main-thread rebuild. The recovery position is read from the voice's counter before the bus is dropped, never by messaging the invalid file or unit. `createOutputOnQueue` is shared with ordinary init, so the rebuild is the launch shape. Its main-thread completion runs only after Stopped is authoritative and only if no newer play submission superseded the reset; `PlaybackController` then publishes that state and re-parks the captured track.

## Verifying

The simulator exercises none of this faithfully. Interruptions (ended with and without `ShouldResume`), route changes (headphone and Bluetooth plug and unplug, playing and paused, with a rate follow when the route's rate differs), media-services reset, background audio past lock, the lock-screen card and whether a cold launch leaves another app's audio playing all need a real device. The RemoteIO carrier has not yet had that pass (`docs/future/render-pipeline-follow-ups.md`).
