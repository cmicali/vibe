# Loading: materialization, admission and the transfer registry

Everything between a path and a usable `AudioFileHandle` (owned by the audio core): `AudioFileMaterializationCoordinator` (the claim), `AudioWorkScheduler` and `AudioWorkAdmissionRules.h` (admission), `AudioLoadingConfiguration` (the immutable limits and the open-timeout seam), `AudioFileOpenRules.h` and `AudioFileOpenTimeoutMath.h` (the decisions, among them the tail window a stream fetches for an open that reads a file's end, and `VibeMountReadsAhead`, the rule for which files read ahead), and `CloudTransferRegistry` (which files are on the wire). The player's side of an open, the deadline, progress identity and prefetch, is `Audio/AGENTS.md`'s; the sweep's retries against the foreground hold are `Metadata/AGENTS.md`'s; the progress monitor is `System/AGENTS.md`'s. Three root `AGENTS.md` guarantees are implemented here: the foreground open outranks background downloads, the six-run handle ceiling, and a row shows the loading bar only while a transfer is running.

## One claim, two stages

**A file's journey from dataless placeholder to handle is one path-keyed claim in two stages.** Stage 1 is the one operation that can make a standardized path local; Playback, Prefetch and the two metadata roles affect admission, never identity. Stage 2 is the purpose-keyed handle runs riding it: one current handle waiter per `(purpose, path)`, so playback and prefetch keep independent handles and delivery while sharing the path's transfer. Only a stage-1 Ready or readable claim dispatches a handle open; Yielded, AdmissionExhausted and Failed become terminal outer errors without touching CoreAudio. `AudioFileOpenRules.h` holds the decisions: `VibeAudioFileOpenBeginDelivery` and `VibeAudioFileOpenDetachDelivery` are the two sides of the race for the one Waiting transition, whichever changes it first owns the outcome, and `VibeStandardizedAudioOpenPath` is the one spelling every claim is keyed by, using `VibeComparablePath` without filesystem I/O. **TRAP: `URLByStandardizingPath` stats the target**, so it can stall the player or state queue before the bounded probe begins; compare spelling alone.

**The stage-1 state queue owns in-memory claim state and performs no filesystem or provider I/O.** A first waiter synchronously installs a `Probing` claim, then one private `AudioWorkScheduler` classifies the path off-queue; same-path waiters join and share the probe. A fresh answer starts an immediately admitted claim; a dataless claim that had to wait or be readmitted enters `Refreshing` after reserving its lane, re-probes in that admitted worker, and becomes `Running` only when state accepts the fresh answer. The successful paths are therefore `Probing → Running` and `Probing → Pending → Refreshing → Running`, and dataless readmission routes through `Pending` or `Refreshing` likewise. **A fresh local answer is the run's answer**: an initial probe's settles the claim Ready in the next state turn with no operation, lane or worker, and a start refresh's settles it from that worker without running the operation it made; a local verdict carried from an earlier run is not fresh, and runs an operation that probes again, since the file may have been evicted since. Object identity drops a result from a cancelled or replaced probe, and a ready operation records the path local before any cancellation or rebind restart decision.

The accepted dataless verdict is reported through `onDataless` at most once per open token, on its completion queue and only while delivery is still waiting. Joining an already-classified claim or rebinding a handle run reports it too. Rebinding cancels the superseded token, dropping its queued callback.

**A remote transfer is readable before it is complete, and readable is a field of `Running`, not a state.** The operation's `onReadable` comes from a placeholder store's fetch (`System/Remote/AGENTS.md`). A provider never calls it. It runs on the client's shared delivery queue. From there it only hops to the state queue (its `TRAP:`). There `claimBecameReadable:` matches the run by generation. It drops a readable whose transfer already finished, which the lookup answers nil for. Otherwise it keeps the run's `CloudFileAvailability`, which marks the claim readable. It serves every playback and prefetch waiter as a Ready would. A handle run's open is dispatched in that same turn, never through its token. The claim stays `Running`, and its metadata waiters wait for complete. A playback or prefetch waiter joining a readable claim is served on the next state turn, once its run holds its token. Complete then settles the claim Ready as before, delivering to nobody new; a failure settles its remaining waiters Failed and the availability fails the delivered handles' reads with the transfer's error. **A readable stream is held by what it served, counted in one place, the availability's holders** (`addReader`): each open dispatched while readable holds it from its dispatch until its result has been delivered on the state queue, and each handle it returned from its open until its dealloc (`holdStream`, sent on the open's worker before the open lets go), so the count never dips to zero across the handoff; `onLastReaderGone` asks again, reaching the state queue behind the turn that delivered, so a rebound run's waiter is installed first. Only served handles hold: any other reader of the part file, the waveform's, rides the stream and fails with it (`Waveform/AGENTS.md`). That count stands in for the served waiters wherever a playback or prefetch waiter counted (`claimServesForeground:`): the foreground hold stays raised, so the sweep's ranged reads stay off the wire while the user's stream downloads, and a metadata waiter may still join. A run with no waiter and no reader is cancelled (`cancelRunIfAbandoned:`, the one place a last waiter leaving cancels too), so a skip away from a stream stops its download and frees its lane; a metadata waiter still on the claim keeps it running.

**The eight initial-probe slots are process-wide, and their exhaustion is a bounded failure by design.** Production can present about eleven distinct claims at once (playback, prefetch, the scan and priority metadata slots, seven artwork requests), so eight wedged probes can make a later healthy path expire after the five-second grace. That settles as `AdmissionExhausted`, spends one of metadata's three per-path attempts, and can drop the path from the current scan; it replaces an unbounded tail of uncancellable system calls. Refresh probes do not use the slots: a never-returning refresh stays state-isolated but holds its reserved transfer lane, including the one-wide background lane.

## Admission has two lifetimes

**A transfer lane ends when its materialization run settles, which for a stream is when the download completes or fails, not when its handles open; the handle-run ceiling is a separate lifetime.** A readable claim keeps its lane and its registry entry while the handles it served read the part file, so a lane can be held for the whole download under a playing track, exactly as long as before streaming: what changed is that the open and the start overlap it. The lane counts still bound transfers, not opens, and the abandoned-stream cancel is what keeps a rapid skipper from filling the interactive lane with downloads nobody hears. Each coordinator permits at most six live handle runs, process-wide in the app since production shares one: the root guarantee's two-source bound plus room for four stranded opens. A run ends when its open returns. A streaming run leaves the ceiling at delivery, and its transfer runs on. A streaming open never strands, since a cancel ends its waits. A read-ahead open never strands either. Its syscalls run on its handle's own thread, and every wait it makes can be interrupted (`Audio/AGENTS.md`). What can still strand a run is a whole-file open on a local volume, a provider's file included, and the QuickTime reader's URL open. A symlinked path, and any path the mount table cannot place, takes the direct road and can strand a run. The six therefore stand unchanged. A read-ahead thread a dead mount strands is bounded apart from the runs. At eight orphans, a new read-ahead open waits until one exits. That wait can be interrupted too. An existing `(purpose, path)` run is looked up and rebound before the ceiling is checked, so saturation never duplicates or rejects work in flight; a seventh key is refused at once with `VibeAudioFileOpenErrorAdmissionExhausted`, counted in `requestsAdmissionExhausted`, before materialization starts. The ceiling is a private, purpose-blind safety bound over `_handleRuns.count`, not loading policy: no queue, grace, configuration value or per-handle counter, and prefetch can exhaust it and get a later playback key refused. Waveform work keeps its own two fixed-slot schedulers.

**Transfer-lane admission is the coordinator's own** (`admitClaim:…`): running claims against the lane's maximum, then pending claims against the pending bound with a deadline at the grace, and a file already local starts at once outside the lanes, so only dataless claims are bounded. **Only a dataless run holds a lane slot** (`holdsLane`), and a start refresh that finds its file local hands the reserved one back: nothing times a running read out, so a local read stalled on a network share or a sleeping disk, counted, held the one-wide background lane for as long as it blocked, and every cloud row's metadata expired behind it. `AudioWorkAdmissionRules.h`, tested, is the scheduler's: `VibeAudioWorkAdmission` and `VibeAudioWorkAdmissionExpired` are what `AudioWorkScheduler` reads, here for the dataless probe. Transfer exhaustion and the handle-run refusal both map to `VibeAudioFileOpenErrorAdmissionExhausted`: the file never began, which is not that file's playback timeout.

**A run stays registered until its handle open really returns.** A timeout or superseding request detaches the outer delivery and the materialization waiter; a handle open already begun keeps its run until it returns, and a cancellation before stage 2 removes it. **A whole-file open is uncancellable, since it does no waits. A streaming open and a read-ahead open are cancellable at every wait.** The cancel sets the run's `openInterrupted`, and the open's `interrupted:` block answers it. For a stream the cancel also calls the transfer's `wakeWaiters`. Its wait then asks the block at once. A read-ahead's wait needs no wake: it asks the block again every 0.25 s. Either open, parked waiting for bytes, then returns with an interruption. That reaches no one. The waiter was detached, or a rebound one gets a fresh run. That is what makes the player's open deadline stop a stalled stream or a stalled read-ahead. Rebind and the abandoned-then-rebound restart preserve the same dictionary membership, so repeated retries cannot multiply stranded workers. A never-returning whole-file call stays one of the six until process restart, but it holds no transfer slot.

**Cancelling is the only way to stop waiting, and it is deliberately not separable from marking an admitted handle-open run abandoned.** `finishHandleRun:…` tells "this run produced nothing because nobody was waiting" from "this file would not open" by the run's cancelled mark alone; a waiter cleared without it would let the first be delivered to a later same-purpose/path waiter as a nil file with no error, which every caller reads as the file failing. Before the handle open is dispatched, cancellation simply detaches the central request and removes the outer claim. A completion therefore always carries a file or a reason there is none.

**A run that installs another version than its placeholder stood for re-keys every track.** The production operation compares the file's `cacheKey` before and after the run. A remote file downloads as it is now: a Dropbox file re-uploaded since its folder was listed, or a link changed since its open. When the key moved, the operation sends `AudioTrack.invalidateMemoizedCacheKeys`. No waveform or metadata is then filed under, or served from, the old version's entry. A stream's waveform keyed while it streamed is filed under the old key and orphaned, never served for the new version.

**Stage 1 has two kinds of backend behind one `CloudFileMaterializer`.** A provider's placeholder goes through `NSFileCoordinator`. A remote placeholder goes to the remote fetch installed for its root (`System/AGENTS.md`). The Links store is one on both platforms. The Dropbox mirror is one on iOS. The coordinator cannot tell them apart and must not: both are a dataless verdict and a blocking, cancellable operation, and readable is a callback either may make; only the remote one does.

## The transfer registry

**`CloudTransferRegistry` is the coordinator's publication surface for "which files are on the wire", and only a dataless verdict publishes.** A fresh initial answer governs an immediately admitted run; a delayed or readmitted dataless claim publishes from its `Refreshing` worker's fresh answer. `Running` plus `dataless` means begin was published, so `finishClaim:` pairs the end without a second flag, readable or not: a stream's row shows the bar until the download ends. A local run, and a claim merely queued behind lane capacity, publish nothing, which is what bounds the row indicators exactly as the lanes bound the transfers. **It is the one home of "this file is loading, this far", and it watches each transferring path once.** It is main-thread. Each entry has one `DownloadProgressMonitor`, whose fraction is the best source for the backend (`System/AGENTS.md`). That is a remote transfer's own written count, or a provider's publication or poll. Its observers are weak and many: each shell's row list and its player model. `cloudTransferRegistryDidChange:` is coalesced per main turn and fires on begin, end and a shown fraction's change; the optional `didMoveTransferForPath:` is the monitor's uncoalesced movement feed, keyed by the transfer's standardized path, which each shell matches against its loading open's path, standardized once at `didBeginLoading:`, and forwards to `noteOpenProgressForOpenRequestIdentifier:` by its loading open's identifier (`Audio/AGENTS.md`). **TRAP: no consumer runs a monitor of its own.** A second monitor on the playing file means the registry standing aside for it, and a stream starts at its readable edge, long before its download ends, so whatever ends that monitor at `didStartPlaying:` leaves the playing row's fraction frozen, or never set, for the rest of the download. Row wiring is `Playlist/Mac/AGENTS.md`'s and `iOS/AGENTS.md`'s.

## Configuration

**`AudioLoadingConfiguration` is immutable once built, and its diagnostic surface is for tests and the debug channel only.** The safe tuning surface is the background lane width, the local parse concurrency and the prefetch depth; the interactive lanes, pending bounds, grace, retry count and `openTimeouts` are diagnostic, replaced by `Vibe/Debug/`'s category and the test suite, never by production code.
