# DJ FX (the master-bus segment)

`AudioPlayer` owns one `AudioFX` (readonly `fx`): the Q low-kill high-pass with its W double-cutoff boost, and the send-returns, E a long reverb wash, R and T BPM-synced ping-pong delays on 1/8- and 1/16-note taps fed by the controller through `delayTapBPM`. The topology and threading are `AudioFX.h`'s preamble; the AVFAudio traps (MatrixReverb's stale header ranges, dropped pre-attach mixer writes, EQ bypass clicks, and why the install guards are the nodes rather than an engine handle) are `TRAP:` comments in `AudioFX.m`.

**The segment connects live through `setConnected:inEngine:format:` with the engine stopped.** A connected segment asked for another rate — a device switch across rates, since on macOS the graph runs at the device's rate — is torn down to its last connection and wired whole again at the new format, with the recorded intent re-applied as at install. **TRAP: a connect over a standing fan-out can leave the destination's side at the old rate, and an effect whose input and output rates differ fails to initialize at the next start** (AUNBandEQ, -10868, measured on a 44.1 → 96 kHz switch); `testFXChainRewiresAtANewRate` reproduces it. macOS keeps the lightweight `AudioFX` object from init so controls and the BPM feed remain stable; nodes are created on first enable and retained while bypassed. The player's one `setBitPerfectOutput:exclusiveOutput:enableFX:` call applies both settings through the existing playback-preserving device rebuild. Bit-perfect outranks the stored FX preference; leaving it restores that preference. The player clears effect intent synchronously when a bypass is submitted; the queued bypass only preempts ramps and resets processing and wet tails, so it cannot erase a later UI action. The shell redraws the cleared flags once through `updateFXIndicators`. `masterBusOutputNode` answers nil while disconnected, so the level tap follows the actual route. iOS passes `enableFX:NO` and keeps no FX object.

**`AudioFX` holds plain on-off state per effect; telling a tap from a hold is `TransportKeyMonitor`'s job.** One coupling is enforced here so every path gets it: **clearing `lowKillEnabled` also clears `lowKillBoostActive`**, because the boost modifies that filter and a latched W must not hold the cutoff up while the low kill reads off.

**The numbers are `AudioFXMath.h`, header-only and tested**: the three-way low-kill cutoff, the ping-pong tap and lane times with their no-tempo fallback, the lane feedback and the swell target. The fast logic suite checks the math; `VibeAudioTests` renders the real graph offline (`Tests/CLAUDE.md`).

**`installInEngine:format:` wires the segment once, on the player queue, at first connection; the object exists before its nodes, so intent set early is applied at install.** Every toggle is click-free (cutoff sweeps and gate fades on the player queue with generation preemption), and **off is colorless**: the low kill sweeps to the 20 Hz floor and then swaps its bands to a 0 dB parametric type, an identity biquad (round-off bounded at −200 dBFS by the render suite), because a high-pass parked at the floor still lifted the sub-bass through its resonant peak.
