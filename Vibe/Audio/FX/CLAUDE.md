# DJ FX (the master-bus segment)

`AudioPlayer` owns one `AudioFX` (readonly `fx`): the Q low-kill high-pass with its W double-cutoff boost, and the send-returns, E a long reverb wash, R and T BPM-synced ping-pong delays on 1/8- and 1/16-note taps fed by the controller through `delayTapBPM`. The topology and threading are `AudioFX.h`'s preamble; the traps (MatrixReverb's stale header ranges, the frames-per-slice a directly hosted unit defaults to, the unit that writes back into a lent input buffer) are `TRAP:` comments in `AudioFX.m`.

**The segment is a stage of the render, not a graph.** `VibeFXChainRender` runs in place over the master bus's buffers, on the audio thread, under the same `CA_REALTIME_API` discipline as the voice bus: Apple's units — AUNBandEQ, MatrixReverb (Reverb2 off macOS), AUDelay — are hosted through the AudioUnit C API and rendered with an input callback that copies the unit's scratch into the unit's own input buffer; every mixer the engine graph needed is buffer arithmetic: a gate is a gain the queue targets and the audio thread slews at the rate `AVAudioMixerNode` slewed its volume (full scale per 25 ms, measured), a pan is that mixer's balance law (the far side attenuates linearly), a sum is an add, and the two delay sends' identical low-cuts are one filter over their summed returns.

**Idle FX cost nothing, and dormant FX are sample-exact.** A stage renders only while it has work — a send while its gate is open or its tail (the units' own `kAudioUnitProperty_TailTime`) still rings, the low kill while on, sweeping or settling for 250 ms after it parked flat — and is otherwise reset, forgotten and skipped, so a connected chain with nothing engaged is a pass-through the render suite compares exactly, and `unitRenders` (`debugEngineCounts`) does not advance. Nothing on the audio thread decides activity; it reads the stages' flags. A rested stage joins the render before its gate opens, so it starts from silence under the gate's fade-in. A gate settled on its target is one multiply per channel and a closed one a clear; the sums and the returns' pans are vDSP operations, the pan landing straight in the sum.

**The segment connects through `setConnected:format:maximumFrameCount:` with the output stopped, and is in the render only while connected.** The player publishes the chain's pointer into the master bus after a connect and withdraws it before a disconnect or a re-host (`reconcileFXOnQueue`), so a disconnected segment costs the render nothing — not even its stages' flags — and no parameter write or ramp step reaches its units until the next connect; a disconnect with tails still ringing rests every stage at once and the output is the bus, sample for sample. The units are hosted at the first connect and kept across toggles; a connect at another rate — a device switch across rates, since on macOS the pipeline runs at the device's rate — hosts them again at the new format and re-applies the recorded intent, because an effect cannot convert between its input and output. A hosting is one allocation the render is handed whole, so a re-host swaps it; the old hosting's disposal, a stage's rest after its tail and the disconnect's resets all wait for the render to be seen outside the chain (the player's `afterRenderLeaves`), so no unit is reset or disposed under a render, and a stage engaged again before its deferred rest runs is left as it is. macOS keeps the lightweight `AudioFX` object from init so controls and the BPM feed remain stable. The player's one `setBitPerfectOutput:exclusiveOutput:enableFX:` call applies both settings through the existing playback-preserving device rebuild. Bit-perfect outranks the stored FX preference; leaving it restores that preference. The player clears effect intent synchronously when a bypass is submitted; the queued bypass only preempts ramps and resets processing and wet tails, so it cannot erase a later UI action. The shell redraws the cleared flags once through `updateFXIndicators`. `connected` answers whether the segment is in the chain. iOS passes `enableFX:NO` and keeps no FX object.

**`AudioFX` holds plain on-off state per effect; telling a tap from a hold is `TransportKeyMonitor`'s job.** One coupling is enforced here so every path gets it: **clearing `lowKillEnabled` also clears `lowKillBoostActive`**, because the boost modifies that filter and a latched W must not hold the cutoff up while the low kill reads off.

**The numbers are `AudioFXMath.h`, header-only and tested**: the three-way low-kill cutoff, the ping-pong tap and lane times with their no-tempo fallback, the lane feedback and the swell target. The fast logic suite checks the math, `AudioFXChainTests` renders the chain on its own over a virtual clock (an idle chain exact, a held send echoing at its tap and resting after its tail, the low kill cutting and resting exactly, a rate change), and `VibeAudioTests` renders the whole player offline (`Tests/CLAUDE.md`).

**Every toggle is click-free** (cutoff sweeps and gate fades on the player queue with generation preemption), and **off is colorless**: the low kill sweeps to the 20 Hz floor and then swaps its bands to a 0 dB parametric type, an identity biquad, because a high-pass parked at the floor still lifted the sub-bass through its resonant peak; settled, the unit is reset and skipped, and a reset flat EQ is exact from its first frame (measured).
