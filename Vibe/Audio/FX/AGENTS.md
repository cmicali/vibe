# DJ FX (the master-bus segment) and the pitch stage

`AudioPlayer` owns one `AudioFX` (readonly `fx`). It has the Q low-kill high-pass with its W double-cutoff boost, and three send-returns: E a long reverb wash, R and T BPM-synced ping-pong delays on 1/8- and 1/16-note taps fed through `delayTapBPM`. The topology and threading are `AudioFX.h`'s preamble. The pipeline's half of the API is `AudioFX+Pipeline.h`: the connect, `markDead`, the chain, the render counters and `VibeFXChainRender`. The traps (MatrixReverb's stale header ranges, the frames per slice a directly hosted unit defaults to, the unit that writes back into a lent input buffer) are `TRAP:` comments in `AudioFX.m`.

**The segment is a stage of the render, not a graph.** `VibeFXChainRender` runs in place over the master bus's buffers on the audio thread, under the voice bus's `CA_REALTIME_API` discipline. Apple's units (AUNBandEQ, MatrixReverb, AUDelay) are hosted through the AudioUnit C API. Each renders with an input callback that copies the scratch into the unit's own input buffer. Every mixer is buffer arithmetic. A gate is a gain the queue targets and the audio thread slews (full scale per 25 ms). A pan is a balance law. A sum is an add. The two delay sends' identical low-cuts are one filter over their summed returns. **TRAP: every unit's render status is read.** The first failure ends the chain's render with its status and nothing of that unit mixed. The master bus silences the slice, and the output unit counts a dropout. A failed return holds stale scratch that would otherwise play as the current audio.

**Idle FX cost nothing, and dormant FX are sample-exact.** A stage renders only while it has work. A send works while its gate is open or its tail (`kAudioUnitProperty_TailTime`) still rings. The low kill works while on, sweeping, or settling for 250 ms after it parked flat. Otherwise a stage is reset and skipped. A connected chain with nothing engaged is then a pass-through the render suite compares exactly, and `unitRenders` (`debugRenderCounts`) does not advance. The audio thread decides no activity: it reads the stages' flags. The player's idle stop reads them too (`sendsActive`, `longestTailSeconds`), so the output is kept until a tail rests, and no longer than the longest declared tail. A rested stage joins the render before its gate opens, so it starts from silence under the gate's fade-in.

**The segment connects through `setConnected:format:maximumFrameCount:` with the output stopped, and is in the render only while connected.** The player publishes the chain's pointer into the master bus after a connect and withdraws it before a disconnect or a re-host (`reconcileFXOnQueue`). A disconnected segment costs the render nothing, and no parameter write reaches its units until the next connect. A disconnect with tails ringing rests every stage at once, so the output is the bus, sample for sample. The units are hosted at the first connect and kept across toggles. A connect at another rate hosts them again and re-applies the recorded intent, because an effect cannot convert between its input and output. On macOS that is a device switch across rates, since the pipeline runs at the device's rate.

**Nothing is reset or disposed under a render.** A hosting is one allocation the render is handed whole, so a re-host swaps it. The old hosting's disposal, a stage's rest after its tail and the disconnect's resets all wait for the render to be seen outside the chain (the player's `afterRenderLeaves`). A stage engaged again before its deferred rest runs is left as it is.

Both platforms keep the `AudioFX` object from init, so controls and the BPM feed stay stable. An iOS media-services reset withdraws the chain and calls `markDead`. Ramps are preempted, deferred rests skip the orphaned units, and retirement only disposes them. The next connect hosts fresh units, even at the same rate, and re-applies the recorded intent.

**Enabling and bypass.** On the mac, `setBitPerfectOutput:exclusiveOutput:enableFX:allowAnyDevice:` applies both settings through the playback-preserving device rebuild. Bit-perfect outranks the stored FX preference, and leaving it restores that preference. iOS applies the setting through `setFXEnabled:`, which stops the output, reconciles and puts a playing track back (`resumeOutputAfterEditOnQueue:reason:`). Both clear effect intent synchronously when a bypass is submitted (`clearFXIntent`: the mac's five toggles and the pad's levels alike). The queued bypass only preempts ramps and resets processing and tails, so it cannot erase a later UI action. The mac redraws the cleared flags once through `updateFXIndicators`. `connected` answers whether the segment is in the chain. **The reverb unit differs per platform**: MatrixReverb on the mac, AUReverb2 on iOS, each tuned toward the same long wash.

**Hosting the ten units is the chain's one real cost** (the `AudioFX: hosting` timing line: about 44 ms in the iOS simulator, 132 ms on a Mac Studio). It is paid when the bus format is first known, at launch rather than inside the first play. It is paid again when the output's rate changes or media services reset. A play submitted during launch waits behind it.

**`AudioFX`'s intent is one number per stage (`lowKillCutoffHz`, `reverbSendLevel`, `delaySendLevel`), and the on-off setters write it.** The mac's toggles resolve to the cutoff through `VibeLowKillCutoffHz` and open a send at its base level with the held swell. The iOS pad writes the numbers directly, which clears the toggles' memory and swells nothing. Telling a tap from a hold is `TransportKeyMonitor`'s job. **Clearing `lowKillEnabled` also clears `lowKillBoostActive`**, because the boost modifies that filter and a latched W must not hold the cutoff up while the low kill reads off. A level or cutoff written on every drag frame touches the queue only on a real change. A move of an open gate is written to its target, which the audio thread slews toward. A cutoff move within `kLowKillDirectMoveRatio` of where the filter sits is written to the unit directly. Anything larger (an open from silence, a close, a flick, the toggles) preempts the ramp in flight and continues from where the gate or filter sits. A stream of positions then lands as one motion without a timer per frame.

**The numbers are `AudioFXMath.h`, header-only and tested**: the three-way low-kill cutoff, the iOS pad's two axes, the ping-pong tap and lane times with their no-tempo fallback, the lane feedback and the swell target. The pad's y is the cutoff on a log curve up to `kFXPadLowCutMaxHz`. Its x is the reverb level, and past `kFXPadDelayOnset` the 1/8-note delay on top. `AudioFXChainTests` renders the chain alone over a virtual clock, and `VibeAudioTests` renders the whole player offline (`Tests/AGENTS.md`).

**Every toggle is click-free** (cutoff sweeps and gate fades on the player queue with generation preemption), **and off is colorless.** The low kill sweeps to the 20 Hz floor, then swaps its bands to a 0 dB parametric type, an identity biquad. A high-pass parked at the floor still lifts the sub-bass through its resonant peak. Settled, the unit is reset and skipped, and a reset flat EQ is exact from its first frame.

## The pitch fader's stage

**`AudioVarispeed` is the pitch fader's stage, and it is not part of the FX segment.** It is Vibe's own code, not an Apple unit. It runs before the segment, at the bus rate. It owns the kernel and its tables, the ring, the position, the bypass at zero, the engage and disengage, and the replay. The pipeline hosts one per source segment, pulls nothing itself, and frees what the stage replaced once no render is inside (`Audio/AGENTS.md`).

**At zero pitch it is a bit-perfect pass-through**: nothing converted, delayed or copied. The bypass is what makes zero exact, since the kernel is a low-pass at a ratio of 1 too (`testZeroPitchIsBitPerfectAndTogglesAreClickFree`). A stage builds no kernel until the pitch first leaves zero.

**The kernel** is a Kaiser-windowed sinc with 64 zero crossings each side, read from a polyphase table with a cubic across phases, summed in double. Its −6 dB point is 22 kHz at 48 kHz, the same fraction of any rate. Above a ratio of 1 it is stretched by the ratio, so its cutoff follows the output's Nyquist. `VibeVarispeedSetPitch` builds a new stretch's table on the queue and publishes it by pointer. The render ramps the ratio across each slice, so a drag glides. A slice that ramps the ratio down keeps the table it last used, which is stretched for where the ramp starts. The target's table would let the start of the ramp fold back (`testARampDownKeepsTheWiderKernel`). So a replaced table is freed only once the render no longer uses it (`VibeVarispeedRetire`).

**The edges.** Leaving zero, the stage first plays the widest kernel's half-width of frames directly and records them in its ring as the kernel's past. It then starts the converter at the next source frame, its ratio ramped from 1, and pulls the source into the ring a half-width ahead. Returning to zero replays the frames it had pulled ahead before the source continues. Those are the source's own samples, so from the first frame after the return the track is the file exactly, with no frame skipped or repeated (`testPitchEngagesAndDisengagesAtTheExactFrame`, at every rate from 44.1 to 192 kHz and in mono). At zero pitch settled the ring is not written (`varispeedHistoryWrites` in `debugRenderCounts` holds flat). The ring's size and the engage's history derive from the kernel's constants in `AudioVarispeed.m`, the one home of its geometry.

**Off zero, what plays is the position.** The bus leads the output by the kernel's half-width and one or two frames, and nothing else. `testPitchedTransportKeepsThePositionHeard` reads a sawtooth through a seek, a pause past the idle stop, a gapless splice and an output rate change. `testALongPitchedPlayKeepsItsPosition` reads every frame of a 6.5-minute play, past 2^24 frames, and its track end. `AudioVarispeedTests`, in the fast suite, drives the stage alone with random schedules of pitch and slice size, mono, and the pitch moved from another thread. Run it under the thread sanitizer when the stage's threading changes.

`testPitchQuality` holds the stage to −145 dB of distortion and false tones, flat to 20 kHz, at 48 and 96 kHz. The `pitch` benchmark group (the `vibe-perf` skill) holds its cost beside Apple's Varispeed (`pitch.apple…`). `docs/audio-quality.md` has both.
