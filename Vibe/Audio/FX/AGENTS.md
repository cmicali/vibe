# DJ FX (the master-bus segment) and the pitch converter

`AudioPlayer` owns one `AudioFX` (readonly `fx`): the Q low-kill high-pass with its W double-cutoff boost, and the send-returns, E a long reverb wash, R and T BPM-synced ping-pong delays on 1/8- and 1/16-note taps fed by the controller through `delayTapBPM`. The topology and threading are `AudioFX.h`'s preamble; the traps (MatrixReverb's stale header ranges, the frames-per-slice a directly hosted unit defaults to, the unit that writes back into a lent input buffer) are `TRAP:` comments in `AudioFX.m`.

**The segment is a stage of the render, not a graph.** `VibeFXChainRender` runs in place over the master bus's buffers, on the audio thread, under the same `CA_REALTIME_API` discipline as the voice bus: Apple's units — AUNBandEQ, MatrixReverb, AUDelay — are hosted through the AudioUnit C API and rendered with an input callback that copies the unit's scratch into the unit's own input buffer; every mixer is buffer arithmetic: a gate is a gain the queue targets and the audio thread slews (full scale per 25 ms), a pan is a balance law (the far side attenuates linearly), a sum is an add, and the two delay sends' identical low-cuts are one filter over their summed returns. **Every unit's render status is read**: the first failure ends the chain's render with its status and nothing of that unit mixed, the master bus silences the slice, and the status reaches the output unit, which counts a dropout — a return whose render failed holds stale scratch that would otherwise play as the current audio.

**Idle FX cost nothing, and dormant FX are sample-exact.** A stage renders only while it has work — a send while its gate is open or its tail (the units' own `kAudioUnitProperty_TailTime`) still rings, the low kill while on, sweeping or settling for 250 ms after it parked flat — and is otherwise reset, forgotten and skipped, so a connected chain with nothing engaged is a pass-through the render suite compares exactly, and `unitRenders` (`debugRenderCounts`) does not advance. Nothing on the audio thread decides activity; it reads the stages' flags. The player's idle stop reads them too (`sendsActive`, `longestTailSeconds`): a tail is a sound the units are still making, so the output is kept until it rests, and no longer than the longest declared tail. A rested stage joins the render before its gate opens, so it starts from silence under the gate's fade-in. A gate settled on its target is one multiply per channel and a closed one a clear; the sums and the returns' pans are vDSP operations, the pan landing straight in the sum.

**The segment connects through `setConnected:format:maximumFrameCount:` with the output stopped, and is in the render only while connected.** The player publishes the chain's pointer into the master bus after a connect and withdraws it before a disconnect or a re-host (`reconcileFXOnQueue`), so a disconnected segment costs the render nothing — not even its stages' flags — and no parameter write or ramp step reaches its units until the next connect; a disconnect with tails still ringing rests every stage at once and the output is the bus, sample for sample. The units are hosted at the first connect and kept across toggles; a connect at another rate — a device switch across rates, since on macOS the pipeline runs at the device's rate — hosts them again at the new format and re-applies the recorded intent, because an effect cannot convert between its input and output. A hosting is one allocation the render is handed whole, so a re-host swaps it; the old hosting's disposal, a stage's rest after its tail and the disconnect's resets all wait for the render to be seen outside the chain (the player's `afterRenderLeaves`), so no unit is reset or disposed under a render, and a stage engaged again before its deferred rest runs is left as it is. Both platforms keep the lightweight `AudioFX` object from init so controls and the BPM feed remain stable. An iOS media-services reset withdraws the chain and calls `markDead`: ramps are preempted, deferred rests skip the orphaned units, and retirement only disposes them. The next connect hosts fresh units even at the same rate and re-applies the recorded intent, including when FX was bypassed at reset. On the mac the player's one `setBitPerfectOutput:exclusiveOutput:enableFX:allowAnyDevice:` call applies both settings through the existing playback-preserving device rebuild; bit-perfect outranks the stored FX preference, and leaving it restores that preference. iOS applies the same setting through `setFXEnabled:`, which stops the output, reconciles and puts a playing track back (`resumeOutputAfterEditOnQueue:reason:`, the tail the route follow shares). Both clear effect intent synchronously when a bypass is submitted (`clearFXIntent`, the mac's five toggles and the pad's levels alike); the queued bypass only preempts ramps and resets processing and wet tails, so it cannot erase a later UI action. The mac shell redraws the cleared flags once through `updateFXIndicators`. `connected` answers whether the segment is in the chain. **The reverb unit differs per platform**: MatrixReverb on the mac, AUReverb2 on iOS, each tuned in its own constant block toward the same long wash; everything else in the chain is the same units.

**Hosting the ten units is the chain's one real cost, paid when the bus format is first known — at launch, not inside the first play — and again when the output's rate changes or media services reset.** Measured cold (the `AudioFX: hosting` timing line): 44 ms in the iOS simulator, 132 ms on a Mac Studio. A play submitted during launch waits behind it; a device reading is still owed.

**`AudioFX`'s intent is one number per stage — `lowKillCutoffHz`, `reverbSendLevel`, `delaySendLevel` — and the on-off setters are writers of it.** The mac's toggles resolve to the cutoff through `VibeLowKillCutoffHz` and open a send at its base level with the held swell; the iOS pad writes the numbers directly, which clears the toggles' memory and swells nothing. Telling a tap from a hold is `TransportKeyMonitor`'s job. One coupling is enforced here so every path gets it: **clearing `lowKillEnabled` also clears `lowKillBoostActive`**, because the boost modifies that filter and a latched W must not hold the cutoff up while the low kill reads off. A level or cutoff written on every drag frame touches the queue only on a real change. A move of an already-open gate is written to its target directly — the audio thread slews the gain toward it — and a cutoff move within `kLowKillDirectMoveRatio` of where the engaged filter sits is written to the unit directly; anything larger (an open from silence, a close, a flick, the toggles) preempts the ramp in flight and continues from where the gate or filter sits, so a stream of positions lands as one motion without a timer per frame.

**The numbers are `AudioFXMath.h`, header-only and tested**: the three-way low-kill cutoff, the iOS pad's two axes (y the cutoff on a log curve from parked to `kFXPadLowCutMaxHz`; x the reverb level, and past `kFXPadDelayOnset` the 1/8-note delay's on top), the ping-pong tap and lane times with their no-tempo fallback, the lane feedback and the swell target. The fast logic suite checks the math, `AudioFXChainTests` renders the chain on its own over a virtual clock (an idle chain exact, a held send echoing at its tap and resting after its tail, the low kill cutting and resting exactly, a rate change), and `VibeAudioTests` renders the whole player offline (`Tests/AGENTS.md`).

**Every toggle is click-free** (cutoff sweeps and gate fades on the player queue with generation preemption), and **off is colorless**: the low kill sweeps to the 20 Hz floor and then swaps its bands to a 0 dB parametric type, an identity biquad, because a high-pass parked at the floor still lifts the sub-bass through its resonant peak; settled, the unit is reset and skipped, and a reset flat EQ is exact from its first frame (measured).

## The pitch fader's converter

**`AudioVarispeed` is the pitch fader's converter, and it is not part of the FX segment.** It runs before the segment in the render, at the bus rate, only while the pitch is off zero. It is the arithmetic alone: the kernel, its polyphase tables and the conversion from a ring. The pipeline owns everything around it: the bypass at zero, the engage and disengage, the ring and the replay, and each table's lifetime (`Audio/AGENTS.md`, `AudioPlayer+Pipeline.m`). Unlike the FX units it is Vibe's own code, not an Apple unit, so nothing about it is hosted. `testPitchQuality` holds its quality, and the `pitch` benchmark group (the `vibe-perf` skill) its cost, beside Apple's Varispeed as the render hosted it (`pitch.apple…`). `docs/audio-quality.md` has both.
