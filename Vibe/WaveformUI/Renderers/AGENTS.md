# Waveform renderers

The strategies both views draw through, the morph engine they share and the level mapping. `AudioWaveformRenderer` is the base; `WaveformRendererRegistry` is the one home of identifier → renderer and of the fallback chain for a persisted style, so the two platforms cannot drift on which styles exist. The renderer and morph-engine headers carry C++ — `.mm` importers only (`../AGENTS.md`); `WaveformRendererRegistry.h` (plain ObjC) and `WaveformLevelMath.h` (plain C) are the two a `.m` may import.

**Compare styles by identifier, never by class.** Variants share classes — Wiggle is `DetailedAudioWaveformRenderer`, the oversampling trio share a file, Cupertino subclasses Basic — and the resolved registry identifier is what persists. Wiggle's is `wiggle_centered` whatever Centered says; the retired `wiggle` (Wiggle MC) is read only by the migrations that make it Wiggle with Centered off.

The registry's settings preview renders the actual style into a static bitmap with the caller's palette, density, width and levels. Its shared synthetic sample includes fine transients: a smooth envelope alone hides the Detailed family's sampling differences at thumbnail size. Preview rendering never changes the live renderer's geometry or sampling counts. Under a theme's `playheadColor` the bake behind it draws what the views draw, the waveform wholly played and the line where the progress is (`../AGENTS.md`).

## Families

Two families and one flat style, split by *how progress and hover quantize*:

- **Continuous** — Detailed, its oversampling variants, Wiggle and 3-Band: the played clip edge is the playhead, hover lights a thin slice (a whole loop for Wiggle, so it does not flicker into dots between strokes).
- **Block** — Basic, Cupertino, Sonic Cirrus: hover lights the whole block under the cursor and the played fill advances a block at a time. `VibeBlockIndexForX`/`VibeBlockBoundaryForProgress` (`AudioWaveformRenderer.h`) are the one home of both rules. **Presentation only: the seek a hover or click reports stays continuous.**
- **Cupertino Basic** — the Apple Music pill, the one style that never reads the samples. It has no morph engine, so settle and the convert dip are base no-ops and it never bakes; having a waveform at all decides only whether the pill shows.

**Wiggle draws its centerline directly** — a shape-layer stroke live, a source-in Core Graphics stroke in the bitmap, both from `VibeNewWigglePath`. Never expand it into a filled outline first: that repeats the stroker's work on every resize and morph frame.

## Level mapping

**Bar heights draw energy, not peaks.** `VibeWaveformBarLevel` (`WaveformLevelMath.h`, Foundation-free so `WaveformLevelMathTests` pins it) is the one chunk-to-level mapping: RMS against `kVibeWaveformFullScaleRMS`. Peaks peg on limited masters — at Basic's pitch every bar holds a full-scale kick and the strip reads as a block.

**Every bar's level is read through `AudioWaveform.getBarMeanSquares`, with a reach** (`Audio/Waveform/AGENTS.md` has the mechanism). Bars about a beat long — a six-minute track across a window — ripple without it as a resize moves their edges: 3-Band's outline moved 0.45 to 0.8pt per point of width on three dance tracks, and moves 0.14 to 0.43pt with it, most of what is left being hits moving with the stretch. **Not a wider, tapered window**, which holds stiller still (0.08 to 0.2pt) but averages every hit into its neighbours: zoomed in, the kicks it rounds off are the picture. **The reach fades out as the bars get short** (`VibeWaveformWindowReach`): bars fine enough to draw each hit need none, and it would fatten every hit by a bar. 3-Band has it whole up to 2,048 bars and none from 4,096, where a bar is two chunks; the other styles whole up to 512 and none at the floor's 1,024 columns below, which no resize moves. **A style that reads a level any other way brings the ripple back.**

**Every bar-level consumer but 3-Band reads its levels as energy columns, `energyColumnLevelsForBarCount:waveform:`**, never finer than 1/1024 of the track (`kVibeWaveformEnergyColumns`) however fine the bars: RMS over less than a beat converges back to peak and would peg the oversampling styles' one-chunk bars. Up to 1,024 bars a bar is its own column; past that, `VibeWaveformEnergyColumnIndexForBar` maps each bar to the column it falls in. Only the level is floored. The Detailed family keeps its min/max envelope as the *shape*, each bar's own, scaled against its column's peak widened by the bar's own, so that peak draws the column's level and no bar passes the envelope (the `TRAP:` in `fillEnvelope:` has why both peaks), through `fillEnvelope:` — the one sampling hook behind both the live target and the envelope bake; Cupertino overrides it to ±level, and Sonic Cirrus and Wiggle sample through `fillEnergyLevels:count:stride:waveform:`.

**Normalize and Gain are the renderer's `normalizesLevels` and `gainDB`, handed over by each view as the theme is.** Normalize only raises: `VibeWaveformFullScaleRMSForColumns` caps the track's loudest column at the fixed reference, read from the energy columns the fill just took, so it is the windows the bars draw, over the whole track, and costs no second pass over the waveform. 3-Band measures its own on the same rules and ceiling (`VibeWaveformNormalizedFullScaleRMS`, below). **It waits for the whole track** (`AudioWaveform.isComplete`, the loader's verdict carried on the data): a streaming load's loudest column is only the loudest so far, so a reference re-measured per delivery rose through the load and shrank bars already drawn. A partial waveform draws at the fixed reference and the completing delivery raises it. The raise is one vertical scale while Gain is 0, which `normalizationGainForWaveform:` names (3-Band from its band reference), so the iOS scrubber grows its bitmap into it rather than jumping. A streaming load is partial for the length of its download, and what has not arrived is unfilled chunks drawn as silence's hairline; drawing that region apart (a downloaded fraction carried on the data, its own alpha past it, in every family and the envelope bake) is not built. Gain is a display gain ahead of the clamp plus a proportional curve bend (`kVibeWaveformGainDBPerExponentDoubling`). **A level change is a target change under the same identity**, which the morph's fast path would skip, so both setters call `invalidateTarget` and the bars ease to their new heights. Cupertino Basic ignores both.

## Centered

**`centered`, on by default, draws about the midline; off grounds every bar on the band's foot (`VibeBarBaseline`) at the height it had about the midline**, so the level mapping and the seek band stay as they are. **The ramp reaches below the band, so a foot on the baseline is as bright as the midline it left** (`VibeBarRampDrop`: every ramp's axis, the band's and Basic's full-view one, drops by half the band): aimed as when centered, every quiet grounded bar sat in its dimmest stretch. Each style re-aims on a toggle, live and baked alike. Three geometry homes read it — `fillBarRects` (Detailed, its oversampling variants, Basic and Cupertino), `VibeAddThreeBandPaths` and `VibeNewWigglePath`, whose grounded loops are what Wiggle MC was. Cupertino's fade mirrors its bars about the midline, so grounded it takes Basic's ramp. Sonic Cirrus and Cupertino Basic ignore it (`supportsCenteringForIdentifier:`). The views hand it over as they do density and width: the mac from its theme's `waveformCentered`, iOS from `AppSettings.waveformCentered` for the card and `widgetWaveformCentered` for the widget, which the scrubber's and widget's signatures carry so a toggle re-bakes. Each switch dims for a style that ignores it, so the raw value is the drawn one.

## Bar count and thickness

**The count follows the drawn width at the style's designed pitch**, so a resize adds bars rather than stretching them. Basic, Cupertino and Sonic Cirrus share `blockBarCountForWidth:` (4pt, capped at 1,024); Detailed and Wiggle use `numBarsForWidth:`. `barDensity` multiplies the block and Wiggle counts, defaulting to 1; Detailed and its fixed-count oversampling variants ignore it, as does the flat Cupertino Basic pill. The registry gates density and width separately; width also scales Cupertino Basic's resting and hovered pill heights, with its seek band covering the enlarged pill. `barWidthScale` scales the supported styles' bar thickness (Wiggle's stroke) without changing the count; discrete bars share `scaledBarWidth:pitch:` to stop at their slot edges. Wiggle's live stroke, hover and bitmap bake share the scaled geometry. Wiggle's count reads `samplingWidth` when set, so an iOS pinch stretches its loops instead of resampling them (`../iOS/AGENTS.md`).

## The morph engine

`WaveformMorphEngine` owns the displayed and target vectors, the 60 Hz ease and the retarget decision tree; each family installs a geometry callback and a fill. **Do not duplicate it per family** — near-identical copies diverge. Its header states the decision tree; the rules a caller is checked against:

- **`identity` is compare-only and never dereferenced.** It cannot false-match because the view retains the old waveform while the new one is allocated.
- **A geometry change rebuilds instantly; a content change eases.** A settled waveform's count change installs the new samples in one rebuild, and a resize mid-morph resamples the displayed bars and keeps easing, so loading, gain and the convert sweep survive a resize.
- **A nil↔non-nil identity flip always rebuilds**: a silent track's all-zero waveform is sample-identical to the collapsed target but draws hairlines. `barMinHeight` — 1 with a waveform, 0 without — is the engine's policy so both families agree.
- **The Detailed family pixel-rounds only when settled**; mid-morph rounding quantizes the motion into visible steps.
- **`settleImmediately` exists because every morph frame is a full-view repaint** — for Detailed a 4,096-bar mask path — and a couple of pager cells easing at once blow a scroll's frame budget. The base forwards it as `settleMorphImmediately`.

## The convert sweep

**Convert to FLAC's progress is drawn with the waveform itself; there is no separate progress element.** `dipBarsFromFraction:toFraction:` → `dipDisplayedSamplesFromFraction:toFraction:` zeroes the displayed samples in the newly crossed span, rebuilds once so the notch is seen at zero, and lets the standard ease carry them back to the unchanged target, so the graded trail behind the front falls out for free. The span rounds outward to `samplesPerBar` so an edge bar is never half-zeroed; a fraction maps to the array linearly in both families because samples run left to right. Keeping the front and feeding only the new span is `../Mac/AGENTS.md`'s; reporting the fractions is `Audio/Mac/Convert/AGENTS.md`'s.

## The envelope bake

The envelope-image API (`AudioWaveformRenderer.h`, beside `supportsEnvelopeBake`) exists for the iOS scrubber's settled fast path (`../iOS/AGENTS.md`); the mac view never calls it, and Detailed and 3-Band implement it. **The bitmap must stay pixel-identical to the live layers**: both go through `fillBarRects` for geometry and pixel rounding (bitmap workers on their own scratch, off the live mask's state), `VibeNewBarMaskPath` for the bars' mask — one outline where the bars abut, which fills byte for byte as their rects do once pixel-rounded, in fewer path elements — `VibeNewWigglePath` for lines and `gradientColorsForColor:` for stops, and the band both aim the gradient at is `VibeAimBarGradient`/`VibeFillBarGradient`'s (`AudioWaveformRenderer.h`). 3-Band's go through `VibeAddThreeBandPaths`, and its bake applies the played side's mask stops over the same band. **A subclass that changes its gradient aim or fill quantization must answer `supportsEnvelopeBake` for itself** — Basic overrides it back to NO and Cupertino inherits that. A NULL unplayed bake means the unplayed side is the played bitmap at `unplayedOverPlayedOpacity`. Detailed answers NULL while `unplayedSharesPlayedHue`, valid because both sides share the ramp; a two-hue theme bakes the unplayed image separately, doubling that cell's bitmap bytes — a trade `WaveformZoomMath.h`'s budget does not model. 3-Band always answers NULL.

## 3-Band

A DJ player's 3-band display: the low, mid and high bands' levels. **Its own class because its fill is seven colored layers**, where every other bar style fills one theme gradient through one mask; the morph engine carries three samples a bar. **Its bands exist only in a waveform decoded for a request that asked for them** (`readsBandsForIdentifier:`, `Audio/Waveform/AGENTS.md`); one without them draws the silent hairline until the band decode the shell asks for lands.

**Each height shows the set of bands reaching it.** Each layer is one polygon through the bars' centers, mirrored about the midline or standing on the baseline, out to the lowest band of its set, and the layers paint in order — the three bands alone, the three pairs, all three — so at every height the layer on top is the set of bands reaching it: the low alone out to a leading low, low+mid where the mid joins, the all-bands core out to the quietest. The envelopes nest as their sets do, since a set's lowest band is never above a subset's, so the painter's order alone colors them and the fill's antialiasing smooths the steps between bars; every set's envelope is built, the later ones covering the rest. Not pixel-snapped columns, which read as stair steps wherever a bar spans pixels. `VibeAddThreeBandPaths` is the one home of the geometry, which the live layers and the bake both draw, so the two stay pixel-identical, plus, only while the hover slice shows, the outline it is masked to. It shares Detailed's band, seek band and hover slice (`VibeBarVScale`, `AudioWaveformRenderer.h`).

**The energy windows follow the drawn width, not the 1/1024 floor**: a bar a point, up to the waveform's 8,192 chunks (`kVibeWaveformMaxBars`), each bar's levels its own window's, with the reach that holds them still through a resize (Level mapping, above). At a window's width a point is about the floor's column; zoomed in, the scrubber's bars get short enough to draw each kick. Floored at 1/1024, a zoomed bar spans most of a beat, and kick and gap alias against the beat into a slow swell — a 6:02 track's 0.35 s columns against 134 BPM's 0.45 s beats. The point is the other bound: sub-point bars, each a fraction of a beat, read as kick and gap flickering blue and amber from bar to bar. One band per bar against the broadband reference scaled by a per-band share (`kBandShareOfFullScale`). The shares keep dance masters' measured balance, where the mids sit about 12 dB under the lows and the highs about 17 dB — one reference drew hi-hats as slivers, and equal ones swamped the strip in the all-bands core — and put the outer edge where Detailed draws: over 55 masters, a median of 0.78 against Detailed's 0.81, at full height in 19% of columns against its 22%. **Normalize measures the loudest band against its share**, not the full mix, so the tallest band at the track's loudest column reaches full height; against the full mix no band could. **It is its own reference for speed**: within the 1,024 columns it reads the mean squares the fill just took, where measuring them through the waveform again cost a fifth of every resize frame's instructions; past them it measures 1,024 columns the same way, so the reference does not step as a resize crosses 1,024 bars. The iOS scrubber always normalizes, so there the shares set only the balance. Gain moves all three alike.

**The fills are the theme's `bandColors`, in this painter's order; the theme also gives the gradient and the hover slice.** The gradient is Detailed's ramp over Detailed's band — full at the top, its bottom level at the bottom — which `flatFill` drops. Not a fade mirrored about the midline: the outer ring is most of what 3-Band shows, so dimming the band's edges dims the whole strip. The palettes and their light forms are `WaveformTheme`'s (`../AGENTS.md`); the view's re-resolved theme brings the light one on an appearance flip like every style's. **The seven layers are drawn once, under one mask of two sides** split at the playhead: the played side at full level, the unplayed at half, each carrying the ramp. A dimmed second stack would hand every rebuild's paths to fourteen layers, each of which copies the path it is handed, and the mask dims the composited stack, so the outer rings never show through the inner ones.
