# Main window: layout and chrome

Read this before changing layout numbers, the glass chrome, the art-tint pipeline or the codec line. Behavior is `AGENTS.md`.

## Layout

The layout is programmatic: no nibs, absolute frames and autoresizing masks. **The body layout lives in `MainPlayerContentViewInternal.h`.** The view and its `+Construction` category both read it. `MainWindowLayout.h` holds shared window sizes and the artwork exclusion band. The volume control owns its columns. The slider's width and its gap are at the top of `VolumeControlView.m`. Frames are authored at the design size, `kMainWindowContentWidth` × `kMainWindowDesignHeight`. Each subview's mask says how it stretches to the user's window size.

**The artist line clears the codec line's text, not its frame.** The codec text is right-aligned in a column sized for its worst case: a long codec string behind three FX symbols. Reserving that whole column would cost the artist line a third of its width at design size. `layoutArtistLineClearOfCodecLine` re-caps the artist line against what actually renders. It runs from both moving inputs: geometry through `resizeSubviewsWithOldSize:` (live drags included, reusing the last measured width) and text through `TrackDisplayController`'s compose. The layout block's static frame is the worst-case fallback. The title line only has to clear the shorter BPM line, which its shrink-to-fit width already does.

**The header glass and tint run from the artwork's right edge to the window's right edge.** They are rectangular, and the window shapes the outer corners. Neither extends behind the artwork, whose transparent pixels must show only the window backdrop.

Every outer corner uses the theme's `resolvedWindowCornerRadius`: the contentView layer, the window backdrop, the background cover and the pitch panel's right-edge path. It is the slider's value while the theme's Corner radius is Custom, else the standard 16pt (`kVibeThemeCornerRadiusDefault`). No consumer reads the raw `windowCornerRadius`. `applyWindowChrome` re-applies every consumer live.

## The transport buttons

**The transport row is themed in one pass**, `MainPlayerContentView.applyThemedTransportButtons`, which is the `TransportButtons` live effect's whole body. Each of the playlist toggle, play and next buttons draws the theme's custom image when its Dark/Light pair names one. An unset side takes the other side's image. Otherwise it draws its glyph, a free-text SF Symbol name with the factory glyph as the fallback (`ResolvedGlyph`). The glyph takes the theme's resting color, and `SymbolButton` derives hover and disabled from it (`setSymbolColorsFromRestingColor:`).

**The buttons' Dark/Light pair is keyed by what is under them, never by the appearance.** With the gradient on, the backdrop is dark whatever the cover. With it off, `ArtworkDisplayController` samples the lower band of every image it installs through `VibeImageLowerBandIsDark` (`Common/PlatformImage.h`). It pushes the answer into `setTransportBackdropDark:hasArtwork:`. A track's crop is sampled on the render worker. The placeholder is sampled on main, and again on every appearance flip (`refreshTintWashes`), because its pixels follow the appearance. Unset color slots are white and black at the resting strength, so a bright cover gets dark glyphs by default.

**The play button's state goes through the content view** (`setPlayButtonShowsPause:`, with `updateUI` its one caller). The pause state has its own theme slots: `pauseButtonGlyph` and the `pauseButtonImageDark`/`Light` pair. Each image slot falls back to its glyph on its own, so a theme with only a play image still shows a pause glyph while playing.

**The darkening gradient over the art's lower edge is the theme's `buttonGradient`.** None leaves the art bare. Show on hover fades it with the buttons in `setControlsShown:animated:`. Show on artwork shows it only over an installed track cover. Always shows it over the default image too. The artwork controller publishes cover presence with the backdrop brightness, from the same accepted image install. Pending art keeps the previous answer. Gradient visibility and button contrast share `transportGradientEnabled`, so hiding the gradient over a placeholder also restores the placeholder's own button palette. `showTransportButtons` off hides the three buttons and their gradient through the hover funnel. Hidden buttons leave hit testing and accessibility. Keyboard and menu actions keep working.

## The window controls and the app icon

**The app icon and the Dock tile are one effect**, `AppIcon`. Its body is `MainPlayerController.applyAppIcon`, which also runs at launch. The theme's `appIcon` image is composed onto the icon grid like the artwork tile, so a square picture reads as an icon in the Dock. It goes into `NSApp.applicationIconImage`. nil restores the bundle's icon. Then `ArtworkDisplayController.applyDockIcon` re-decides the tile from `AppSettings.dockIcon`. The tile shows the art crop while a track's art is up and the setting says Album art, else the app icon. The tile's reset reads the application's icon live (`NSDockTile+Util`), so a custom icon shows through it.

The red close and yellow minimize lights are custom `SymbolButton`s over the artwork, not AppKit's standard window buttons. The window is borderless and has no zoom button. They share the header controls' whole-window hover fade. `AppSettings.showTrafficLights` is read in one place, `MainPlayerController.applyTrafficLights`. It reaches `MainPlayerContentView.setTrafficLightsShown:`, which re-runs the fade funnel from the current pointer position. **`setControlsShown:animated:` is the one place button visibility is decided.** It hides a switched-off pair outright, so neither hit testing nor accessibility can reach an invisible control.

## The volume control

"Vol", the slider and its percentage are one view, `VolumeControlView`. It sizes its columns, renders the percentage and resolves the slider's colors. `MainPlayerContentView` places it, shadows its labels and decides its alpha and hidden flag in `setControlsShown:animated:`. It uses the corner readouts' info face and kerning and the artist's color. It is hidden while `AppSettings.volumeControl` is off. The slider is a `VibeSlider`, never an `NSSlider` (`Mac/Controls/AGENTS.md`). The theme dresses it through `applyVolumeControl`, the `Volume` effect, which `ThemeApply` includes.

- **Place.** `volumeLocation` puts it in the top-right corner (the default) or centered in the time row's gap. In the corner its bottom sits on the title's cap height and its right end on the total time's, with the percentage right-aligned like the time. `showVolumeLabels` off drops the two labels and shrinks the view to the bare slider. **The label columns are measured by the labels' own cells** (`LabelCellWidth` in `VolumeControlView.m`). The readouts' negative kern makes a bare string's size too short, and a centered cell would drop "100%"'s last glyph.
- **When it shows.** The theme's `volumeVisibility` is always, when the pointer is close, or when the pointer is on it. The window hover never shows it, since that hover is for the transport. The close area is the slider's frame plus `kVolumeHoverOutsetX` and `kVolumeHoverOutsetY`, clipped to the player view. Unclipped, the area would reach past the window's edge, where leaving the window would not hide the slider, and over the pitch panel. `placeVolumeHoverArea` moves the tracking area whenever the slider moves. Crossing the area only re-runs the funnel, which tests the pointer against the same rect. So a slider appearing under a still pointer shows correctly. There is no area when the slider is off or always shown.
- **It swaps with what shares its place**, in the same hover fade. In the corner (`VolumeControlView.atTopRight`) the codec and BPM lines fade out as it fades in. When it always shows there, they stay hidden. The artist line caps against whichever of the codec text and the control reaches further left, so it never moves during the swap. In the time row the drop hint swaps instead, through `setDropHintShown:`, so the funnel decides both. The control is built after the corner readouts, because at zero alpha they still take clicks.
- **TRAP: a drag holds the hover open** (`volumeSliderDidMove`, from the slider action's event type). The pointer can leave the hover area mid-drag, and fading the control would leave the knob in hand invisible while it still sets the volume. Only the slider's action at the mouse-up clears the hold, and the release re-decides the hover from the pointer. `dump_state.ui.volumeSlider.dragging` reports it.
- **Colors.** The fill is `volumeBar`, resolved in `VolumeControlView`'s `applyColorsDark:artworkColor:`. The content view's `applyVolumeColors` passes it the appearance and the waveform's art color. Every resolution of the waveform's theme calls `applyVolumeColors` (`waveformViewDidResolveTheme:`), so a theme change, a waveform edit, an art color and an appearance flip all reach it. None is the system accent. Waveform is the waveform's played color at full alpha. Artwork is `WaveformTheme`'s `legibleArtworkColor:` clamp of the art color, falling back to Mono's played color. Custom is the pair as picked. The knob is `volumeKnob`, on the same ladder. Its default, Same as bar, is the color the fill draws. None is the system's white knob. Like `NSSlider`'s, the fill draws gray while the window is not key.

## Chrome: three layers

The chrome is macOS 26 `NSGlassEffectView`, in three layers. **Before macOS 26 the two glass layers fall back to frosted behind-window `NSVisualEffectView`s** (`UnderWindowBackground`, `StateActive`). Their blur is shaped by `frostCornerMaskWithRadius:`, because an `NSVisualEffectView`'s blur region ignores a layer `cornerRadius`. Both creation sites branch on `@available(macOS 26.0, *)`.

**1. The backdrop.** A window-spanning glass backdrop in the **Clear** style sits behind everything (`buildContentInWindow:`). Directly over it sits the theme's **background cover** (`windowBackgroundOverlayView`). It is a plain layer colored per appearance, with its alpha as the opacity. It shows under the **frosted** and **solid** styles (`VibeWindowBackgroundTakesColor`) and hides under glass and clear. `applyWindowBackground` re-colors it from the appearance-change funnel, because its layer color is a CGColor and not dynamic. Full alpha reads as a solid window, less as tinted glass.

**2. The header panel and its tint.** The header and waveform area gets a **Regular**-style glass panel in `MainPlayerContentView`. Under the theme's **clear** or **solid** background there is no panel (`applyWindowBackgroundStyle`, run from `applyWindowBackground`). Under clear, the Clear backdrop is the whole look. The panel is hidden rather than restyled Clear, because a second Clear pane over the backdrop compounds into a lighter band. Under solid, the cover is the whole look. **Frosted is solid's cover with the panel kept over it.** The tint wash stays where the panel was.

Over the panel, `headerTintView` is a passthrough layer view washed with the track's dominant art color (`VibeDominantColorOfImage`, `Common/PlatformImage.h`). **What the header does with the art it has is `ArtworkDisplayRules.h`**: a pure function of four facts, tested without a window, a Dock tile or a decode. `ArtworkDisplayController` owns the art view, the tint, the Dock icon and the deferred art loads, and schedules the work. It square-crops and samples one private image copy together on a serial utility lane, with at most one render running plus one replaceable latest request. A main-thread delivery checked against the generation and the exact track, metadata and source installs the matching image and color together. Changing any target invalidates pending renders, but the installed art stays up through an unresolved transition. Its injected renderer and publication sink let `AudioTrackTests` hold and settle real requests.

**The wash's clamps are perceptual, not HSB** (`NSColor+OKLCH`). HSB brightness is blind to hue, and an HSB cap let bright-hued art wash out the unplayed waveform. Lightness is capped at OKLCH L ≤ 0.30 in dark mode (over a light waveform) and held at L ≥ 0.87 in light mode (over a dark one). Chroma is capped moderately in both. **An out-of-gamut result gives up chroma, never lightness or hue.**

Tint changes fade over `kVibeArtCrossfadeDuration`, which `CrossfadingImageView`'s art crossfade shares, so both move on one clock.

**The theme's two tint fields choose the washes.** `windowTint` is the header's, with stable identifiers `mono`, `artwork` (the default) and `custom`, shown as None, Artwork color and Custom. `playlistTint` is the playlist's, on the same ladder with `mono` the default. Each has its own custom color pair. `resolvedWashForTint:customColor:isDark:` is the one place that resolves either. Mono is no wash. Artwork is the clamped art color. Custom is the stored color for the appearance, **used exactly as picked, alpha and all**, since the clamps exist to tame a color nobody chose. An unset custom color resolves as mono, so the editor seeds one when Custom is chosen. Only the washes follow the setting. The art color still settles, so the Dock icon and the `album_art` waveform theme look the same under every choice. A writer requests `VibeSettingsLiveEffectWindowTint` to fade both washes across (`refreshTintWashes`).

**The wash is deliberately not the glass's own `tintColor`.** AppKit discards a glass view's tint whenever the window is not key. The tint wash and playlist frost (`NSVisualEffectStateActive`) keep their own appearance. The glass views retain the system's inactive dimming.

**3. The playlist frost.** A behind-window `NSVisualEffectView` uses `UnderWindowBackground` in both appearances. The light `WindowBackground` material is effectively opaque paint. Two layers sit over it, both under the scroll view, because an `NSClipView` background does not composite translucent colors over a backdrop. So they also cover the empty area below the last row.

- The **background layer** (`applyPlaylistBackground`) mirrors the window's own background. Under glass it is the unthemed lift: clear in dark, a brightening white wash in light, because row text is not readable over Clear glass. Under **solid** it hides the frost and carries the theme's playlist background color. Under **clear** the frost and the lift both go. `applyWindowBackgroundStyle` re-applies it, since a window-style edit moves it.
- **A solid window hides the frost whatever the playlist's style.** The frost blurs what is behind the window, not the cover, so it would punch the desktop through a solid window. A frosted window keeps it, as it keeps the header's panel.
- `playlistTintView` is the playlist's `headerTintView`. `ArtworkDisplayController` resolves and fades the theme's `playlistTint` wash with the header's rules.

**TRAP: an `NSGlassEffectView` inside `MainPlayerContentView` must not stretch from design height to window height.** Its SwiftUI hosting fights the autoresizing stretch, and the window silently refuses to grow past the design height. Hence the frost is an `NSVisualEffectView`. The trap is that stretch, not height-flexibility itself: the backdrop is created full-bleed at live bounds, `NSViewHeightSizable`, and resizes cleanly. Width-flexibility is fine for both glass views.

**TRAP: `refreshTintWashes` must read light or dark from the *window*, not from `headerTintView`.** Its caller is the content view's `viewDidChangeEffectiveAppearance`. AppKit updates the tree top-down, so a subview there still reports the outgoing appearance, and the wash would lag one flip behind. `applyWindowBackground` reads the window for the same reason.

## The codec line and FX indicators

The codec line is also the status indicator. SF Symbols draw inline at the head of the same right-aligned run, glued to the codec text. The order is the latched effects, then shuffle and repeat, then the bit-perfect lock, always last against the text.

- Low kill shows the filled dial while its boost is on, since the boost modifies that filter. Reverb has one symbol. Each active delay shows an echo (`wave.3.right`, its `.circle` for the 1/16), matching the FX menu, and never a loop, which is repeat's. Shuffle and repeat (`repeat` or `repeat.1`) show only while on.
- **Bit-perfect output** shows a closed lock (`lock.fill`) while the current track is delivered bit-perfect. It shows an open lock (`lock.open`) while the mode is on but the track is not delivered bit-perfect: an unsupported rate, a lossy source, a scaled volume, the volume slider below full. It shows nothing while the mode is off. The open lock's reason is the codec label's tooltip (`renderBitPerfectToolTip:`), the same sentence Settings > Audio Output shows. `VibeFXDisplayState.bitPerfect` carries the three states. `updateFXIndicators` fills it from `AudioPlayer.bitPerfectReport` on the report's own change edge, `audioPlayerDidChangeBitPerfectReport:`. Reading it right after a setter would read the previous report.

The line has **two independent inputs**: codec text and FX state. `TrackDisplayController` composes it from the last of each, because FX are deck state that outlives any track. They persist across track changes and into the empty state.

`AppSettings.showFileInfo` off empties the codec text and the BPM/key line at render time (`renderState:`, `renderBPM:`). **The FX symbols are deck state, not file info, and keep composing.** `showStatusIcons` is the separate switch that hides the FX symbols and the lock, its tooltip included, without changing any audio state.

`AppSettings.showBPM` and `.showKey` each blank their own half of the BPM line upstream of the render. `effectiveTempoDidChange` passes 0 for a hidden tempo and -1 for a hidden key, so the separator goes with either half. Neither touches detection, tags or the delay's BPM-synced taps. The FX write is unconditional, since a hidden readout must not change the audio.

Two adjustments are optical. Symbols draw at **bold** weight, because the default stroke is a hairline at this size. The two dial glyphs get their own size multiplier, because they spend much of their box on tick marks.

Symbols render a step brighter than the codec text, at full `secondaryLabelColor`, matching the time labels. **So both corner labels carry their dimming in the text color**, not a field-wide alpha (the theme's `resolvedInfoColor` in `cornerTextAttributes`). A field alpha would dim the symbols too. The symbols are template images, so their tint follows the label through appearance changes.

## The key label

The BPM line carries the key after a `|` separator, the one the codec line uses between its fields. With `AppSettings.keyColorsEnabled` on, the key alone is drawn bold in its Camelot color. There is one hue per wheel number, with 1 green as on the printed wheel. So harmonically compatible keys sit in neighboring hues, and a relative major and minor share one. The palette is a `dynamicProvider` color per number (`camelotColor` in `TrackDisplayController.m`). It is less saturated on dark chrome and darker on light, since one fixed hue cannot read on both.

**TRAP: the BPM line's change guard cannot be the string alone.** Toggling the color setting leaves the text identical while the attributes must change. So `_lastKeyColorKey`, which starts at -1, is compared alongside the string.
