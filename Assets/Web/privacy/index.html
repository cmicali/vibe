Privacy Policy

Vibe collects nothing. It has no account of its own, no analytics, and no servers. Your music, and everything Vibe works out about it, stays on your device. The one exception is Dropbox on iPhone and iPad: if you choose to connect it, Vibe talks to Dropbox directly, and to nothing else.

Last updated 4 October 2026

What Vibe collects

Nothing. Vibe does not collect, sell, or share personal information, and nothing it handles is ever sent to me. There is no Vibe account, no user profile, no usage tracking, no crash reporting, and no advertising.

What stays on your device

To do its job, Vibe reads and stores things locally, inside its own sandboxed container:

  • The audio files you open, and the folders you grant it access to.
  • Tags, artwork, tempo, and waveform data read from those files, cached so the next open is fast.
  • Your settings and playback state, plus three local counters — files opened, folders opened, and total time played — shown in the About window.
  • On iPhone and iPad, if you connect Dropbox: copies of the Dropbox files you open, limited to the size you set in Settings, and the sign-in token Dropbox issues, kept in the device’s Keychain.

None of it leaves your Mac or iPhone except as described under Dropbox below.

Network access

On the Mac, Vibe has none. It runs in Apple’s App Sandbox without the network client entitlement, so the operating system will not let it open an outbound connection even if it tried. On iPhone and iPad, Vibe goes online only to reach Dropbox, and only after you connect a Dropbox account. Otherwise, the only links that leave the app are ones you click or tap yourself — the GitHub links in the About window — which open in your browser.

Dropbox on iPhone and iPad

Connecting Dropbox is optional. You sign in on Dropbox’s own page, so Vibe never sees your password. Dropbox grants Vibe read-only access: it can list, search, and download your files, and read your account’s name to show which account is connected. It cannot change, delete, or share anything in your Dropbox.

Vibe talks to Dropbox directly from your device, over an encrypted connection; nothing passes through any server of mine. What Vibe sends Dropbox is only what it needs to show you your files: the folders you open, the searches you type, and the files you play. Dropbox’s privacy policy governs what Dropbox does with that.

Signing out of Dropbox in Vibe ends Vibe’s access on Dropbox’s side, and deletes the sign-in token and every file Vibe downloaded.

Files kept in cloud storage

If you play a file from iCloud Drive, or from Dropbox or another provider through the Files app or the Finder, macOS or iOS downloads it through that provider’s own system extension, under the account you have already signed into. In that case Vibe asks the operating system for the file; it never contacts the provider itself and never sees your credentials. That provider’s privacy policy governs the transfer.

Now Playing

While a track is playing, Vibe hands its title, artist, and artwork to the operating system’s Now Playing service, so the track can appear in Control Center, on the Lock Screen, and on connected accessories. That is a system feature, and Vibe is not the one distributing the information.

The App Store

If you installed Vibe from the App Store, Apple handled the download and any purchase. Apple gives me aggregate, anonymous sales figures, and crash reports from users who chose to share diagnostics with developers. None of that identifies you to me. Apple’s privacy policy governs it.

Children

Vibe collects no information from anyone, children included.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version is posted on this page and the date above changes with it. Vibe’s source code is public, so any change to what the app actually does is visible in the commit history.

Contact
