## Findings

### 1. [P2] A failed manual device selection can overwrite a nested successful re-adoption

[AudioPlayer+Devices.m:477](/Users/cmicali/projects/vibe/Vibe/Audio/Mac/Devices/AudioPlayer+Devices.m:477); entry from [AudioPlayer.m:1188](/Users/cmicali/projects/vibe/Vibe/Audio/AudioPlayer.m:1188).

With saved device A pending while playback is audible, manually select B and make B's output-unit bind fail. The failed rebuild publishes Stopped and synchronously resolves A. A now binds successfully and applies its remembered bit-perfect/exclusive modes. When B's outer selection unwinds, its rollback overwrites those modes with the values from before either selection and releases the newly adopted device's preparation. The requested device remains A and its pending identity has already been cleared.

The real player probe observed binds `[B, A]`, requested A, and both applied modes false despite A's saved modes being true. Holding the lookup guard around the resolver's own binds fixes recursion but does not exclude resolution from an unrelated selection's reset. Prevent opportunistic re-adoption until the outer device mutation has settled, or otherwise make its rollback unable to undo a newer bind.

### 2. [P2] A failed re-adoption at natural track end suppresses the finish callback

[AudioPlayer.m:1232](/Users/cmicali/projects/vibe/Vibe/Audio/AudioPlayer.m:1232).

`finishPlaybackOnQueue` resolves the pending device before capturing the completed play's submission identifier. A refused bind calls `resetToStoppedStateOnQueue`, which clears that identifier. The finish path then captures zero and drops `didFinishPlaying:` as stale. A normally completed track does not advance to its successor.

The probe rendered a complete two-second WAV with an available pending device whose HAL bind was refused. Expected one finish callback; received zero, with a device error instead. Preserve the track-end settlement before optional device recovery; recovery failure must not erase a completed play's identity.

### 3. [P2] Losing the last output can turn an unplug into a natural track end

[AudioPlayer+Devices.m:890](/Users/cmicali/projects/vibe/Vibe/Audio/Mac/Devices/AudioPlayer+Devices.m:890), followed by [the missing-output park at line 707](/Users/cmicali/projects/vibe/Vibe/Audio/Mac/Devices/AudioPlayer+Devices.m:707).

`abandonBitPerfectForVanishedDeviceOnQueue` stops the engine without retiring its scheduled segment. If the default-output read succeeds with no device, the fallback parks the player Paused and performs no rebuild, so nothing invalidates the stop-fired segment completion. That completion calls `finishPlaybackOnQueue`, discards the resumable track, and sends the natural-end callback that the shell uses for auto-advance. The same callback-retirement requirement already exists in paused idle shutdown and the new termination fix.

The probe played 4,096 frames, removed the selected bit-perfect device, and supplied a confirmed empty default. It received pause, error, then **finish**, rather than remaining parked. Retire the old scheduled segment before stopping and preserve/reschedule the paused continuation without retaining a stale gapless splice.

### 4. [P2] A queued FX settings edit can undo carried modes before they are persisted

[AudioPlayer+Devices.m:1486](/Users/cmicali/projects/vibe/Vibe/Audio/Mac/Devices/AudioPlayer+Devices.m:1486); deferred persistence at [MainPlayerController+PlayerEvents.m:354](/Users/cmicali/projects/vibe/Vibe/Mac/MainWindow/MainPlayerController+PlayerEvents.m:354).

Re-adopting the same model on a new USB port immediately applies the old UID's modes on the player queue, but saves them under the new UID only in the asynchronous main-thread device announcement. A global FX settings edit already queued behind adoption re-reads the new UID before that callback runs. It sees an empty record and disables both modes. The later callback persists enabled modes, leaving Settings and the live player inconsistent; that callback intentionally does not reapply modes.

The probe held main until the queued settings edit completed, then delivered the normal carry to the injected mode store. The destination record ended enabled while both live modes were false. Keep the carried mode source valid through this publication window, or make persistence and application one ordered operation without introducing a main/player-queue synchronous cycle.

### 5. [P3] The new pre-play clock sample emits avoidable AVFAudio assertions

[AudioPlayer+Engine.m:66](/Users/cmicali/projects/vibe/Vibe/Audio/AudioPlayer+Engine.m:66).

The new baseline probe tests only whether `lastRenderTime` is non-nil before calling `playerTimeForNodeTime:`. A non-nil timestamp can have neither validity flag set, especially before initial render or after an engine stop. AVFAudio logs an assertion and throws; the catch prevents a crash but does not suppress the framework's error. The ordinary position getter already checks both validity flags for exactly this reason.

The restored 43-test audio suite passed but emitted **259** instances of the invalid-node-time assertion. Guard with `sampleTimeValid || hostTimeValid` before conversion, and skip this hardware timing probe entirely for manual rendering. This is a diagnostic regression, not evidence of corrupted samples or an observed production crash.

## Additional instrumentation worth adding

1. **Time the exclusive-output and HAL phases at entry and exit, using the existing play/segment identity.** The timer in `startEngineAndPlayNode:` starts *after* `acquireExclusiveOutputOnQueue`. Its engine/node timings therefore omit the operation most closely associated with the reporter's symptom. Log before/after hog acquisition and release, default-follow settlement, device pin, format confirmation, engine start and node play. Include requested/bound device, success/OSStatus where available, elapsed time and whether the settle deadline expired. Existing format/rebind logs cover some successful exits; entry records are needed when a call never returns. Use values already being read, not additional HAL probes on the hot path.

2. **Complete the transport-to-UI timeline and explain discarded callbacks.** Extend submission/queue-admission timing to seek and resume; retain play plus segment identity through node scheduling, recovery and main-thread delivery. Log accepted/dropped segment and transport settlements with captured/current identities and reason. Add a bounded first main-thread position-update event after a start/seek. That distinguishes time waiting for the player queue, rendering, delivery to main, and the displayed counter—the reporter specifically says the counter freezes. Do not log every UI tick or render callback.

3. **Make Save Debug Info available during a player/HAL stall.** `VibeDebugInfoText` performs fresh HAL diagnostics and an unbounded synchronous player-queue snapshot before collecting the persisted log. A wedged queue or HAL call can therefore prevent the very report needed to diagnose it. Collect the log and already-published state independently; time-limit optional fresh device/player snapshots and label missing/stale sections with their timestamps. Do not block the exporter waiting for a timed-out worker to return. This is a code-inspection risk; this review did not hang a real driver.

4. **Measure signal only if symptom-marked logs still show prompt rendering.** Node-clock progress cannot establish non-silent samples or audible DAC output. A bounded, optional measurement at the existing post-mix level tap could record the first above-threshold sample and peak/RMS plus sample/host time, without opening the file again. Publish preallocated counters and log off the audio thread; report when no tap is active and avoid making the demand-driven analyzer permanently active. Even this measures the software signal only. A loopback or synchronized screen/sound recording remains the way to investigate downstream audible delay. This is a second-stage tool, not a prerequisite for the next diagnostic beta.

Keep the current persisted beta logging, symptom markers (including Greek physical M), stall onset/recovery and matching dSYMs. The existing 47–58 ms beta7 render timings without symptom markers still do not settle the audible-delay question.
